# Whole Life Carbon for Energy Assessors: What It Means and Where It Stands in 2026

## Learning Objectives
- Define whole life carbon, embodied carbon, upfront carbon and operational carbon as the RICS professional standard defines them
- Describe the life cycle modules A to D and place an RdSAP calculation within them
- State the status at October 2026 of the RICS standard, BS EN 15978 and whole life carbon in building regulation and London planning policy in England
- Explain what PAS 2035:2023 and the RdSAP 10 specification ask of you on carbon, and what they leave out
- Advise a client on the carbon of retrofit measures without overstating what an EPC or a retrofit assessment shows

---

## Introduction

Every carbon figure you produce in RdSAP is about the energy a dwelling uses once it is occupied. Whole life carbon adds the emissions from making, transporting, installing, maintaining and replacing the materials in a building, and from dealing with them at the end of its life.

The definitions come from the RICS professional standard, which "applies globally" (RICS standard, front matter). The parts on building regulation and planning cover England only; the part on EPC reform covers England and Wales. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland were not otherwise checked: if you work there, ask your building standards or building control body, or your local planning authority, what applies.

This edition replaces the July 2026 edition. That edition made end of life a third component of whole life carbon, when the RICS standard counts it as part of embodied carbon; said EPC methodology reflects modules B6 and B7, when B7 is water; and cited the 2017 RICS first edition, BS EN 15978:2011 and the 2019 UKGBC framework, all since superseded or withdrawn. It presented national regulation of embodied carbon as expected and told you to watch "Part Z": no government document read for this course commits to such regulation, and Part Z appears in none of the consultation, response or circular texts read. Its payback periods and product comparisons had no source and have gone.

The course is correct to 14 October 2026.

## Section 1: The vocabulary, as the RICS standard defines it

**In short:** When you discuss carbon with clients or designers, the RICS definitions are the reference: embodied carbon covers modules A0 to A5, B1 to B5 and C1 to C4, so end of life is part of it, while operational carbon is B6 for energy and B7 for water. Module D sits outside the whole life carbon total and is reported separately, and a component replaced during the study period counts again under B4.

The reference document is the RICS professional standard "Whole life carbon assessment for the built environment", 2nd edition, September 2023 (version 3, August 2024). Its carbon definitions are:
- Whole life carbon: the sum of all asset-related greenhouse gas emissions and removals, "both operational and embodied", over the life cycle of an asset including its disposal: modules A0 to A5, B1 to B7, B8 (optional) and C1 to C4 (RICS standard, Carbon definitions).
- Embodied carbon: the total emissions and removals associated with materials and construction processes throughout the life cycle: modules A0 to A5, B1 to B5 and C1 to C4 (Carbon definitions).
- Upfront carbon: emissions associated with materials and construction processes up to practical completion, modules A0 to A5. It excludes the biogenic carbon sequestered in the installed products at practical completion (Carbon definitions).
- Operational carbon: for energy (module B6), emissions arising from all energy consumed by an asset in use over its life cycle; for water (module B7), emissions from water supply and wastewater treatment (Carbon definitions).
- User carbon (module B8): emissions associated with the user's use of the building. It is optional (Carbon definitions).

Two consequences follow. End of life (modules C1 to C4) is part of embodied carbon, not a separate component. And module D sits outside the whole life carbon total: it covers potential benefits and loads "beyond the system boundary", and a full assessment includes it but reports it separately (RICS standard, section 2.1).

### The life cycle modules

RICS sets out the modular structure in its Figure 2, which it describes as "adapted from EN 15978, EN 17472 and EN 15643" (section 2.1). As named in Figure 2 and described in section 2.1:
- A0: non-physical processes before construction, such as preliminary studies, tests and design.
- A1 to A3: raw material supply, transport, manufacturing.
- A4 and A5: transport, then construction and installation.
- B1 to B5: use, maintenance, repair, replacement, refurbishment.
- B6 and B7: operational energy, operational water.
- B8: user activities not covered in B1 to B7.
- C1 to C4: deconstruction and demolition, transport, waste processing, disposal.
- D1: potential loads and benefits from reuse, recycling, energy recovery or landfilling of material arising during construction, use or end of life.
- D2: potential benefits of utilities exported from the asset, such as electricity.

A component replaced during the study period, such as a window, therefore appears twice: in A1 to A5 when first installed, and again under B4 replacement.

For domestic projects the reference study period is 60 years, and the standard adds that a 120-year assessment of the envelope "should" also be undertaken (RICS standard, section 4.2). The standard deals with climate change impacts only (section 1.4.5).

## Section 2: The documents and where they stand

**In short:** The RICS standard binds RICS members when they carry out a whole life carbon assessment; no source read for this course obliges you, as an energy or retrofit assessor, to carry one out. Under it a single house is a small project, where assessing only upfront and embodied carbon is allowable, subject to the conditions below. BS EN 15978:2011 was withdrawn on 31 July 2026, and the text of BS EN 15978:2026 was not available for this course.

### The RICS professional standard

The 2nd edition is a "RICS PROFESSIONAL STANDARD", effective from 1 July 2024 (RICS standard, cover). RICS calls it "a significant expansion of the first, published in 2017", and says the version 3 changes are "minor and mostly editorial" (RICS whole life carbon assessment web page).

The standard says "Members must comply with an RICS professional standard", and separates mandatory requirements, which use "must", from recommended best practice, which uses "should" (RICS standard, front matter). The RICS web page puts it more narrowly: members need to follow the 2nd edition's requirements "when completing a whole life carbon assessment". So it binds RICS members when they carry out an assessment. Nothing in it, or in any other source read for this course, obliges an energy assessor or retrofit assessor to carry one out.

Its scope includes the "retrofit/refurbishment of existing assets" (section 1.4). Three provisions matter for domestic work:
- Retrofit and refurbishment projects should be treated as new projects and must report against all life cycle stages, with module D reported separately (section 3.3).
- New material added in a retrofit counts in modules A1 to A5. Retained elements are assumed to have no impact from their original manufacture, but their maintenance or replacement in use is reported in module B (section 3.3).
- For small projects, under 1,000 square metres gross internal area or "ten dwellings or fewer", it is allowable to assess only upfront and embodied carbon (modules A1 to A5, B1 to B5 and C1 to C4), with other mechanisms used to reduce operational carbon. Embodied and operational carbon then "cannot be brought together" to understand whole life carbon (section 3.8).

A single house is a small project on that definition. The standard also says that reusing and refurbishing existing structures is "often the most efficient approach" in whole life carbon terms (section 3.2).

### BS EN 15978 and BS EN 15804

BSI lists BS EN 15978:2011 ("Calculation method") as withdrawn on 31 July 2026, and BS EN 15978:2026, "Sustainability of construction works. Assessment of environmental performance of buildings. Requirements and guidance", as current, published the same day (BSI catalogue). BSI's scope note says it specifies a calculation method based on life cycle assessment and is applicable to "new, existing buildings and buildings undergoing refurbishment". Its text was not available for this course, so the module list above is the RICS one; for the 2026 standard's own definitions, read the standard.

BS EN 15804:2012+A2:2019 sets the "core product category rules" for Type III environmental declarations for construction products (BSI catalogue), that is, environmental product declarations (EPDs). The RICS standard prefers EPDs that use EN 15804 and ISO 21930:2017, says any EPD should be independently verified to ISO 14025, with third-party verification preferred, and says to use generic carbon factors where no EPD exists (section 4.7).

UKGBC marks its 2019 "Net Zero Carbon Buildings: A Framework Definition", which the old edition cited, as "superseded by the Whole Life Carbon Framework" and "no longer in use" (UKGBC framework page).

## Section 3: Whole life carbon in regulation and planning in England

**In short:** Embodied carbon is outside the Building Regulations in England, none of the main 2026 amendments concerns it, and the checks for this course found no government consultation on regulating it and no commitment to do so. In London, the whole life-cycle carbon policy reaches a domestic retrofit only if the work is part of an application referred to the Mayor; other planning authorities were not checked, so ask them.

### Building Regulations: operational only

In its December 2023 Future Homes and Buildings Standards consultation, the government said embodied carbon was "beyond the scope of this consultation and the existing Building Regulations", accepted that it is "a significant contributor to the whole life carbon of a building", and said it "intends to consult" on measuring and reducing embodied carbon in new buildings "in due course" (2023 consultation, paragraph 1.1.4).

The response came on 24 March 2026. The Building Regulations etc. (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2026, SI 2026/335, apply to building work in England and come into force on 24 March 2027, or 24 September 2027 for some provisions in relation to higher-risk building work (SI 2026/335, regulation 1). Building Circular Letter 01/2026 lists the main changes: a new requirement L3 for on-site renewable electricity generation for new dwellings, Part L amended to reflect "the dual aims of reducing greenhouse gas emissions and conserving energy", a wider definition of fixed building services, and a new regulation 40C on information for homeowners. None concerns embodied carbon.

The 2026 Approved Document L Volume 1, now titled "Energy and greenhouse gas emissions", is "for use in England" and takes effect on 24 March 2027 (ADL Volume 1, 2026 edition, front pages). Its metrics for new dwellings are a target primary energy rate, a target emission rate in kgCO2/m2 per year and a target fabric energy efficiency rate (paragraph 1.4). In the parts read for this course (to paragraph 4.12) it does not mention embodied or whole life carbon. In the parts of the government response read (to paragraph 7.90), the only mention of embodied carbon is a respondents' comment that guidance "should cover embodied carbon" (response, paragraph 5.69), which the government's answer does not take up (paragraph 5.71).

In July 2025 MHCLG published research by AECOM, "Consideration of embodied carbon in new buildings". The cover note says publication "does not indicate any specific future action" and that the government is considering "whether government interventions would be appropriate" (cover note, 7 July 2025).

So, at 14 October 2026, embodied carbon is not regulated by the Building Regulations in England, and the checks made for this course found no government consultation on regulating it and no commitment to do so.

### EPC reform (England and Wales)

The government consulted on reforming the Energy Performance of Buildings regime in England and Wales from 4 December 2024 to 26 February 2025. Its partial response (21 January 2026, updated 9 March 2026) confirms four new headline metrics for domestic EPCs: energy cost, fabric performance, heating system and smart readiness (partial response, paragraph 25). It keeps a secondary carbon-based metric "to provide a snapshot of the modelled emissions produced by the building" (paragraph 30). It does not mention embodied carbon, whole life carbon or life cycle. It aimed to deliver new EPCs "from October 2026" (paragraph 74), but a 9 March 2026 update on the consultation page moves the launch "to the second half of 2027". Ask your accreditation scheme which EPC format applies to your lodgements now.

### London planning policy

The Greater London Authority's London Plan Guidance "Whole Life-Cycle Carbon Assessments" (March 2022) says Policy SI 2 F of the London Plan 2021 "applies to planning applications which are referred to the Mayor" (paragraph 1.1.1), and that assessments for non-referable major developments are "encouraged". An assessment covers modules A, B, C and D and should be submitted at pre-application (where relevant), with the application, and after construction. The guidance points to BS EN 15978:2011 and the 2017 RICS first edition, and the GLA's web page says its guidance and template are still "underpinned by first edition methodology", with revisions for the 2nd edition being considered.

It reaches a domestic retrofit only if the work forms part of an application referred to the Mayor. The GLA's Draft London Plan of 16 July 2026 lists a policy GHR3, "Whole life-cycle carbon"; it is a draft, its text was not read for this course, and the GLA or your borough can say what it proposes. Other local planning authorities may have policies of their own; this course did not check them, so ask the authority.

## Section 4: What it means for your work

**In short:** It is sensible to say plainly in your write-up that its carbon figures are for energy in use only: neither an RdSAP figure nor PAS 2035 carbon cost-effectiveness covers the carbon in making and installing a measure. If a client wants the embodied side looked at, point them to the manufacturer's EPD and check whether it is independently verified; do not quote payback periods or rank products by embodied carbon unless based on verified EPDs for the actual products.

### What an RdSAP calculation covers

RdSAP is the UK approved methodology for assessing energy performance and generating EPCs for existing dwellings, and it expresses energy performance as an asset rating, an indicator of "the cost of energy" for standardised use of the building (RdSAP10 specification v4.0, Introduction). The notes to Table 32 class energy ratings as calculations "including regulated energy uses only". The CO2 and primary energy factors are those of SAP 10.2 Table 12 (section 19.2), given as kg CO2e per kWh and including methane and nitrous oxide (Table 32 and notes). The Environmental Impact Rating is "carbon-based" (section 19.2), and total running costs, CO2 emissions and primary energy after improvements are totalled for space heating, water heating and lighting (section 20).

Against the modules in Section 1, then, an RdSAP carbon figure estimates part of module B6: emissions from the regulated energy uses, under standardised use. It has nothing for modules A, B1 to B5, B7 or C. Where a dwelling has PV, the assessment "may include a benefit for the export of excess generation to the Grid" (specification Table 30, reference 8; Table 32 has a factor for "electricity sold to grid, PV"), which RICS would report separately in module D2. The embodied carbon of the measures you recommend is outside the calculation.

### What PAS 2035:2023 asks

PAS 2035:2023 does not use the terms "embodied carbon" or "whole life carbon" anywhere in its text. Its Introduction lists among its aims reduced "greenhouse gas emissions associated with energy use" and "reduced environmental impacts of buildings" (PAS 2035:2023, clause 0, items c) and d)). Its only pointer to the subject is a guide called "Whole life assessment for low carbon design" in a reference list "for guidance only" (clause 15.2).

The carbon figures PAS 2035 does require are operational:
- The retrofit assessment data are used to estimate annual fuel use, fuel costs and carbon dioxide emissions, using a recognised model such as RdSAP, SAP or PHPP (clause 7.3.3).
- In the improvement options evaluation, the Retrofit Coordinator calculates the "carbon cost-effectiveness" of each candidate measure: the lifetime cost of the measure divided by the lifetime reduction in carbon dioxide emissions, that is, the annual reduction multiplied by the estimated life (clause 8.1.1 b) and Note 2).
- Those emissions use the carbon dioxide emission factors in the current SAP, or in PHPP (clause 8.1.3 c)).

Carbon cost-effectiveness is a cost per unit of operational CO2 saved. It says nothing about the carbon released in making and installing the measure, and should not be presented to a client as a whole life carbon figure. PAS 2030:2023, the installer's companion standard, does not use either term.

### Advising clients

When a client asks whether a measure is "low carbon", the honest answer has two halves: your assessment covers emissions from energy use under standard assumptions, and the carbon in the product and its installation is a separate question your software does not answer.

If a client or designer wants the embodied side looked at:
- Point them to the manufacturer's EPD to BS EN 15804 for the specific product, and check which modules it declares and whether it has been independently verified (RICS standard, section 4.7).
- Under the RICS standard, upfront carbon excludes the biogenic carbon sequestered in installed products, so bio-based products' stored carbon does not reduce it (Carbon definitions).
- For a full assessment, look for someone working to the RICS standard; for a single dwelling the small project provisions in section 3.8 apply.

A sensible habit is to say plainly in your write-up that its carbon figures are for energy in use only. Do not quote payback periods or rank products by embodied carbon unless the arithmetic comes from verified EPDs for the actual products. Within PAS 2035 the improvement options evaluation is the Retrofit Coordinator's (clause 8.1.1) and the retrofit design is the Retrofit Designer's (clause 8), so if a client wants embodied carbon to weigh in those choices, raise it with the coordinator and designer.

## Key Takeaways
- Under the RICS standard, embodied carbon is modules A0 to A5, B1 to B5 and C1 to C4; operational carbon is B6 (energy) and B7 (water); module D is reported separately.
- The RICS standard's 2nd edition took effect on 1 July 2024; RICS members must follow it when carrying out a whole life carbon assessment.
- BS EN 15978:2011 was withdrawn on 31 July 2026 and BSI lists BS EN 15978:2026 as current; RICS prefers EPDs to EN 15804, now BS EN 15804:2012+A2:2019.
- An RdSAP carbon figure is part of module B6 (regulated energy, standardised use), plus any PV export benefit RICS would put in D2; it has no embodied carbon.
- PAS 2035:2023 does not mention embodied or whole life carbon; its carbon cost-effectiveness uses operational CO2 savings only.
- At 14 October 2026 embodied carbon is outside the Building Regulations in England, the 2026 amendments do not add it, and no commitment to regulate it was found.
- In London, Policy SI 2 F requires a whole life-cycle carbon assessment for planning applications referred to the Mayor.
- For product carbon, use a verified EPD for the actual product, and offer no payback figure you have not calculated from one.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Under the RICS professional standard, which modules make up embodied carbon?

A) A1 to A3 only
B) A0 to A5, B6 and B7
C) A0 to A5, B1 to B5 and C1 to C4
D) A0 to A5, B1 to B7, C1 to C4 and D

**Correct answer: C.** The RICS carbon definitions give embodied carbon as A0 to A5, B1 to B5 and C1 to C4; B6 and B7 are operational and module D is reported separately (RICS standard, Carbon definitions and section 2.1).

---

**Q2.** Which statement about the carbon figures from an RdSAP 10 calculation is correct?

A) They are emissions from energy use, calculated with the SAP 10.2 emission factors, with nothing for making or installing materials
B) They include emissions from water supply and wastewater treatment (module B7)
C) They include the embodied carbon of each recommended improvement measure
D) They are calculated from the manufacturers' EPDs for the products recorded

**Correct answer: A.** The specification applies the SAP 10.2 Table 12 factors per kWh to regulated energy uses, totalled for space heating, water heating and lighting (RdSAP10 specification v4.0, sections 19.2 and 20, Table 32 notes).

---

**Q3.** What was the position on regulating embodied carbon in England on 14 October 2026?

A) Embodied carbon limits have applied through Part L since 2025
B) SI 2026/335 requires embodied carbon reporting for new dwellings from 24 March 2027
C) The government has consulted and decided to regulate embodied carbon from 2028
D) Embodied carbon is outside the Building Regulations, and no government commitment to regulate it was found

**Correct answer: D.** The 2023 consultation put embodied carbon beyond the existing Building Regulations and said only that the government intends to consult "in due course"; the 2026 changes do not cover it (2023 consultation, paragraph 1.1.4; Building Circular Letter 01/2026).

---

**Q4.** In PAS 2035:2023, what is the "carbon cost-effectiveness" the Retrofit Coordinator calculates for each candidate measure?

A) The time taken for operational savings to repay the embodied carbon of the measure
B) The lifetime cost of the measure divided by its lifetime reduction in carbon dioxide emissions, using SAP or PHPP emission factors
C) A whole life carbon assessment of the measure to the RICS standard
D) The change in the dwelling's Environmental Impact Rating after the measure

**Correct answer: B.** Note 2 to clause 8.1.1 defines it as lifetime cost divided by lifetime CO2 reduction, and clause 8.1.3 c) requires the SAP or PHPP emission factors (PAS 2035:2023, clauses 8.1.1 and 8.1.3).

---

**Q5.** To which applications does London Plan Policy SI 2 F apply its whole life-cycle carbon assessment requirement?

A) All domestic retrofit work in Greater London
B) Every EPC lodged for a London property
C) Planning applications referred to the Mayor, with assessments for other major developments encouraged
D) All planning applications in England from 2027

**Correct answer: C.** The GLA guidance says Policy SI 2 F applies to planning applications referred to the Mayor and encourages assessments for non-referable major developments (GLA guidance, March 2022, paragraph 1.1.1).
