## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- State the regulations a Scottish domestic EPC is issued under today, what they require of the certificate and the register, and the status of the reform instruments as at 28 October 2026.
- Explain what an Approved Organisation is, what the published documents say about assessors accredited in England and Wales, and which questions only your own scheme can answer.
- Choose the Scottish age band for each building part and trace the defaults that follow: wall thickness and U-values, roofs, floors, doors and thermal bridging.
- Date glazing against the Scottish trigger years of 2003 and 2023 and apply the Scottish window, door and curtain wall defaults.
- Apply each convention that carries Scottish wording, and recognise how country, region, climate, meter and other data items behave for a Scottish address.

## Introduction

RdSAP 10 is one method across the UK. The Specification's data set lists five countries (England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland and the Isle of Man), and the same rules expand the reduced data set into a SAP 10.2 calculation for each (RdSAP10 Specification v4.0, Introduction and Table 31 item 1-1). What changes in Scotland is the law the certificate is issued under, the register it goes to, the body you must belong to, default tables keyed to Scottish dates, a separate Scottish text for two conventions and Scottish wording in six more. An assessor trained in England can get most of a Scottish survey right by habit and still enter the wrong age band, the wrong glazing age or evidence that Scotland does not accept.

**Where this course applies.** Scotland. It is written for domestic energy assessors, many accredited in England and Wales, who want to lodge EPCs for existing dwellings in Scotland. It does not cover new dwellings, non-domestic certificates or Northern Ireland. Rules for England, Wales and Northern Ireland appear only for contrast.

The sources are primary and are cited as the course goes: the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2008 (SSI 2008/309) as amended; the 2025 Regulations (SSI 2025/417) as made; the draft 2026 Amendment Regulations and the Scottish Parliament's committee papers on them; the Scottish Government's pages on Approved Organisations, EPC reform and the Scottish EPC Accreditation Scheme; and the full texts of the RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0 (BRE, 7 June 2026) and the RdSAP 10 Conventions version 12.2 (applicable from 6 August 2026), searched for every occurrence of "Scotland" and "Scottish". Three documents could not be opened: the Scottish EPC Register's FAQ (January 2025), the Scottish Government's current operational framework for Approved Organisations, and the 2022 and April 2024 editions of standard 6.9 of the domestic technical handbook. Where one of them would settle a point, the text says so and tells you to ask your Approved Organisation.

The reform that will replace the 2008 Regulations is the subject of MER-SCO-002 and appears here only where it affects today's work. The course is correct to 28 October 2026. Two things in it move this week: the lodgement fee is due to change on 31 October 2026, and the instrument postponing the reform had not been made on the date of checking (Section 1).

## Section 1: The Legal Basis Today

**In short:** Under the 2008 Regulations an EPC must be issued by a qualified member of an approved organisation for that category of building, and is valid for 10 years. Any deviation from the Specification and Conventions applicable at the time of assessment invalidates the calculation. The instrument postponing the reform was still a draft on 28 October 2026, so in the first week of November check for the made instrument and read your Approved Organisation's notice before you lodge.

### The 2008 Regulations

The Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2008 came into force on 4 January 2009 and extend to Scotland only (regulation 1). Where a building is to be sold or let, the owner must make a copy of a valid EPC and the related recommendations report available free of charge to a prospective buyer or tenant, and provide a copy free of charge to the buyer or tenant; renewing a lease with the same tenant is not a letting for this purpose (regulation 5, substituted from 1 October 2012 by SSI 2012/208).

Regulation 6(1) sets what the certificate must do. It must:
- express the asset rating in a way approved by the Scottish Ministers under regulation 7(b) (6(1)(a));
- include a reference value, an energy performance indicator and a numerical indicator of primary energy use (6(1)(b), (ba) and (bb));
- contain cost-effective and technically feasible recommendations, and say that more detail is in the recommendations report (6(1)(c) and (ca));
- give the address, the name of the approved organisation and the date of issue (6(1)(d));
- "be issued by a qualified member of an approved organisation for that category of building or building unit" (6(1)(e)).

The asset rating is a numerical indicator of the estimated energy consumed and carbon dioxide emitted under a standardised use of the building (regulation 2). The certificate and its recommendations report cease to be valid "for all other purposes, on the expiry of a period of 10 years from the date on which the document was issued"; for the green deal disclosure obligations the period is one year (regulation 6(2), substituted from 27 January 2013 by SSI 2013/12). The recommendations report gives more detail on the cost effectiveness of the recommendations and the steps to implement them (regulation 6A).

### The method

The Regulations do not name RdSAP. The Scottish Ministers must approve "a methodology of calculation of the energy performance of buildings or building units", based on Annex 1 to Directive 2010/31/EU (regulation 7, substituted from 21 December 2012 by SSI 2012/315). The Scottish Government's 2019 review records that it "took the decision in 2008 to adopt the same respective methodologies" as the rest of the UK: SAP for new homes, RdSAP for existing homes and SBEM for non-domestic buildings (Domestic and non-domestic energy performance certificates: review, 7 February 2019, section 2). The Specification calls RdSAP "the UK approved methodology", but its footnotes cite only the England and Wales regulations (Introduction). This course did not find the Scottish Ministers' approval notice under regulation 7.

Two sentences govern every assessment. Any deviation from the Specification and the Conventions "applicable at the time of assessment invalidates the calculation and therefore cannot be used for generating EPC" (Specification, Introduction). Where a convention conflicts with the published SAP specification, "the Convention takes precedence" (Conventions v12.2, front page). Version 12.2 says it is applicable from 6 August 2026; this course found no Scottish Government notice of its adoption, so confirm with your Approved Organisation which edition applies to Scottish lodgements.

### The 2025 Regulations and the 2026 Amendment Regulations

The Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2025 (SSI 2025/417) were made on 16 December 2025 and will replace the 2008 Regulations (Explanatory Note). Regulations 1, 2, 11 (approved organisations), 12 (accreditation schemes) and 36 came into force on 1 January 2026; as made, the rest come into force on 31 October 2026 (regulation 1(2) and (3)). As made, regulation 36 keeps approvals under regulation 8 of the 2008 Regulations in effect until 31 October 2026.

The draft Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Amendment Regulations 2026 were laid on 24 August 2026 (Economy, Tourism and Energy Committee, Paper 6). Draft regulation 3 substitutes "30 April 2028" for "31 October 2026" as the date of full commencement, moves the regulation 36 dates to 29 April 2028 and 30 April 2028, and moves the transitional dates to match. The draft would come into force on 30 October 2026, and its regulation 2 (fees) on 31 October 2026 (draft regulation 1(2) and (3)). The Committee agreed motion S7M-00959 on 29 September 2026 and recommended to the Parliament that the instrument be approved (4th Report, 2026, published 7 October 2026, paragraph 21).

On 28 October 2026 legislation.gov.uk still described it as "a draft item of legislation" not yet made as a Scottish Statutory Instrument, and this course found no record of the Parliament's approval vote. So, on the date of checking: the 2025 Regulations as made still name 31 October 2026; the draft that would substitute 30 April 2028 is due to take effect on 30 October 2026 if made; and the Scottish Government has said that "the majority of the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2008 and current EPC system will now remain in place until Spring 2028" (Energy Performance Certificate Reform: updates, 24 August 2026). In the first week of November, check legislation.gov.uk for the made instrument and read your Approved Organisation's notice before you lodge. If the made text differs from the draft, the made text governs.

### The lodgement fee

The keeper may charge £2.60 for entering the data for a dwelling and £12.10 for any other building (regulation 10A of the 2008 Regulations, sums substituted from 1 October 2017 by SSI 2017/225). Draft regulation 2 of the 2026 Amendment Regulations substitutes new regulations 10 and 10A from 31 October 2026, with fees of £6.00 for a dwelling and £15.50 for other buildings, a power that "only applies where the keeper of a register is a Scottish public authority" (new regulation 10A(2)). These are the sums in regulation 15 of the 2025 Regulations. The Scottish Government says the fees "will rise as planned, on 31 October 2026", which "will ensure technical and operational infrastructure is ready" and support "the development of an effective audit regime" (updates, 24 August 2026). How the fee is collected from you depends on your Approved Organisation's terms, which this course has not seen.

## Section 2: The Register and the Approved Organisations

**In short:** Before you issue a certificate you must ensure its data is sent to the register, and registered data must not be altered: a mistake is dealt with by a corrected lodgement, and you tell your Approved Organisation. Every assessor in Scotland must belong to at least one Approved Organisation; accreditation in England and Wales is not enough by itself, so ask your scheme in writing what it requires before your first Scottish job.

### The register duty

There must be registers of energy performance data from which an EPC and recommendations report may be produced, each maintained by a keeper, defined as "the Scottish Ministers or a person appointed by the Scottish Ministers to keep a register on their behalf" (regulations 2 and 10(1) and (2) of the 2008 Regulations, regulation 10 substituted from 1 October 2012 by SSI 2012/190). Your duty is regulation 10(4): where a member of an approved organisation issues either document, "that person must ensure that the energy performance data relating to that document is sent to the relevant register before the document is issued". Registered data carries a unique reference, must not be altered once registered (green deal information apart) and stays on the register for at least 10 years (regulation 10(5), (5A) and (6)).

### The Scottish EPC Register in practice

The register is at scottishepcregister.org.uk. The Scottish Government's impact assessment describes it as "currently maintained by the Energy Saving Trust which is appointed by the Scottish Ministers as Keeper of the Register" (Business and Regulatory Impact Assessment, 24 August 2026, section 1). The register's documents page lists an EPC Register FAQ (January 2025) for "customers and assessors"; it refused automated access on 23 and 28 October 2026, so what it says about access, timescales or corrections is not quoted here.

The same page carries a Missing Address Submission Form (August 2023): "An assessor submitting this form will receive a temporary UPRN so that they may complete an EPC lodgement." Part A, which the form asks you to complete in full, takes your Approved Organisation and your membership number with it; Part B is for an address missing from the register and Part C for one that is present but wrong.

Registered data must not be altered, and convention 9.03 deals with a mistake by a corrected lodgement: "If you lodge an EPC in error and lodge a corrected EPC, inform your accreditation scheme so that the erroneous one can be marked 'not for issue'." In Scotland the body you belong to is an approved organisation, so that is who you tell; ask for its procedure before you need it.

### Approved Organisations

An approved organisation is "an organisation approved by the Scottish Ministers in accordance with this regulation as an organisation whose members may issue energy performance certificates", and its approval may be limited to categories of building (regulation 8(1) and (2)). The Ministers must first be satisfied that it will ensure certificates are prepared "in a consistent, accurate and independent manner" by "fit and proper persons who are qualified by their education, training and experience" (regulation 8(3)).

The Scottish Government's page on approved organisation status (updated 6 March 2026) says: "All EPC assessors in Scotland must be a member of at least one Approved Organisation." Approved Organisations "are appointed by the Scottish Ministers", existing ones "must reapply to keep their status", and they handle "member accreditation and registration". The page does not list them, and this course found no published list of those that accredit existing-dwelling assessors.

### Coming in from England and Wales

Accreditation in England and Wales does not by itself let you issue a Scottish EPC. The 2019 review: "an English EPC Assessor cannot issue EPCs in Scotland without becoming a member of a Scottish Approved Organisation that has signed a protocol agreement with the Scottish Government" (section 2). The 2026 impact assessment describes a UK-wide market, "with assessors able to operate throughout the four nations, provided they meet the requirements of the respective regulations" (BRIA, section 1); in Scotland that includes Approved Organisation membership.

What an Approved Organisation asks of an existing England and Wales member is not in any document this course could open: the current operational framework, which would hold the protocol, returned no content. The Scottish EPC Accreditation Scheme operating requirements (first published 6 March 2026, updated 14 October 2026) are written for the scheme under the 2025 Regulations, and their chapter on assessors states no date from which it applies. As a guide to direction, they say:
- assessors "may be members of more than one AO, but it is envisaged that assessors will only have one single membership of the Scottish EPC Accreditation Scheme"; applications are checked against all other Approved Organisations for current or past membership; and every assessor application is subject to a Level 1 Disclosure, an existing one being accepted only if produced within 3 years (2.1.2);
- entry criteria are determined by the Scottish Ministers and based on accredited qualifications "and/or" an "Accreditation of Prior Experiential Learning (APEL) route" (2.2.4);
- members must be able to "use data recording and calculation software approved for use in Scotland" (2.2.7).

Today's route is set by each Approved Organisation. Before your first Scottish job, ask your scheme in writing whether it is an Approved Organisation for existing dwellings in Scotland, what it requires of an existing member, whether your software covers Scottish lodgement, and how it issues Scotland-specific guidance.

## Section 3: Age Bands

**In short:** Most defaults are read by age band, so your first job is to take each building part's band from the Scottish column of Table 1. A part built in 1900 to 1918, 1965 or 1966, 1983, 1991, 1996 to 1998, 2007 or 2023 takes a different letter from England and Wales. In other years the letter matches, but Scottish tables and footnotes still apply, so the same letter does not mean the same defaults.

### Why the band comes first

Most defaults in the Specification are read by age band, so in Scotland the band is the first thing to get right. Table 31 item 2-1 asks for the band of each building part "According to Table 1", noting the "differences for England, Wales, Scotland". Bands relate to "the age of each building part", and band M "can apply to extensions added to an older property" (section 2). A roof room takes the band of its building part unless evidence such as planning applications or dated photographs proves otherwise (convention 3.13), and an upper storey of a different build date is entered as a vertical extension (convention 2.11). The dates in Table 1 "are generally one year after a change in regulations, to allow for completion of dwellings approved under the previous regulations" (section 2).

### Table 1: the Scottish column

Years of construction, Scotland first and England and Wales second (Table 1):
- Band A: before 1919; before 1900.
- Band B: 1919 to 1929; 1900 to 1929.
- Band C: 1930 to 1949 in both.
- Band D: 1950 to 1964; 1950 to 1966.
- Band E: 1965 to 1975; 1967 to 1975.
- Band F: 1976 to 1983; 1976 to 1982.
- Band G: 1984 to 1991; 1983 to 1990.
- Band H: 1992 to 1998; 1991 to 1995.
- Band I: 1999 to 2002; 1996 to 2002.
- Band J: 2003 to 2007; 2003 to 2006.
- Band K: 2008 to 2011; 2007 to 2011.
- Band L: 2012 to 2023; 2012 to 2022.
- Band M: 2024 onwards; 2023 onwards.

Read side by side, the two columns give a building part a different letter only if it was built in 1900 to 1918, 1965 or 1966, 1983, 1991, 1996 to 1998, 2007 or 2023. In every other year the letter is the same, but it then draws on Scottish tables and footnotes, so the same letter does not mean the same defaults (Table 7 rather than Table 6 for walls, and the Scottish entries in Tables 3, 18, 19, 20 and 26). For conversions the Specification sends you to the Conventions (section 2), and Scotland has its own text there (Section 7).

### Worked examples

Five cases show the effect:
- A tenement completed in 1905 is band A in Scotland and would be band B in England.
- A four-in-a-block built in 1966 is band E in Scotland and band D in England.
- A house built in 1997 is band H in Scotland and band I in England.
- A house built in 2007 is band J in Scotland and band K in England. The thermal bridging factor follows the band of the main dwelling, 0.11 W/m²K for band J and 0.08 for bands K to M (Table 21), and an external door without evidence of insulation is 3.0 W/m²K in bands A to J but 2.0 in band K (Table 26).
- A 1930s house with an extension built in 2023: the main dwelling is band C in both countries; the extension is band L in Scotland and band M in England.

## Section 4: Walls, Roofs and Floors

**In short:** Measure each external wall's thickness; the Table 3 default is only for when you cannot, and in Scotland it adds thickness to stone and cavity walls, which for uninsulated stone in bands A to D means a lower U-value. For a Scottish address, wall U-values come from Table 7, not Table 6, and Tables 18 to 20 for roofs and floors have Scottish entries. Some tables with no Scottish column still change with the Scottish band.

### Wall thickness

Measure the thickness of each external wall of each building part; evidence is required for each different thickness (convention 2.22; Specification, section 3.5). For stone, assess thickness at each elevation and storey and use an alternative wall if it varies by more than 100 mm (convention 2.13). Table 3 is used "only when the wall thickness could not be measured", and it is the only place Scotland changes a thickness:
- Stone: 500 mm in bands A to D, 450 mm in E, 420 mm in F to H and 450 mm from I; "If in Scotland add 200 mm for bands A and B, and 100 mm for other bands".
- Cavity: 250 mm in bands A to E, 260 mm in F, 270 mm in G and H and 300 mm from I; "If in Scotland add 50 mm".
- Solid brick, timber frame, cob and system build: no Scottish addition.

The default matters beyond the wall itself. For uninsulated stone and solid brick in bands A to D the wall U-value is calculated from thickness (sections 5.6 and 5.7), external dimensions are converted to internal using it (section 3.4), and the ground floor calculation uses the wall thickness "as provided in the RdSAP data set or from Table 3" (section 5.12).

### Worked example: an unmeasurable sandstone wall

A band A sandstone wall whose thickness cannot be measured takes 500 + 200 = 700 mm in Scotland and 500 mm in England (Table 3). Section 5.6 gives U = 54.876 × W^-0.561, with W in mm (Table 12): 1.39 W/m²K at 700 mm and 1.68 at 500 mm. With lath and plaster, section 5.8 applies U = 1/(1/U0 + R) with R = 0.17 m²K/W for "drylining including laths and plaster" (Table 14 note), giving 1.12 against 1.31. For granite or whinstone, U = 45.315 × W^-0.513 gives 1.57 at 700 mm and 1.87 at 500 mm. U-values are rounded to two decimal places (section 15). The Scottish default assumes a thicker wall and so a lower U-value; a measured thickness replaces it. Dry-lining is identified by tap test, and "If tap test is inconclusive regard as not dry-lined" (convention 3.06).

### Wall U-values: Table 7

Section 5.4 gives a wall table per nation; for a Scottish address it is Table 7, not Table 6 (England). Comparing the as-built rows:
- Bands A to D: stone and solid brick are calculated under 5.6 and 5.7 in both; cob, cavity, timber frame and system build have the same values in both.
- Band E: sandstone or limestone is 1.5 in Table 7 against 1.7 in Table 6, each with footnote a, "Or from equations in 5.6 if the calculated U-value is less than 1.7"; granite or whinstone is 1.7 in both.
- Bands F, G and I: the same in both.
- Band H: stone, solid brick, cavity and system build are 0.45 in Scotland against 0.60 in England; timber frame (0.40) and cob (0.60) are the same.
- Bands J to M: every as-built type is 0.30, 0.25, 0.22 and 0.17 in Scotland against 0.35, 0.30, 0.28 and 0.26 in England.

One row runs the other way: a filled cavity in band H is 0.45 in Table 7, marked as assumed as built, against 0.35 in Table 6. In both tables, a wall known to have added insulation of unknown thickness takes "the row in the table for 50 mm insulation". So a 1995 cavity wall as built is 0.45 in Scotland and 0.60 in England (band H in both), and a 2010 timber frame wall is 0.25 against 0.30 (band K in both).

### Roofs: Table 18

Where loft insulation can be measured, Table 16 applies, and for a roof room of known insulation, Table 17; neither has a Scottish entry. Table 18 is for when the thickness "cannot be determined", and "If retrofit insulation present of unknown thickness use 50 mm" (section 5.11.4). It carries two Scottish footnotes:
- Band K: the flat roof, room-in-roof, thatched roof and thatched room-in-roof columns show 0.25 with footnote (2), "0.20 W/m²K in Scotland".
- Band L: the pitched roof column for insulation between joists or unknown shows 0.16 (270 mm) with footnote (3), "0.15 W/m²K in Scotland".

So a 2010 flat roof of undetermined insulation is 0.20 in Scotland and 0.25 in England; a 2009 room in roof with no determinable insulation takes 0.20 for all elements; and a 2015 pitched roof whose boarded loft cannot be measured is 0.15 against 0.16. The rest of Table 18 is the same for all nations. Its note explains why defaults may not match the regulations of the day: they "take account of joists" and "may differ from Elemental U-values in regulations applicable at the time of construction".

### Ground and exposed floors: Tables 19 and 20

A ground floor U-value is calculated to BS EN ISO 13370 from area, exposed perimeter and wall thickness, with construction and all-over insulation taken from Table 19 where unknown (section 5.12). The Scottish column assumes insulation earlier and thicker. Scotland first, England and Wales second:
- Bands A to G: none in either (where construction is unknown, A and B are suspended timber, or solid with underfloor heating; C to G solid).
- Band H: 25 mm; none.
- Band I: 50 mm; 25 mm.
- Bands J and K: 75 mm and 100 mm in both.
- Band L: 120 mm; 100 mm.
- Band M: 180 mm; 140 mm.

For retrofitted floor insulation, "use the greater of 50 mm and the thickness according to the age band" (Table 19, note 2). A 1995 solid floor of unknown insulation is calculated with 25 mm in Scotland and none in England. For exposed and semi-exposed upper floors, Table 20 gives 0.22 in band L with footnote (2), "0.18 W/m²K in Scotland", and 0.18 in band M with footnote (3), "0.15 W/m²K in Scotland and Wales".

### The knock-on through the band

Some tables have no Scottish column but are read by band, so the Scottish band changes them anyway: thermal bridging (0.15 for bands A to I, 0.11 for J, 0.08 for K to M, by the band of the main dwelling, Table 21), basement walls and floors (Table 23) and doors (Table 26). The 2007 house in Section 3 shows the effect.

## Section 5: Windows, Doors and Curtain Walls

**In short:** In Scotland the glazing steps fall at 2003 and 2023, where England and Wales use 2002 and 2022, so the same spacer bar date can give a different glazing age and U-value. With no evidence of the date, choose unknown; the convention names no Scottish equivalent of FENSA or CERTASS, so ask your Approved Organisation what installation evidence it accepts. Band L door U-values and the curtain wall date also differ in Scotland.

### The Scottish glazing dates

The Specification dates multiple glazing by country: 2002 and 2022 in England and Wales, "2003 in Scotland" and "2023 in Scotland" for the same steps (section 3.7.1, footnotes 7 and 8; Table 31 item 6-5). Table 24 gives the defaults for wood or PVC frames:
- Double glazing, "Scotland: before 2003": 3.1, 2.8 or 2.7 for a 6 mm, 12 mm or 16 mm or more gap (triple glazing 2.4, 2.1 or 2.0).
- Double or triple, "Scotland: 2003 or later": 2.0 (metal frame 2.2, roof window 2.3).
- Double or triple, "Scotland: 2023 or later": 1.4 (roof window 1.6).

The pre-2003 double glazing row is also used "for conservatories and for other double or triple glazing whose installation date is unknown" (Table 24, note to that row). Convention 2.16 expresses the steps as ages: "Age 1 means pre 2002 in E&W (2003 in Scotland, 2006 in NI)", Age 2 is "2003-2022 in Scotland" and Age 3 is 2023 onwards.

### Dating the glazing

Convention 3.12a: "Choose unknown date if there is no evidence of the date." Units can be dated by:
- the manufacturing date on the spacer bar, or possibly the frame;
- documentary evidence of installation, "e.g. FENSA / CERTASS / Building Control certificate or manufacturers guarantee";
- the property build date if after the trigger dates, for Scotland "Post 2003 / post 2023";
- a thermal spacer bar, which "indicates glazing age post 2002 (E&W), 2003 (Scotland) or 2006 (NI)".

The convention names FENSA, CERTASS, a Building Control certificate or a manufacturer's guarantee and no Scottish equivalent, so ask your Approved Organisation what installation evidence it accepts for Scottish windows. For non-metal frames that are pre-2003 in Scotland or of unknown period, identify the gap to the nearest of 6, 12 or 16 mm, or select 6 for a narrow gap and 16 for a wide one if it cannot easily be identified (convention 3.12b). Secondary glazing over Age 1 double glazing is recorded as Age 2 double glazing, and over Age 2 as Age 3 (convention 2.16).

### Worked examples

Four cases, all PVC:
- Spacer bars stamped 2002, 12 mm gap: in Scotland, pre-2003, Age 1, 2.8 from Table 24; the same windows in England are "2002 or later", 2.0.
- Spacer bars stamped 2022: in Scotland, Age 2, 2.0; in England, Age 3, 1.4.
- A flat in a 2010 block with original windows and no stamps or documents: the build date is after the Scottish trigger of 2003, so 3.12a(c) gives Age 2 and 2.0.
- The 2002 units with secondary glazing fitted: Age 1 plus secondary is recorded as Age 2 double glazing (convention 2.16).

The Scottish date also governs improvement measure O3, which applies where at least 80% of the window area is double glazing "with PVC frames and 12 mm gap installed before 2002 (E&W) or 2003 (Scotland) or 2006 (Northern Ireland)" and models replacement at U = 1.4 and g = 0.63 (Table 33).

### Doors and curtain walls

Table 26 gives an external door 3.0 W/m²K in bands A to J, 2.0 in K, 1.4 in M, and in band L "E/W and N.I: U-value =1.8" but "Scotland: U-value =1.6"; a door to an unheated corridor or stairwell is 1.4 in any band. A door counts as insulated only on documentary evidence including a U-value or a manufacturer reference from which it can be found (convention 3.09). A curtain wall without documentary evidence is 2.0 W/m²K if pre-2023, and "for post-2023 (2024 in Scotland)" takes the window values in the notes to Table 24 (section 5.18).

## Section 6: Country, Region, Climate and Other Data Items

**In short:** You must make sure the correct country is identified so the EPC is lodged in the right register; do not let a postcode or the region your software suggests settle it. Location within Scotland changes costs, emissions and primary energy but not the two ratings, which use UK average climate data. Dual 24-hour meters are possible in Scotland, and flats off a common stair follow the UK-wide rules.

### Country and region

The first data item is the country (Table 31 item 1-1), and convention 1.03 makes it your responsibility near the border: "Assessors must ensure that the correct country is identified so that the EPC will be lodged in the appropriate register." The region is "Derived from the postcode of the property" (item 1-2), using Table U6, which the Specification reproduces from SAP 10.2. For Scotland it gives:
- Region 14, West Scotland: FK, G, KA, ML and PA, and districts PH49 and PH50.
- Region 15, East Scotland: DD, EH, KY and PH (other than the EH and PH districts listed elsewhere).
- Region 16, North East Scotland: AB, and districts IV30 to IV32, IV36 and PH26.
- Region 17, Highland: IV and KW (other than the districts listed elsewhere), and PH19 to PH25 and PH30 to PH44.
- Region 18, Western Isles: HS. Region 19, Orkney: KW15 to KW17. Region 20, Shetland: ZE.
- Region 8, "North West England / South West Scotland": DG is "8S"; CA and LA7 to LA23 are "8E".
- Region 9, "Borders Scotland / Borders England": TD and EH43 to EH46 are "9S"; TD12, TD15, NE, SR (other than SR7 and SR8, which are region 10) and DH4 to DH5 are "9E".

The letters on regions 8 and 9 match the countries in the region names, but the Specification does not explain them, and the country is a separate item. Table U6 assigns whole areas and districts to one region, so do not let a postcode, or the region your software suggests, settle the country; 1.03 makes that your check.

### What the climate does and does not change

Section 14 limits the effect of location. "For ratings (SAP rating and EI rating), the calculations are done using the UK average climate data"; "For costs and savings, energy demand, total emissions and primary energy", the climate data for the location is used, "provided for each postcode district". Two Scottish dwellings with identical data, one with a ZE postcode (Shetland, region 20) and one with a G postcode (West Scotland, region 14), therefore get the same energy efficiency and environmental impact ratings; the location shows in costs, emissions, primary energy and the savings behind the recommendations. (Compare a Scottish and an English dwelling and the ratings can differ, because the country selects different default tables.) The regional means in Table U1 show the scale: Highland (region 17) is below the UK average in every month, 3.1°C against 4.3°C in January and 13.2°C against 16.6°C in July.

### Meters, rooms and transactions

The meter is recorded as single, dual, dual 18-hour, dual 24-hour or unknown, and "Dual 24-hour is possible in Scotland and some parts of northern Northumberland"; a dual 24-hour meter gives the 24-hour tariff (section 12), which Table 32 lists as the "24-hour heating tariff". Gather additional information, such as a recent electricity bill, if choosing a particular tariff, and otherwise treat a tele-switch as a single meter and a time-switch as dual (convention 9.13).

Habitable rooms are counted by the RdSAP definition (section 9.1.1; convention 2.04a), and the Specification notes that they are "In Scotland, usually referred to as 'apartments'" (section 9.1, footnote 9); whatever the sales particulars say, the count follows the RdSAP definition. The transaction types include "stock condition survey/EESSH", which the Specification does not expand (Table 31 item 1-3); where more than one type applies, seek clarification from the client and in doubt choose the one nearest the top of the list, and record Right to Buy as a non-marketed sale (convention 9.11). Addendum 8, on feed-in tariffs with PV or a wind turbine, is written for "England, Wales or Scotland" (Table 30).

### Flats off a shared stair

Neither the Specification nor the Conventions uses the word "tenement", and no rule for flats carries Scottish wording; a flat off a common stair is assessed under the UK-wide rules. The ones you will use most:
- flat or maisonette makes no difference to the calculation, "as definitions vary across the UK" (convention 1.02a);
- a flat reached by an enclosed heated or unheated corridor or stairwell has a draught lobby (convention 2.09b);
- a heated stairwell "is one with one or more controlled fixed heaters" (convention 2.26), and there can be only one sheltered wall input per dwelling (convention 2.03);
- a private access stairwell, as in "four in a block dwelling", is unheated if separated by an external quality door and part of the dwelling if not (convention 2.25);
- record the construction of the dwelling's own heat loss walls, not the superstructure, and for a cavity wall on the third storey or above select "Access issues" from the hard to treat cavity wall addenda (convention 3.14).

## Section 7: The Scottish Conventions, One by One

**In short:** For a converted heated building, the Scottish text of 3.05a works from the building warrant application date: with acceptable documentary evidence of an application on or after 1 May 2005 you apply the U-values it lists, and otherwise the original construction date, in each case subject to the exceptions set out below. Under the Scottish text of 9.02, evidence of intent to install does not qualify, and there is no oversight route.

Searching Conventions v12.2 for "Scotland" and "Scottish" finds Scottish wording in conventions 1.03, 2.16, 3.05a, 3.05b, 3.08, 3.12a, 3.12b and 9.02, and otherwise only in the revision history. Conventions 1.03, 2.16, 3.12a and 3.12b are covered above.

### 3.05a: conversions of heated buildings

Convention 3.05a has two texts. The text "Applicable to England, Wales and Northern Ireland" says that for a conversion of a building originally intended to be heated (a school, hotel or community hall made into dwellings, or a house sub-divided into flats) you "always use the original construction date", and specify upgraded elements only on documentary or visual evidence.

The text "Applicable to SCOTLAND ONLY", added on 1 June 2021, works from the building warrant. Where there is acceptable documentary evidence of the date of application for building warrant for the conversion, and it "was on or after 01 May 2005", you apply these U-values, unless there is acceptable documentary evidence of the construction of the thermal elements or "inspection identifies evidence to indicate a poorer value should be assigned":
- application from 1 May 2005 to 30 September 2010: wall 0.70, roof 0.35, floor 0.70;
- application from 1 October 2010: wall 0.30, roof 0.25, floor 0.25.

"In all other cases, apply the date of construction of the original building", unless there is documentary evidence of the thermal elements. Evidence is judged under the Scottish text of 9.02, and "information on building warrant applications is available online from the building standards register of the relevant local authority".

Worked example: a Victorian school converted into flats. The local authority's building standards register shows a warrant application for the conversion in March 2008; there are no drawings or calculations for the thermal elements and nothing on inspection suggests worse. You apply wall 0.70, roof 0.35 and floor 0.70. Had the application been made in 2012, the values would be 0.30, 0.25 and 0.25. Had it been made in 2003, or had you no documentary evidence of the date, you would use the original construction date, which is what the England, Wales and Northern Ireland text does in every case.

### 3.05b: conversions of unheated buildings

For a conversion of an unheated building (barn, farm building, warehouse, mill, station, workshop), use the conversion date as the date of construction, based on documentary evidence such as the date of sign-off or details of installed insulation, which in Scotland come from the "relevant local authority Building Standards Register (Scotland)" (convention 3.05b). A farm steading converted in 2009 is band K on the Scottish column.

### 3.08: U-value entry

Default U-values "must not be overwritten unless specific documentary evidence" of the thermal conductivity of the element's materials is provided, prepared in accordance with BR 443. The evidence must be relevant building control approval that defines the construction and states the calculated U-value, or a U-value calculation "produced or verified by a person with suitable expertise and experience", shown for example by OCDEA membership, to which footnote 1 adds: "In Scotland, membership of an Approved Organisation scheme for EPCs for new domestic buildings" (convention 3.08). The convention does not say what counts as "building control approval" in Scotland; ask your Approved Organisation whether building warrant documents stating a U-value meet that limb.

### 9.02: documentary evidence

Convention 9.02 has a separate Scottish text, and its differences decide what you may record from paperwork alone. Comparing the two:
- Both accept "official correspondence from the applicable Registered Social Landlord (RSL)" and certificates, warranties and guarantees, in a list that "includes, but is not limited to" those items; the England, Wales and Northern Ireland list adds "any documents verifying that work has been carried out".
- Both require you to be "confident, and able to demonstrate" that the documents relate to the property. The Scottish text requires "no physical evidence to the contrary"; the other says "no physical or other documentary evidence to the contrary".
- England, Wales and Northern Ireland: "Evidence of intent to install does not on its own qualify", but intent supported by "evidence of subsequent Building Control oversight, or visual evidence that such an upgrade has been undertaken does qualify". Route (a), intent plus oversight, treats the element as upgraded as the planning documents indicate; route (b), intent or oversight plus a visible upgrade, gives the minimum selectable upgrade, using "unknown insulation thickness" if there is no evidence of thickness.
- Scotland: "Evidence of intent to install does not qualify as acceptable documentary evidence." There is no oversight route.

The revision history records that in 2021 the "Scotland-only version remains as the original 9.02 in version 11.1". Version 12.2 amended 9.02 on 13 July 2026; compared with version 12.1, the change is to route (b) of the England, Wales and Northern Ireland text, which now refers to "unknown insulation thickness" where it referred to the lowest selectable thickness better than the as-built assumption. The Scottish text is unchanged.

Worked example: a housing association's planning drawings show cavity wall insulation to be installed and a building control file shows the work was overseen, but nothing is visible on site. In England or Wales, route (a) lets you treat the wall "as having been upgraded as indicated in the planning documents". In Scotland the drawings are evidence of intent, which does not qualify, and the Scottish text says nothing about building control oversight, so ask your Approved Organisation whether a building standards record of the completed work counts as a certificate under it. Unless you hold acceptable evidence, such as the landlord's correspondence confirming the installation or an installer's warranty for the property, you "assume as-built if no evidence of retro-fitted insulation" (Specification, section 5.2.3). A sensible habit on Scottish jobs is to keep a copy of each document you rely on and note in your site notes why it meets the Scottish text.

### Conventions with no Scottish wording

Convention 2.12 says, when combining more than four extensions, "refer to Section 6 of RdSAP 10 for U-values". That is the RdSAP 10 Specification, not section 6 (energy) of the Scottish technical handbooks; in Specification v4.0 the U-value tables are in section 5 and section 6 is conservatories. Convention 9.10 (hard to treat cavity walls) asks you to record possible high exposure in exposure zones 3 or 4 "(see map at end of these conventions)" and "If in doubt record as possible high exposure"; the Appendix 1 map is an image missing from the text copy used for this course, so read the zone for a Scottish address from the PDF. Convention 10.01 (air tightness) accepts a pressure test certificate from a member of "a recognised air tightness Competent Persons Scheme (EAS or ATTMA), that is approved by Government", with no Scottish variation.

### The certificate

Scotland adds a building standard to the content set by regulation 6(1). Standard 6.9 requires that "an energy performance certificate for the building is affixed to the building", and "Scottish Ministers have directed local authorities to apply Standard 6.9 (a) to existing buildings", limited to buildings "that are being sold or rented out" (clause 6.9.0). Clause 6.9.2 lists what the certificate shows, including current and potential energy efficiency and environmental impact ratings on seven-band A to G scales and "a statement to the effect that the EPC must be affixed to the building and not to be removed unless it is replaced with an updated version"; clause 6.9.3 suggests a meter cupboard or a place near the water stopcock (Building standards technical handbook 2020: domestic; later editions would not open). From full commencement of the 2025 Regulations, regulation 34 omits paragraph 6.9 from schedule 5 to the Building (Scotland) Regulations 2004. Scottish EPCs "look different" from those elsewhere in the UK because of Scottish Government decisions on format and content (2019 review, section 2); this course has not seen the current template.

### What does not differ

The arithmetic is the same: one Specification, one Conventions document and, for the two ratings, one UK average climate (section 14). The country selection changes the defaults (Tables 1, 3, 7, 18, 19, 20, 24 and 26, and section 5.18). You change your evidence: building warrant dates under 3.05a, the 2003 and 2023 glazing triggers, and the Scottish text of 9.02. The data then goes to a different register, under different regulations, as a member of a different body.

## Key Takeaways
- Scottish domestic EPCs are issued under SSI 2008/309 as amended, by a qualified member of an approved organisation, and are valid for 10 years (regulation 6(1)(e) and 6(2)(b)).
- On 28 October 2026 the instrument moving full commencement of the 2025 Regulations to 30 April 2028 was still a draft; the Scottish Government says the majority of the 2008 Regulations and the current EPC system remain until spring 2028. Check legislation.gov.uk and your Approved Organisation's notice in early November.
- Data must reach the register before the certificate is issued (regulation 10(4)); the domestic lodgement fee is due to rise from £2.60 to £6.00 on 31 October 2026 (draft regulation 2; gov.scot update, 24 August 2026).
- "All EPC assessors in Scotland must be a member of at least one Approved Organisation." What your scheme requires of an England and Wales member is set by the scheme: ask, in writing.
- Take the age band from the Scottish column of Table 1; for 1900 to 1918, 1965 to 1966, 1983, 1991, 1996 to 1998, 2007 and 2023 the letter itself differs, and in every year the Scottish tables and footnotes apply.
- Unmeasured stone walls get 200 mm extra in bands A and B and 100 mm otherwise, cavities 50 mm (Table 3); measure wherever you can.
- Date glazing to 2003 and 2023 in Scotland (Table 24; conventions 2.16 and 3.12a): a 2002 spacer bar is Age 1 in Scotland and Age 2 in England.
- In Scotland evidence of intent to install is never acceptable documentary evidence (convention 9.02), and conversions of heated buildings follow the building warrant rules of convention 3.05a.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** A detached house in Scotland was built in 2007. Which age band does RdSAP 10 assign, and what thermal bridging factor follows from it?

A) Band K, y = 0.08 W/m²K
B) Band K, y = 0.11 W/m²K
C) Band J, y = 0.11 W/m²K
D) Band J, y = 0.08 W/m²K

**Correct answer: C.** Table 1 of the Specification gives Scotland band J as 2003 to 2007 (in England 2007 is band K), and Table 21 sets y at 0.11 for band J and 0.08 for bands K to M.

---

**Q2.** PVC double glazed windows in a flat in Scotland have a 12 mm gap and spacer bars stamped 2002. There is no other evidence. What U-value does Table 24 give?

A) 2.8 W/m²K, from the "Scotland: before 2003" row
B) 2.0 W/m²K, from the "2003 or later" row
C) 1.4 W/m²K, from the "2023 or later" row
D) 3.1 W/m²K, because a stamped date cannot be used

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 3.12a(a) accepts the spacer bar date, 2002 is before the Scottish trigger of 2003 (Table 24; convention 2.16), and the pre-2003 row gives 2.8 for a 12 mm gap; 3.1 is the 6 mm value.

---

**Q3.** A registered social landlord gives you a letter saying external wall insulation "will be installed under the current programme". Nothing is visible on site. Under convention 9.02 in Scotland, what can you record on the strength of the letter?

A) The upgrade, because RSL correspondence is acceptable evidence
B) The minimum selectable upgrade of that type
C) The upgrade, provided Building Control oversight follows
D) Nothing: evidence of intent to install does not qualify as acceptable documentary evidence

**Correct answer: D.** The Scotland-only text of 9.02 states "Evidence of intent to install does not qualify as acceptable documentary evidence"; B and C describe routes in the England, Wales and Northern Ireland text, and RSL correspondence counts only where it is not evidence of intent.

---

**Q4.** A former church in Scotland was converted into a dwelling. The local authority's building standards register shows the building warrant application for the conversion was made in 2012. There is no documentary evidence of the thermal elements and nothing on inspection suggests a poorer value. What wall U-value does convention 3.05a apply?

A) 0.70 W/m²K
B) 0.30 W/m²K
C) The Table 7 value for the original building's age band
D) 0.25 W/m²K

**Correct answer: B.** The Scotland-only text of 3.05a gives wall 0.30, roof 0.25 and floor 0.25 for warrant applications from 1 October 2010; 0.70 is the wall value for 1 May 2005 to 30 September 2010, and the original construction date applies only in the other cases.

---

**Q5.** Under the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2008, when must the energy performance data be sent to the register?

A) Within 7 days of the site visit
B) Within 28 days of issue
C) Before the EPC or recommendations report is issued
D) Only when the dwelling is sold

**Correct answer: C.** Regulation 10(4) requires the member of an approved organisation to ensure that the data "is sent to the relevant register before the document is issued".
