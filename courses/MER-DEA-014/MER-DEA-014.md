# EPC Audit Preparation: Building an Evidence File That Stands Up
**Course ID:** MER-DEA-014 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026

## Learning Objectives
- Explain what convention 9.02 accepts as documentary evidence, including rules (a) and (b) and the separate wording for Scotland.
- Identify the entries for which the conventions require a photograph, a site note or a document.
- Record "unknown", "no access" and removed recommendations in the way the conventions allow, with the site note they require.
- State what evidence is needed before a default U-value, window value, air permeability figure or design flow temperature is replaced.
- Run a pre-lodgement check of an assessment file against the conventions and your own scheme's evidence list.

---

## Introduction

This course is about the file behind the EPC: the photographs, sketch, site notes and documents that show why each entry was made. It is built from the RdSAP 10 Conventions v12.2, which apply from 6 August 2026 and take precedence over the specification where the two conflict (conventions, front page).

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition gave audit sampling rates, an annual audit cycle and a list of common failures, none of which could be traced to a source, and they have been removed. Neither the conventions nor the specification says how often a scheme audits its members, how files are selected, what counts as a pass or what follows an unsuccessful audit. Convention 1.01 simply tells assessors to refer to scheme guidance for the use of RdSAP. Ask your accreditation scheme for its own audit rules.

Section 4 uses one accreditation scheme's "Evidence Required for Audit" list for RdSAP. It is that scheme's requirement, not a rule for every scheme. Its change record ends in February 2022 and it does not mention items such as pressure test certificates or PV batteries. Use your own scheme's current version.

## Section 1: What the Conventions Mean by Evidence

### Three kinds of evidence

The conventions rely on three kinds of evidence: what you see or measure on the day, photographs, and documents. Some entries accept more than one kind. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, upgraded elements in a converted building can be specified where documentary or visual evidence is available (convention 3.05a). Others accept only a document: a door is counted as insulated only if documentary evidence is provided (convention 3.09).

The starting position is "as built". Assume it wherever there is no evidence of retrofitted insulation (convention 3.03a).

### Documentary evidence in England, Wales and Northern Ireland

Convention 9.02 says acceptable documentary evidence includes, but is not limited to, official correspondence from the applicable Registered Social Landlord, certificates, warranties or guarantees, or any documents verifying that work has been carried out. You must be confident, and able to demonstrate, that the documentation relates to the actual property being assessed and/or that the work has been carried out, and that there is no physical or other documentary evidence to the contrary (convention 9.02).

Evidence of intent to install does not qualify on its own. It does qualify when supported by evidence of subsequent Building Control oversight, or by visual evidence that the upgrade has been undertaken (convention 9.02). The convention then sets out two cases:
- Rule (a): if you can demonstrate both an intention to upgrade the element (in planning documents, for example) and Building Control oversight of the work, the element can be treated as upgraded as indicated in the planning documents (convention 9.02).
- Rule (b): if you can demonstrate only one of those, but can also see that an upgrade has occurred, the element can be treated as upgraded using the minimum selectable upgrade of the relevant type defined in RdSAP. If the change is from "as built" to "insulated" and you have no evidence of thickness, use unknown insulation thickness. For roof insulation between joists, use 100 mm as the lowest selectable thickness (convention 9.02).

### Scotland

In Scotland, acceptable documentary evidence includes, but is not limited to, official correspondence from the applicable Registered Social Landlord or certificates, warranties and guarantees. The assessor must be confident, and able to demonstrate, that the documentation relates to the actual property and that there is no physical evidence to the contrary. Evidence of intent to install does not qualify, and the Scotland wording has no equivalent of rules (a) and (b) (convention 9.02, Scotland only).

### What is left to your scheme

Convention 9.02 gives an open list. The example scheme accepts guarantees, warranties, building control notices, letters from Registered Social Landlords, utility bills and installers' certificates, and says it cannot accept invoices as proof of installation (scheme evidence list). Convention 9.02 does not mention invoices, so check your own scheme's position. The conventions also do not say whether an occupier's verbal statement counts as evidence. Ask your scheme before relying on one.

## Section 2: Evidence for the Fabric

### Dimensions and walls

State on the site survey notes or plans whether the dimensions recorded are internal or external (convention 2.01). Do not mix the two (convention 2.18). If a basement is included in the assessment, internal dimensions must be used throughout (convention 2.05).

Measure wall thickness in mm for each building part and for any alternative wall within it. Evidence is required for each different thickness. Thickness can be measured at door or window reveals, or by comparing internal and external measurements (convention 2.22).

### Conversions and roof room age

For a conversion of a building originally intended to be heated, or a sub-divided dwelling, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, always use the original construction date and specify upgraded elements only where documentary or visual evidence is available (convention 3.05a). Scotland has separate wording (convention 3.05a, Scotland only). For a conversion of an unheated building, use the conversion date as the date of construction, based on documentary evidence such as the date of building control sign off (convention 3.05b).

The age band of a roof room is the same as the building part unless evidence proves otherwise. That evidence includes documents such as planning applications, and dated photographs of the property (convention 3.13).

### Lofts and roofs

Where safe and practicable access to the loft is possible, measure the loft insulation and provide photographic evidence of its measured thickness (convention 3.04). Convention 3.04 then separates four situations:
- "No access" means there is no loft hatch or other means of gaining access to the loft space.
- If there is a hatch or other means of access but it could not be used on the day, for example because it is painted over, record "access, loft insulation unknown".
- If the insulation is fully obstructed by boarding or stored items, enter "pitched, access, loft insulation unknown" unless the householder has documentary evidence (maximum thickness is the depth of the joists), lifts the boards or removes the obstructions.
- If the loft or part of it is boarded and you can establish and evidence the insulation under the boards at multiple locations, through gaps or extending in from the edges, treat the boarded area as insulated to the thickness the evidence proves.

"Unknown" needs care, because selecting it automatically suppresses any insulation recommendation (convention 3.03b). Use "unknown" insulation type only in exceptional circumstances, such as conflicting evidence, a fully boarded or obstructed loft, or a pitched roof with no access or access prevented and no documentary evidence. In these cases clarification must be provided in site notes (convention 3.03b). "Unknown" thickness is likewise for exceptional circumstances: conflicting evidence of thickness, or insulation you can see but cannot measure (convention 3.03c).

### Windows and doors

Choose unknown glazing date if there is no evidence of the date. Multiple glazed units can be dated from the manufacturing date on the spacer bar or frame, from documentary evidence of the installation date such as a FENSA, CERTASS or Building Control certificate or a manufacturer's guarantee, or from the property build date if it is after the trigger dates in the convention (convention 3.12a).

If secondary glazing has been removed for the summer, enter it only if you can confirm that the panels exist and can be re-fitted, and record the evidence on site notes (convention 2.16).

Default window U-values and g-values can be replaced with known data only if documentary evidence is provided, which can be a Window Energy Rating certificate as defined by BFRC or manufacturer's data. The U-value is for the whole window, not the centre pane (convention 3.10). A door is counted as insulated only with documentary evidence that includes a U-value, or a manufacturer reference that lets you obtain the U-value from the manufacturer (convention 3.09).

### Overwriting a U-value

Default U-values for walls, roofs and floors must not be overwritten unless there is specific documentary evidence of the thermal conductivity of the individual materials of the element in the property being assessed, with the calculation undertaken in accordance with BR 443, Conventions for U-value calculations (convention 3.08). The evidence must be provided and recorded, and must be one of two things:
- relevant building control approval that both correctly defines the construction and states the calculated U-value
- a U-value calculation produced or verified by a person with suitable expertise and experience (convention 3.08).

Suitable expertise and experience can be shown by, but is not limited to, membership of a recognised U-value calculation competency scheme, or OCDEA or Level 4 non-domestic energy assessor membership, or any other process recognised by accreditation schemes or approved organisations and Government. For Scotland, the convention's footnote to OCDEA reads: membership of an Approved Organisation scheme for EPCs for new domestic buildings (convention 3.08).

## Section 3: Evidence for Services, Renewables and Recommendations

### Heating

If a heating source or heat emitters are present but not accessible for inspection, and no documentary evidence is made available, the assessment cannot be completed until access has been provided, community heating excepted (convention 4.03).

Several heating entries stay at a default unless a document supports something else:
- Design flow temperature for condensing boilers and heat pumps is recorded as unknown unless there is documentary evidence that the system has been designed and commissioned as a low temperature one (convention 4.01b).
- For heat pumps, MCS installation is "yes" only if documentary evidence is available (specification Table 31, item 7-4).
- A Heat-as-a-Service installation needs specific documentary evidence to confirm its presence (convention 4.05b).
- Where two main systems serve the same heating circuit, assume a 50/50 split. A different ratio can be used only with clear documentary evidence (convention 4.09).
- The fuel type "LPG subject to special condition 18" is entered if documentary evidence, such as billing information, confirms the property receives LPG at mains gas prices (convention 4.11).

Heating control from a mobile application can be included only where a fixed receiver is present and there is evidence of the type of controls: time, temperature, zone (convention 4.19).

### PV, batteries and diverters

Potential sources for the peak power of a PV array include the system specification documents or the schematic wiring diagram. Connection of the PV to the dwelling's meter must be verified by the presence of a PV generation meter or by documentary evidence (convention 9.05).

Batteries and diverters are treated differently when evidence is missing. For a PV battery, photographic or written evidence of the manufacturer, model and usable capacity in kWh is required, and if it cannot be obtained a default of 5 kWh per battery is used. For a PV diverter, written evidence of the manufacturer and model is required, and if it cannot be obtained the diverter cannot be entered (convention 9.05).

### Other technologies

Flue gas heat recovery and waste water heat recovery are included only if the product is found in the PCDB. If the model cannot be found there is no default and the device is left out of the assessment, but its presence should be recorded in site notes (conventions 9.06 and 9.08). Documentary evidence is required to overwrite the default values for a wind turbine (convention 9.07a). Small-scale hydro is recorded as a simple yes or no, along with basic documentary evidence confirming its presence (convention 9.07b).

### Air pressure tests

The default must not be replaced with a pressure test result unless specific documentary evidence is available. That evidence is a pressure test certificate, from a blower door test at 50 Pa or a low-pressure pulse test at 4 Pa, produced by a person with suitable expertise and experience, which can be shown by membership of a recognised air tightness Competent Persons Scheme approved by Government. If material changes have been made to the property since the test, the certificate becomes invalid for RdSAP entry (convention 10.01).

### Recommendations

Recommendations should be removed only if there is documentary evidence showing that a specific recommendation is not appropriate. A listed building or a property in a conservation area is not sufficient grounds in its own right. If a recommendation is removed, this must be recorded in site notes (convention 8.01). The specification adds that it is recorded with reasons (specification section 16).

## Section 4: One Scheme's Evidence List and a Pre-Lodgement Check

### What one scheme asks to see

The example scheme's test is that the evidence must be sufficient for an auditor to re-create the EPC without using the data input report, so every entry must be supported by a photograph, floor plan sketch, written site notes or other acceptable documentary evidence (scheme evidence list). That one scheme's list includes:
- Photographs of all elevations appropriate to the detachment of the property, showing the dwelling from its highest to its lowest extent.
- Wall construction: evidence of build type, wall thickness measurements and retrofitted insulation.
- Evidence of access to the loft space or lack of it, and loft insulation photographs showing both depth and overall coverage.
- Photographs of roof rooms, openings, heating systems and controls, and the hot water cylinder including insulation type and depth. If a cylinder thermostat is assumed, that should be documented in site notes.
- The electricity meter, evidence of heating fuel, a selection of low energy lamps, and evidence to support any renewable or low-carbon technology.
- A floor plan sketch showing the outer envelope of all levels and all parts, with every dimension needed to verify floor area, heat loss perimeter and ceiling height, and the heat loss walls marked (scheme evidence list).

That scheme also requires photographs to be date stamped with a date appropriate to the assessment, and does not accept undated or indistinct photographs. Where a required photograph cannot be taken, the reason must be detailed in site notes (scheme evidence list). Your scheme's rules on dates, formats and upload deadlines may differ, so read them.

### Pre-lodgement check

Before lodging, ask:
- Do the site notes or plans say whether dimensions are internal or external (convention 2.01)?
- Is there evidence for each different wall thickness (convention 2.22)?
- Is there a photograph of the measured loft insulation thickness (convention 3.04)?
- Is every "unknown" insulation entry explained in site notes (convention 3.03b)?
- Does every document relate to this property, with nothing on site contradicting it, and is nothing entered on intent to install alone (convention 9.02)?
- Is every replaced default backed by the document the convention names (conventions 3.08, 3.09, 3.10, 4.01b and 10.01)?
- Is there written evidence of manufacturer and model for any diverter, and is PV connection to the dwelling's meter verified (convention 9.05)?
- Is heat recovery equipment that is not in the PCDB noted in site notes (conventions 9.06 and 9.08)?
- Is any removed recommendation supported by a document and recorded in site notes (convention 8.01)?

Other conventions that turn on evidence include 2.23 (sloping sites), 2.27 (curtain walls), 3.07 (thermal conductivity of insulation), 3.11b (shutters), 4.09 (heating split), 4.18 (pump age), 7.03a (mechanical ventilation), 9.09 (solar water heating) and 9.13 (meters and tariffs).

### If you find an error after lodgement

If you lodge an EPC in error and lodge a corrected one, inform your accreditation scheme so that the erroneous one can be marked "not for issue" (convention 9.03). The conventions say nothing more, so follow your scheme's procedure.

## Key Takeaways
- Audit frequency, selection and outcomes are not in the conventions or the specification. Ask your scheme. Convention 1.01 says only to refer to scheme guidance for the use of RdSAP.
- Documentary evidence must relate to the actual property, with no physical evidence to the contrary. The England, Wales and Northern Ireland wording adds "or other documentary evidence" (convention 9.02).
- Intent to install alone is not evidence. Rules (a) and (b) apply in England, Wales and Northern Ireland only (convention 9.02).
- Measured loft insulation needs a photograph of the measured thickness (convention 3.04), and "unknown" insulation must be explained in site notes (convention 3.03b).
- Defaults for U-values, windows, doors, air permeability and design flow temperature are replaced only with the documents the conventions name (conventions 3.08, 3.10, 3.09, 10.01 and 4.01b).
- A PV battery without evidence defaults to 5 kWh. A PV diverter without written evidence of manufacturer and model cannot be entered (convention 9.05).
- A removed recommendation needs documentary evidence and a site note (convention 8.01).

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** In England, planning documents show an intention to add internal wall insulation, there is no evidence of Building Control oversight, you can see that the upgrade has occurred, and there is no evidence of its thickness. Under convention 9.02, how can the wall be entered?

A) As built, because evidence of intent to install never counts
B) As insulated, using the minimum selectable upgrade with unknown insulation thickness
C) As insulated to the thickness shown in the planning documents
D) With "unknown" insulation type

**Correct answer: B.** Rule (b) of convention 9.02 applies where only one of intent or Building Control oversight can be shown but the upgrade can be seen: the element can be treated as upgraded using the minimum selectable upgrade, with unknown thickness if none is evidenced.

---

**Q2.** A loft hatch is present but has been painted over and could not be opened on the day of the visit. No documentary evidence is available. What does convention 3.04 say to record?

A) No access
B) As built
C) Insulation at joists, 100 mm
D) Access, loft insulation unknown

**Correct answer: D.** Convention 3.04 reserves "no access" for cases with no loft hatch or other means of access. A hatch that could not be used on the day is "access, loft insulation unknown".

---

**Q3.** A PV battery is installed but no photographic or written evidence of its manufacturer, model or usable capacity can be obtained. What does convention 9.05 require?

A) Enter the battery with a default of 5 kWh
B) Leave the battery out of the assessment
C) Estimate the capacity from the size of the unit
D) Enter the battery with a default of 4 kWh

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 9.05 says that if the evidence cannot be obtained, a default of 5 kWh per battery is used. A PV diverter cannot be entered without written evidence of manufacturer and model.

---

**Q4.** Which of these would satisfy convention 3.08 as evidence for overwriting a default wall U-value?

A) The householder's statement of the insulation thickness
B) A typical U-value for that construction taken from a textbook
C) Building control approval that correctly defines the construction and states the calculated U-value
D) A manufacturer's brochure for a similar insulation product

**Correct answer: C.** Convention 3.08 accepts either relevant building control approval that both defines the construction and states the calculated U-value, or a U-value calculation produced or verified by a person with suitable expertise and experience.

---

**Q5.** When may a recommendation be removed from the EPC?

A) Whenever the property is a listed building or in a conservation area
B) Only where documentary evidence shows the specific recommendation is not appropriate, with the removal recorded in site notes
C) Whenever the householder says they do not want the measure
D) Never

**Correct answer: B.** Convention 8.01 allows removal only with documentary evidence that a specific recommendation is not appropriate, and requires the removal to be recorded in site notes. Listed or conservation area status is not sufficient in its own right.
