

# Scheme Operating Requirements 2026: What's Changed for Energy Assessors

## Introduction

On 8 September 2026, ECMK confirmed that the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG) had agreed a revised set of Scheme Operating Requirements governing accreditation schemes for Energy Assessors in England and Wales. These requirements define the rules under which schemes such as Elmhurst, Quidos, CIBSE, Stroma, and others must operate — and, by direct extension, they define the compliance envelope within which every individual assessor works.

This is not a cosmetic refresh. The 2026 SOR represent the most substantive revision to scheme governance since the original framework was consolidated, and they respond to several years of accumulated evidence from MHCLG's own monitoring, scheme audit data, consumer complaints, and cross-referencing against the EPC register. The direction of travel is unambiguous: tighter quality assurance, more granular audit criteria, stronger enforcement powers for schemes, higher expectations around data accuracy, and a more structured approach to ongoing competency.

For working assessors, the practical significance is immediate. Audit sampling rates, the criteria against which lodged EPCs are reviewed, the consequences of identified non-compliance, the CPD requirements you must evidence, and the complaints procedures that apply if a consumer or client challenges your work — all of these are governed by the SOR. When the SOR changes, the ground beneath your practice shifts, whether or not your scheme has yet updated its member-facing guidance.

This course strips out the administrative boilerplate and focuses on what has materially changed, why MHCLG has made these changes, and what you need to do differently. We will examine revised audit and quality frameworks, updated competency and CPD structures, new data-quality and lodgement obligations, and the practical compliance steps you should be taking now.

Where the 2026 SOR intersects with technical standards — for example, the correct treatment of glazing data in energy calculations, referencing standards such as [L38] BS 6262-2: Glazing for Buildings: Energy, light and sound — we will note those intersections. But the primary focus here is governance, compliance, and your obligations as an accredited assessor.

If you are practising, this affects you. Read carefully.

## Section 1: Revised Audit Framework and Quality Assurance Mechanisms

The audit regime has always been the principal enforcement mechanism through which schemes ensure assessor quality. The 2026 SOR introduces significant changes to how audits are structured, what triggers them, and how their outcomes feed into your compliance record.

**Risk-Based Audit Stratification**

Previous SOR editions operated broadly on a fixed audit sampling model — schemes were required to audit a defined proportion of each assessor's lodged assessments, with some latitude for risk-based escalation. The 2026 SOR formalises a tiered, risk-stratified audit model. Assessors are categorised based on a composite risk score derived from factors including: length of accreditation, historical audit outcomes, complaint history, volume of lodgements, and statistical outlier analysis of lodged data. Higher-risk assessors face materially increased audit frequency and depth. Newly accredited assessors are automatically placed in the highest audit tier for a defined initial period.

The specific sampling rates and tier thresholds are set by each scheme within parameters defined by the SOR. Assessors should verify their scheme's published audit policy to confirm the exact rates applicable to them.

**Expanded Audit Scope**

Critically, the 2026 SOR broadens the audit scope beyond the lodged EPC or assessment document itself. Schemes are now explicitly required to audit supporting evidence — photographs, measurement records, construction notes, and the rationale for professional judgements such as age-band assumptions or element U-value selections. If you are recording glazing specifications, for example, your evidence should be consistent with the technical parameters described in standards such as [L38] BS 6262-2: Glazing for Buildings: Energy, light and sound, particularly where you have deviated from default values in the calculation methodology.

**Automated Outlier Detection**

The SOR now mandates that schemes deploy automated statistical analysis tools to flag lodgements that fall outside expected parameters for a given property type, age, and region. If your lodged data consistently produces anomalous results — unusually high or low energy ratings relative to comparable properties — this will trigger targeted review regardless of your risk tier.

**Audit Outcome Consequences**

The 2026 SOR strengthens the consequence framework. Schemes must apply a defined escalation pathway: advisory notice, formal warning, supervised practice, suspension, and removal. The revised SOR reduces scheme discretion to downgrade sanctions, meaning that repeated audit failures carry harder consequences than under the previous framework.

For assessors, the message is direct: your file for every assessment must be audit-ready from the moment you lodge. Retrospective reconstruction of evidence is itself now flagged as a compliance concern.

## Section 2: Updated Competency, CPD, and Re-Accreditation Requirements

The 2026 SOR introduces a more structured competency framework, closing what MHCLG had identified as inconsistencies between schemes in how ongoing competence was assessed and how CPD was validated.

**Minimum CPD Hours and Content Requirements**

Under the revised SOR, schemes must require assessors to complete a minimum annual CPD commitment. While the precise hourly requirement is set by each scheme within SOR parameters, the critical change is the introduction of mandatory content categories. A proportion of CPD must now address core regulatory and technical topics specified by MHCLG — these include changes to Building Regulations Part L and Part F, updates to the Standard Assessment Procedure (SAP) and Reduced Data SAP (RdSAP) methodologies, and scheme operating requirements themselves. Generic professional development no longer satisfies the full CPD obligation.

This means assessors must actively select CPD that maps to the mandated categories. Keeping a CPD log that records not just hours but the specific category each activity addresses is now essential for demonstrating compliance at audit or re-accreditation.

**Competency Assessment at Re-Accreditation**

The previous SOR allowed schemes considerable latitude in how they assessed competency at re-accreditation — some schemes relied primarily on CPD records and audit history, while others required periodic testing. The 2026 SOR mandates that all schemes incorporate a competency assessment component at each re-accreditation cycle. The form this takes — online examination, practical assessment, case-study review, or a combination — is for schemes to determine, but the requirement for a substantive assessment (not merely an administrative renewal) is now a firm SOR obligation.

**Technical Knowledge Updates**

Assessors are expected to demonstrate current knowledge of relevant technical standards. Where assessments involve recording glazing data, for instance, familiarity with [L38] BS 6262-2: Glazing for Buildings: Energy, light and sound is relevant to understanding how glazing performance characteristics feed into energy calculations and to justifying non-default inputs. The SOR does not prescribe a reading list, but it does require schemes to ensure that assessors' technical knowledge is current, and audit questioning may now test your understanding of the standards underpinning your input assumptions.

**New Entrant Supervision**

The 2026 SOR also formalises supervision requirements for newly qualified assessors. Schemes must implement a structured probationary period during which new assessors receive enhanced audit scrutiny and, where feasible, mentoring or peer review. This is a direct response to data showing that a disproportionate share of lodgement errors originates from assessors in their first year of practice.

For established assessors, the supervision obligation may also create opportunities — and potentially requirements — to act as mentors or peer reviewers within your scheme.

## Section 3: Data Quality, Lodgement Obligations, and Complaints Handling

Two further areas of material change in the 2026 SOR deserve close attention: data quality at the point of lodgement, and the complaints and disciplinary framework.

**Data Quality and Lodgement Standards**

MHCLG has been concerned for some time that the EPC register contains a significant volume of inaccurate or inconsistent data. The 2026 SOR responds by imposing stricter obligations on schemes to validate data at the point of lodgement, and on assessors to ensure the accuracy and completeness of their submissions.

Schemes must now implement pre-lodgement validation checks that flag common data-entry errors, implausible values, and missing fields before a certificate is registered. For assessors, this means that lodgement software may reject or query submissions that would previously have been accepted without challenge. You should expect more frequent prompts to confirm or evidence non-standard inputs.

Additionally, the SOR now requires assessors to retain a complete evidence file for each lodged assessment for a minimum defined retention period (assessors should confirm the exact period with their scheme, as it is set within SOR-defined parameters). This file must include photographic evidence, measurements, notes on professional judgements, and any correspondence with the client or property owner relevant to the assessment. The evidence file must be producible within a defined timeframe if requested by the scheme for audit, complaint investigation, or regulatory review.

**Corrections and Amendments**

The 2026 SOR establishes a clearer process for correcting lodged assessments. Where an error is identified — whether by the assessor, the scheme, or a third party — the SOR requires schemes to have a formal amendment pathway that records the original data, the correction, and the reason for the change. Assessors who identify errors in their own lodged data are expected to initiate corrections proactively rather than waiting for audit detection. Self-correction is now treated as a positive compliance indicator in audit scoring.

**Complaints and Disciplinary Procedures**

The revised SOR tightens the complaints-handling framework. Schemes must acknowledge complaints within a defined timeframe, investigate within a defined window, and communicate outcomes to both the complainant and the assessor. The SOR now requires schemes to publish anonymised complaint outcome summaries, increasing transparency.

For assessors, the key practical change is that complaint investigations may now draw on a wider evidence base — including the assessor's evidence file, lodgement data analytics, and audit history — rather than relying solely on the specific assessment under complaint. A pattern of minor issues across multiple assessments can now be aggregated and treated as a systemic compliance concern, even if no single assessment crosses a disciplinary threshold in isolation.

Disciplinary outcomes under the 2026 SOR also carry greater portability: schemes are required to share certain disciplinary data with other schemes and with MHCLG, reducing the ability of an assessor subject to sanctions to simply re-register with a different scheme.

## Section 4: Practical Compliance — What You Should Be Doing Now

Theory is straightforward. Implementation is where assessors either maintain their accreditation or lose it. Here is what the 2026 SOR changes mean in practical terms for your day-to-day work.

**Review Your Scheme's Updated Policies**

Every accreditation scheme will publish revised policies, guidance, and member communications to reflect the 2026 SOR. Read them. Do not rely on summaries, forum discussions, or assumptions carried over from previous requirements. If your scheme has not yet published updated guidance, request a timeline — and document your request.

**Build Audit-Ready Evidence Files as Standard**

If you are not already compiling a full evidence file for every assessment — photographs, annotated floor plans, measurement records, notes on construction assumptions, justification for any non-default inputs — start now. The cost of building this habit is modest; the cost of failing an evidence audit is not. Where you are recording glazing characteristics that depart from default assumptions, ensure your evidence demonstrates consistency with recognised standards such as [L38] BS 6262-2: Glazing for Buildings: Energy, light and sound.

**Map Your CPD to Mandatory Categories**

Audit your existing CPD record against the new mandatory content categories. Identify gaps. Plan your CPD calendar for the next twelve months to ensure coverage of all required categories, not just the topics you find most interesting or convenient.

**Prepare for Competency Assessment**

If your re-accreditation falls within the next cycle, anticipate the new competency assessment component. Review current SAP/RdSAP conventions, Building Regulations updates, and the technical standards relevant to your assessment work. Treat re-accreditation as a professional examination, not an administrative renewal.

**Proactively Correct Errors**

If you are aware of lodged assessments that contain errors — however minor — initiate the correction process with your scheme now. Under the 2026 SOR, self-correction is a compliance positive. Uncorrected known errors discovered at audit are a compliance negative with escalating consequences.

**Document Everything**

In a governance environment that is becoming more evidence-based and less discretionary, documentation is your primary defence. If it is not recorded, it did not happen.

## Key Takeaways

- The 2026 SOR introduces risk-stratified audit tiers, meaning your audit frequency and depth now directly reflect your compliance history, lodgement volume, and data patterns.
- Audit scope has expanded beyond the lodged document to include supporting evidence files, photographs, measurement records, and justifications for professional judgements.
- CPD requirements now include mandatory content categories aligned with regulatory and technical priorities — generic CPD alone no longer satisfies your obligation.
- Re-accreditation now requires a substantive competency assessment, not merely administrative renewal and CPD log submission.
- Data quality obligations at lodgement are stricter, with pre-lodgement validation checks and defined evidence retention periods enforced by schemes.
- Self-correction of identified lodgement errors is now a positive compliance indicator; failure to self-correct is an escalating risk factor.
- Disciplinary outcomes are now more portable across schemes, and complaint investigations can aggregate patterns across multiple assessments.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Question 1:** Under the 2026 SOR, which factor is NOT typically used in calculating an assessor's risk-based audit tier?

A) Length of accreditation
B) Historical audit outcomes
C) The assessor's geographic location
D) Statistical outlier analysis of lodged data

**Correct answer: C.** Risk tier calculation is based on factors such as accreditation history, audit outcomes, complaint history, lodgement volume, and data outlier analysis. Geographic location is not a specified risk factor in the audit stratification model.

---

**Question 2:** What is the significance of the 2026 SOR's requirement for schemes to audit supporting evidence files?

A) It replaces the need for schemes to review the lodged EPC itself.
B) It means assessors must be able to produce photographs, measurements, and professional judgement notes for audited assessments.
C) It applies only to newly accredited assessors during their probationary period.
D) It is advisory guidance rather than a mandatory scheme obligation.

**Correct answer: B.** The expanded audit scope requires assessors to evidence their inputs with photographs, measurements, and notes — not just the final lodged document. This applies to all assessors, not just new entrants, and is a mandatory scheme obligation.

---

**Question 3:** Under the revised CPD framework, what is the key structural change?

A) Total CPD hours have been abolished in favour of peer assessment.
B) A proportion of CPD must now cover mandatory content categories specified by MHCLG.
C) CPD is now only required at re-accreditation, not annually.
D) Assessors may self-certify all CPD without scheme verification.

**Correct answer: B.** The 2026 SOR introduces mandatory CPD content categories covering core regulatory and technical topics. Assessors must ensure their CPD portfolio addresses these specified areas, not merely accumulate generic hours.

---

**Question 4:** How does the 2026 SOR treat an assessor who proactively corrects a known error in a lodged assessment?

A) The assessor is automatically referred for disciplinary review.
B) Self-correction is treated as a positive compliance indicator in audit scoring.
C) Self-correction is only permitted within 24 hours of lodgement.
D) The assessor must withdraw the EPC entirely and re-inspect the property.

**Correct answer: B.** The 2026 SOR explicitly recognises proactive self-correction as a positive compliance behaviour. Assessors who identify and correct their own errors demonstrate professional responsibility, which is reflected favourably in audit and compliance assessments.

---

**Question 5:** What change does the 2026 SOR make to the portability of disciplinary outcomes between accreditation schemes?

A) Disciplinary records are permanently sealed and cannot be shared.
B) Schemes are now required to share certain disciplinary data with other schemes and with MHCLG.
C) Assessors may opt out of disciplinary data sharing by written request.
D) Portability applies only to criminal convictions, not scheme-level sanctions.

**Correct answer: B.** The 2026 SOR requires schemes to share specified disciplinary information with other accreditation schemes and with MHCLG, preventing assessors subject to sanctions from avoiding consequences by transferring to a different scheme.

Published by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government. The primary regulatory source for all SOR obligations. Assessors should obtain the current edition directly from MHCLG or via their accreditation scheme. [Check GOV.UK for the latest published version.]

2. **ECMK — Announcement: Updated Scheme Operating Requirements, 8 September 2026**
The formal announcement confirming MHCLG agreement to the revised SOR. Available via the ECMK website.

3. **[L38] BS 6262-2: Glazing for Buildings — Part 2: Energy, light and sound**
British Standards Institution. Relevant to assessors recording glazing specifications and justifying non-default energy calculation inputs. Available from the BSI Knowledge portal: https://knowledge.bsi.group

4. **Approved Document L: Conservation of fuel and power (current edition)**
MHCLG / HM Government. The regulatory context for energy performance standards in buildings, directly relevant to EPC assessment methodology. Available at: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/conservation-of-fuel-and-power-approved-document-l

5. **RdSAP Conventions (current edition)**
Published by BRE on behalf of MHCLG. The conventions document governs how assessors record dwelling characteristics for EPC production. Assessors should confirm they are working to the current edition. Available from the BRE or via accreditation scheme resources.

6. **Your Accreditation Scheme's Updated Member Guidance**
Each scheme will publish its own interpretation and implementation of the 2026 SOR. This is the most directly actionable reference for your individual compliance obligations. Check your scheme's member portal.
