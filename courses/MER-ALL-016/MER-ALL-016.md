# Scheme Operating Requirements: How They Work and What Changed in 2026
**Course ID:** MER-ALL-016 | **CPD Hours:** 1 | **Published:** October 2026
*© Meridian CPD 2026. All rights reserved.*

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- Explain how the Energy Performance of Buildings (England and Wales) Regulations 2012 link you, your accreditation scheme and the Scheme Operating Requirements (SORs).
- Describe the two levels of the SORs, who owns each, and the part played by the Energy Assessor Scheme Operating Board (EASOB).
- Summarise the standing SOR rules on audit, evidence, defective certificates, CPD, complaints and discipline.
- State what version 1.14 of the Level 2 SORs, dated 4 September 2026, changed, including the pre-lodgement declaration and the minimum evidence requirements.
- Tell apart what the SOR documents require from what is still a government proposal.

---

## Introduction

Every EPC you lodge in England or Wales goes through an accreditation scheme, and every scheme works to a common set of rules, the Scheme Operating Requirements. This course explains where those rules come from, what they say about your work, and what changed when the Level 2 SORs were reissued in September 2026.

It covers England and Wales only. The 2012 Regulations "extend to England and Wales" (SI 2012/3118, regulation 1(3)), and Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own schemes and requirements (Level 1 SORs, Preface).

The course is built on the SOR documents as published on EASOB's website: Level 1 (2025 edition, version 1.5), Level 2 (2026 edition, version 1.14) and the DEA Minimum Evidence Requirements (version 1.1). Only about the first 50 of the 81 pages of the Level 2 document could be read, so its later appendices (detailed audit process, consequences of audit failure, software error tolerance, EASOB's terms of reference, glossary) are not taught here. No scheme's news item was used; your own scheme's notice is where to check how it applies the change.

This edition replaces the September 2026 edition. That edition was written without the SOR text. It taught risk-score audit tiers, CPD categories set by MHCLG, a competence test at every re-accreditation, audit credit for self-correction, published complaint summaries and a link to a glazing standard (BS 6262-2), none of which appears in the 2026 change history or in the parts of the SORs read here. It presented long-standing rules as new, described an "amendment pathway" where the rule is replacement, said the SORs govern retrofit assessors, and missed the real changes: the pre-lodgement declaration and the minimum evidence requirements.

This course is correct to 14 October 2026.

## Section 1: Where the SORs Come From

### The legal hook

The Energy Performance of Buildings (England and Wales) Regulations 2012, SI 2012/3118, came into force on 9 January 2013 (regulation 1(4)). "An energy assessor must be a member of an accreditation scheme approved by the Secretary of State" (regulation 22(1)), and an energy assessor is "an individual who is a member of an accreditation scheme" (regulation 2(1)). Your ability to produce certificates rests on that membership.

Before approving a scheme, the Secretary of State must be satisfied that it makes adequate provision for members to carry out "consistent and accurate energy assessments in an independent manner", for members to be fit, proper and qualified, for standard forms, a published code of conduct, indemnity, resolving complaints, entering members' data on the government register, and keeping a register of members (regulation 22(3)).

### Duties the Regulations place on you directly

Some duties sit on you by law, whatever your scheme says:
- Declare in an EPC or inspection report any personal or business relationship with the person who commissioned it, anyone on whose behalf it was commissioned, or anyone you believe has, or may have, an interest in the building or a relationship with either of them (regulation 23).
- Carry out energy assessments "with reasonable care and skill". The relevant person can enforce this, and so can a prospective or actual buyer or tenant while an EPC is valid (regulation 24).
- Make sure the data used to produce an EPC or DEC is entered on the register before you give the document to the person who asked for it (regulation 27(2)).

Registered data "shall not be altered once registered", green deal information apart (regulation 27(4)), and stays on the register for at least 20 years (regulation 27(5)). That is why a wrong certificate is replaced, not edited (Section 3).

### What kind of document the SORs are

The SORs are not legislation. In its 2016 consultation on scheme operations, the government explained that schemes "are bound by the conditions set out in the letter of approval" from the Secretary of State, including the requirement to comply with the SORs, which "set out the minimum operating standards for schemes" (Review of energy assessor accreditation scheme operations, May 2016). Level 1 calls itself the Secretary of State's "current minimum high-level requirements" for organisations approved to run a scheme under regulation 22 (Level 1 SORs, Preface). In May 2024 the then Department for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities tendered for independent audits of schemes "to ensure that they comply with" the SORs and the letter of approval, and said there were "currently six approved Schemes" (Find a Tender notice 015209-2024).

So the SORs bind schemes. They reach you through membership: schemes must "ensure that members comply with the Code of Conduct" (Level 1 SORs, section 4 i).

## Section 2: Who Writes Them: Level 1, Level 2 and EASOB

### Two levels

The SORs come as two documents, both titled "Scheme Operating Requirements for the production of Energy Certificates":
- Level 1: 2025 edition, version 1.5, approved by MHCLG on 20 June 2025. It is short and high level: scheme organisation, fit and proper checks, competence, obligations on schemes, surveillance, discipline, complaints, appeals and support (Level 1 SORs, contents and approval history).
- Level 2: 2026 edition, version 1.14, dated 4 September 2026 and approved by the chair of EASOB. It gives more detail on how schemes meet the requirements of their approval letters (Level 1 SORs, Preface), and points to the minimum evidence requirements for each strand on EASOB's website (Level 2 SORs, section 5.1.1).

The SORs cover EPCs for new and existing dwellings, non-domestic EPCs, display energy certificates (DECs) and air conditioning inspection reports (ACIRs) (Level 1 SORs, Preface).

### EASOB

EASOB describes itself as "a committee that comprises all Schemes and other stakeholders" that defines and documents the rules for producing energy certificates (EASOB website). Its 2020 description says Level 1 "is owned by MHCLG" and Level 2 "is owned by EASOB", which can draft, approve and implement Level 2 amendments and recommend Level 1 changes to MHCLG (About EASOB, version 1.1, September 2020).

The current Level 2 document says it is "managed on behalf of approved Accreditation Schemes" by EASOB, and that MHCLG holds the "Golden Vote": it "can stop any proposed amendment to Level 2" it considers inappropriate or unacceptable (Level 2 SORs, Ownership and Oversight). One scheme's code puts it simply: the SORs "are issued by EASOB for England & Wales" (Elmhurst Energy Code of Conduct, OP03 v33, February 2026).

So Level 1 changes are MHCLG's; Level 2 changes are made by the schemes through EASOB, subject to MHCLG's veto. The 2026 changes are all in Level 2; the Level 1 document on EASOB's website is still the 2025 edition.

### What the SORs do not cover

Retrofit assessment is not in the SORs' scope list (Level 1 SORs, Preface), and Elmhurst's code points retrofit assessors and coordinators to the TrustMark Code of Conduct (Elmhurst Code of Conduct, Scheme Rules). If you are a retrofit assessor, the SORs reach you only through the EPCs you lodge as a DEA.

## Section 3: The Standing Rules That Touch Your Work

### How quality is judged

Level 2 judges a certificate "in order of priority" on four tests: "on the balance of probability it accurately reflects the property being assessed"; where correct, the tolerance is appropriate for the strand; it meets the agreed Conventions; and it meets the strand's minimum evidence requirements (Level 2 SORs, section 5.1.1).

### Audit

The audit rules in the parts read are:
- At least 2% of certificates audited every 12 months, "a significant proportion" randomly sampled, and every member audited periodically (Level 1 SORs, section 5.1 i; Level 2 SORs, section 5.1.1).
- For DEAs, each member has minimum random audits of 0.5% of their lodgements per calendar year, and audits of certificates re-lodged after a failed audit, complaints and new entrants sit outside the 2% (Level 2 SORs, Appendix 5.1).
- Smart audits are "designed to find potentially defective Energy Certificates" and focus on the data and evidence linked to the rule triggered (Level 2 SORs, section 5.1.1). The DEA smart rules are version 1.8, from 1 July 2025, and include overridden U-values for any building part (windows and doors excluded), main building age band M, and no access to the main building loft (DEA SMART rules V1.8, rules 32, 34 and 12).

None of this is new. The government decided to adopt smart auditing in December 2016 (government's response, Review of energy assessor accreditation scheme operations), and its 2024 consultation described the 2% minimum and smart auditing as existing practice (Reforms to the Energy Performance of Buildings regime, chapter 5). How your audit rate rises after a failure sits in appendices not read here, so ask your scheme.

### Evidence

The Level 2 audit evidence clauses say:
- Evidence must be clear enough for the scheme "to replicate the Energy Certificate" (section 5.1.1, audit evidence clause 1).
- It must be "unique to the property visited and be reflective of the nominated date" (clause 2).
- It is floor plans, photographic and/or documentary evidence and other documents supporting your inputs and assumptions, with the minimum per data item in the strand's Minimum Evidence Requirements (clause 3).
- You should always make sure you can get to it, "even if stored in previous employer's files" (clause 4).
- Schemes should not keep audit evidence longer than necessary, and not beyond 10 years from the date of audit (clause 5).

### Defective certificates

Because register data cannot be altered (regulation 27(4)), a wrong certificate is replaced. Members must "re-lodge an Energy Certificate where an earlier Energy Certificate was found to be defective" and ask their scheme to mark the defective one "Not for Issue" (Level 2 SORs, section 5.1.1, re-lodgement audits). If the member is struck off, suspended, no longer practising or refusing to cooperate, the scheme does it (Level 2 SORs, section 4.3). Convention 9.03 says the same from your side: if you lodge an EPC in error and lodge a corrected one, tell your scheme so the wrong one can be marked "not for issue" (RdSAP 10 Conventions v12.2).

### CPD

Schemes must ensure members maintain competence by "undertaking and recording continuing professional development" (Level 1 SORs, section 4 iii). Level 2 Appendix 4.2 sets the detail:
- At least 10 hours a year, plus 5 hours a year for each additional strand.
- At least 50% "certified", meaning validated by a certificate of attendance or completion.
- Submit your CPD record at membership renewal. Schemes check at least 5% of members a year, and failure to submit CPD leads to suspension until a satisfactory record is provided.
- Remedial training ordered after a disciplinary issue "shall not be counted" towards the annual requirement, and schemes require a personal development plan.
- Schemes "must not mandate training with any single training or CPD provider".

Schemes "define the types of CPD which they believe are appropriate" (Appendix 4.2), so check with yours before relying on any course for certified hours.

### Complaints and discipline

The main points are:
- Schemes run a free complaints process, "dealt with in a timely manner", with an independent third-party appeal panel (Level 1 SORs, section 7.1). gov.uk tells the public to complain to the assessor first, then to the assessor's scheme (Get a new energy certificate).
- You need your own complaints procedure, disclosed on request, and should notify your scheme of complaints you receive (Level 2 SORs, section 7.1, clauses 3 and 4).
- Discipline may follow audit failure, breach of obligations or ceasing to be fit and proper; measures must be proportionate, can include suspension or removal, and come with reasons and a right of appeal (Level 1 SORs, section 6.1; Level 2 SORs, section 6.4).
- A scheme must confirm a member's or former member's status and/or disciplinary history if another scheme asks (Level 1 SORs, section 4 xii).

## Section 4: What Changed in September 2026, and What to Do

### The change record

The change history of Level 2 version 1.14, dated 4 September 2026, lists:
- A minimum age: prospective members "must be at least 18 years of age at the time of accreditation" (Appendix 2, section 2.1, paragraph 7).
- Section 5.1.1 updated to refer to the new minimum evidence requirements, with earlier clauses removed and new clauses on an assessor declaration.
- Appendix 5.2 and all strand auditing processes updated to refer to the minimum evidence requirements.
- Section 8, paragraph 5: "recourse to EASOB removed".

That is all version 1.14 records as changed. CPD, the 2% minimum and the complaints rules are not on the list.

### The declaration

The new clauses in section 5.1.1 say:
- Before an EPC, DEC or ACIR is lodged, schemes must ensure that the assessor "makes a declaration confirming that the minimum evidence required" under the methodology, conventions and SORs, as set out in the minimum evidence requirements, "has been obtained and retained" (audit evidence clause 6).
- Schemes must have controls so it is completed "at the point of lodgement" (clause 7).
- Where declarations are "inaccurate or unsupported by retained evidence or recorded justification", schemes must take disciplinary and/or remedial action, which "may include suspension or removal from the Scheme for repeat offenders" (clause 8).

The parts of Level 2 read for this course give no start date for the declaration. How and when your software asks for it is for your scheme to tell you.

### The DEA Minimum Evidence Requirements

EASOB's website lists the DEA Minimum Evidence Requirements, version 1.1, as "effective 1st October 2026", with matching documents for the other strands. For DEAs:
- An auditor "must be able to replicate the EPC", and this is "a minimum evidence list only".
- Floor plan: "A clear sketch plan covering all levels", with measurements and area calculations of all building parts, window measurements and orientation, room layout, and heat loss and party walls identified, in metric to 2 decimal places.
- Site notes: mandatory data not covered elsewhere, and your reasoning on matters such as age, construction and primary heating.
- Photographs: "clear and date stamped, and geo-tagged where possible".
- Documentary evidence: in line with Convention 9.02, specific to the property, and not "a default substitute for a site visit or photographic evidence" where a photograph is reasonably obtainable; record its use, and why no photograph was obtained, in the site notes.

Convention 9.02 adds that "Evidence of intent to install does not on its own qualify" (RdSAP 10 Conventions v12.2).

### What has not changed

In December 2024 the government proposed "introducing specific requirements for CPD for assessors" (Reforms to the Energy Performance of Buildings regime, chapter 5). Its partial response of 21 January 2026 covered questions 1 to 21 only and promised the response on "managing EPC quality" in 2026 (paragraphs 1, 3 and 72). The consultation page, last updated 9 March 2026, showed no such response when checked. Those ideas remain proposals.

### Sensible habits

These are practice, not SOR requirements:
- Read your scheme's notice on version 1.14 and download the minimum evidence requirements for each strand you hold.
- Before declaring, check your evidence against the strand's list, not memory.
- Store evidence where you can reach it if you change employer.
- If you lodge in error, re-lodge and ask your scheme to mark the old certificate Not for Issue.

## Key Takeaways

The points to carry away:
- The 2012 Regulations require scheme membership; the SORs are the Secretary of State's minimum requirements on schemes, reaching you through membership.
- Level 1 (2025, version 1.5) is MHCLG's. Level 2 (2026, version 1.14) is managed by EASOB, with an MHCLG veto.
- The SORs cover EPCs, DECs and air conditioning reports in England and Wales, not retrofit assessment.
- Standing rules: at least 2% of certificates audited a year; smart audits; replacement and "Not for Issue" for defective certificates; 10 hours of CPD a year plus 5 per extra strand, at least half certified.
- Version 1.14 added a minimum age of 18 and a declaration before lodgement that the minimum evidence has been obtained and retained.
- The DEA Minimum Evidence Requirements are listed as effective from 1 October 2026.
- Inaccurate or unsupported declarations can lead to suspension or removal for repeat offenders.
- The December 2024 CPD proposals still await a government response.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Under the 2012 Regulations, which of these is a duty placed directly on the energy assessor?

A) Auditing at least 2% of lodgements every 12 months
B) Making sure the EPC data is entered on the register before giving the EPC to the person who asked for it
C) Publishing anonymised summaries of complaint outcomes
D) Approving accreditation schemes

**Correct answer: B.** Regulation 27(2) places the register duty on the assessor; the 2% audit is a scheme duty under the SORs and approval is the Secretary of State's (Sections 1 and 3).

---

**Q2.** Which statement about the SORs is correct?

A) Level 1 is MHCLG's; Level 2 is managed by EASOB for the schemes, and MHCLG can stop any Level 2 amendment
B) Both levels are statutory instruments made under the 2012 Regulations
C) Each scheme writes its own SORs and there is no common document
D) EASOB owns Level 1 and BRE owns Level 2

**Correct answer: A.** About EASOB and the Level 2 Ownership and Oversight sections say so; the SORs bind schemes through letters of approval, not as legislation (Sections 1 and 2).

---

**Q3.** Under Level 2 Appendix 4.2, what is the minimum CPD for an assessor accredited in the DEA strand and one other strand?

A) 10 hours a year, with no certification requirement
B) 4 hours a year
C) 20 hours a year, all of it certified
D) 15 hours a year, at least half of it certified

**Correct answer: D.** Appendix 4.2 sets at least 10 hours plus 5 for each additional strand, with at least 50% certified (Section 3).

---

**Q4.** What does version 1.14 of the Level 2 SORs require before an EPC is lodged?

A) A scheme auditor must check every EPC first
B) MHCLG must countersign the EPC
C) The assessor must declare that the minimum evidence required has been obtained and retained
D) The client must sign a declaration confirming the inputs

**Correct answer: C.** Section 5.1.1, audit evidence clauses 6 and 7, require the assessor's declaration at the point of lodgement (Section 4).

---

**Q5.** An EPC you lodged last week records the wrong wall type. What is the correct route?

A) Lodge a corrected EPC and ask your scheme to mark the wrong one "Not for Issue"
B) Edit the data already on the register
C) Ask MHCLG to amend the register record directly
D) Leave it until it is picked up at audit

**Correct answer: A.** Registered data "shall not be altered" (regulation 27(4)); Level 2 sections 4.3 and 5.1.1 require re-lodgement and marking the old one Not for Issue, and convention 9.03 tells you to inform your scheme (Sections 1 and 3).
