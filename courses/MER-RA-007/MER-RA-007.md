# Heating System Assessment for Retrofit: What the Retrofit Assessor Must Record
**Course ID:** MER-RA-007 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026
*© Meridian CPD 2026. All rights reserved.*

---

## Learning Objectives
- State what PAS 2035:2023 clause 7.3.2 j) requires the assessment to record about heating, hot water and controls, and what it does not ask for.
- Record age, service history, locations, areas served, working condition, fuel, tariff and the cylinder in a form the coordinator and designer can use.
- Tell what the RdSAP 10 data set needs from what the PAS 2035 record needs, and apply the conventions where they differ.
- Explain the PAS 2035:2023 process for the distressed replacement of a heating appliance and the assessor's part in it.
- Place heat pump design where PAS 2035:2023 and the MCS design standard put it.

---

## Introduction

This course covers the heating, hot water and controls part of a PAS 2035:2023 retrofit assessment: what to record, how to record it usefully, and where the RdSAP 10 entry and the PAS record part company. It is correct to 12 October 2026.

PAS 2035:2023 is a BSI specification, second edition September 2023, not legislation. Its "shall" statements bind you where a scheme, contract or funding rule adopts it; Ofgem, for example, says users of the TrustMark Government-endorsed quality scheme "are required to comply" with it (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, paragraph 4.11). It is not limited to England: Annex A names qualification registers in all four nations. The RdSAP rules come from the Conventions (v12.2) for RdSAP 10, applicable from 6 August 2026, and the RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0 (BRE, 7 June 2026). Where the conventions treat Scotland differently, the course says so.

The detail of entering heating in RdSAP is taught in MER-DEA-005 (main heating, controls, heat pumps), MER-DEA-006 (storage heaters and secondary heating) and MER-DEA-007 (hot water). This course points to them rather than repeating them.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition called its heating fields mandatory under PAS 2035:2023 Annex A, but Annex A covers qualifications; the heating requirement is clause 7.3.2 j), and it does not ask for serial numbers, efficiency figures, radiator sizes or coil size. The old edition also cited a TrustMark heating guidance document that could not be found, gave a web address for the Product Characteristics Database that neither RdSAP document uses, taught plug-in heaters as secondary heating and "standard" storage heaters and coil size as RdSAP entries, and presented heat pump readiness checks as assessment requirements. Unlearn those points.

## Section 1: What PAS 2035:2023 Asks For

**In short:** For heating, hot water and controls, record what is installed, its age and service history where available, where the equipment is, the areas served, and whether it is working correctly or otherwise; the items you record should be in the photographs. Serial numbers, efficiency figures and radiator sizes are not named as requirements, though item b) leaves open how much detail is enough. Whether the boiler should be replaced, or a heat pump would suit, is not your call.

### Clause 7.3.2 j)

Clause 7.3.2 lists what the assessment shall include, as items a) to l). Item j) covers heating. It requires (clause 7.3.2 j)):
- identification of the installed building services, including heating and hot water systems "and their controls"
- their "age and service history (where available)"
- the locations of the equipment
- the areas served
- confirmation that the systems are "working correctly (or otherwise)"

Item b) adds an appraisal of the installed building services "in sufficient detail to establish the suitability of the dwelling for improvement", and item k) an appraisal of occupancy, including any vulnerable persons (clause 7.3.2 b) and k)).

### What it does not ask for

Clause 7.3.2 does not mention boiler serial numbers, efficiency figures, radiator dimensions, pipe sizes, cylinder volume, coil size or a heat pump readiness checklist. None of those is named as a requirement, though item b) leaves open how much detail is enough. Annex A, which the old edition cited, sets out qualifications: clause 7.3.1 requires a Retrofit Assessor to be "trained and qualified in accordance with Annex A".

### Where the record goes

The data collected under 7.3.2 is used, with a recognised energy model such as RdSAP, SAP or PHPP, to estimate annual fuel use, fuel costs and carbon dioxide emissions under actual occupancy where data is available, or standard occupancy (clause 7.3.3). The assessment is reported to the Retrofit Designer with any RdSAP, SAP or PHPP data file and "a photographic record of all the recorded features of the building" (clause 7.4.1). So the boiler, controls, emitters and cylinder you record should be in the photographs.

The coordinator confirms with the designer that the report is sufficient for a design (clause 7.4.3). If it is not, the designer identifies the missing information and the coordinator arranges for the assessor to collate it and include it in an updated report (clauses 7.4.4 and 8.2.5). That is how a designer who needs radiator sizes, or anything else beyond 7.3.2, gets it.

### Who decides

The assessment exists "to provide information about the dwelling for use by a Retrofit Designer" (clause 7.1.1). The decisions belong to others:
- The Retrofit Coordinator carries out the improvement options evaluation and identifies the package behind the medium-term improvement plan (clauses 8.1.1 and 8.1.4).
- The Retrofit Designer designs from the assessment report (clause 8.2.8 c)). Clause 8.2.19, on measures that interact, says the capacity of any new heating appliances or emitters shall take account of the heat loss as affected by improvements to insulation, windows, doors and airtightness.

Whether the boiler should be replaced, or a heat pump would suit, is not the assessor's call.

## Section 2: Recording Heating So the Designer Can Use It

**In short:** A sensible habit is to record the installation date with its source, labelling the occupant's recollection as such; if no paperwork is produced, record that you asked. Keep what you observed separate from what the occupant reports. In RdSAP, a fixed heating or hot water source that is fitted but not working is still taken into account, while your PAS record must say whether it works. A recent bill, seen with the occupant's agreement, gives the coordinator the actual tariff.

Clause 7.3.2 j) says what to record but not how. What follows is practice, except where a reference is given.

### Age and service history

Both are required "where available" (clause 7.3.2 j)), and neither PAS 2035 nor the RdSAP documents set an evidence rule for an installation date. A sensible habit is to record the date and its source (data plate, commissioning certificate, service record, or the occupant's recollection, labelled as such), and the last service date and who did it. If no paperwork is produced, record that you asked.

PAS 2035 guidance notes that a heating system usually has a shorter life than improved fabric, "typically 15 years" against possibly 60 (clause 5.5.2), and that a worn-out boiler should be replaced even if insulation cannot be afforded, though it will be oversized once insulation goes in (clause 5.5.3). Clause 5 is guidance, not requirement, but it shows why the coordinator needs your record of age and condition.

### Locations and areas served

Record where each item is: boiler or heat pump, cylinder, programmer, room thermostat and other controls (clause 7.3.2 j)). Record which rooms each system serves, and where one system feeds both radiators and underfloor heating, which rooms each emitter type serves. Marking them on the floor plan is quick and ties in with the photographs clause 7.4.1 requires.

### Working correctly, or otherwise

Keep what you observed (a fault code, one radiator cold, the system off at the visit) separate from what the occupant reports (rooms that never get warm, hot water running out). The designer can then weigh each.

Here the PAS record and RdSAP diverge. In RdSAP, a fixed heating or hot water source that is fitted but not working, or condemned, is still taken into account when deciding the main and secondary heating and the hot water type (convention 4.03). The RdSAP entry describes what is installed; clause 7.3.2 j) requires the record to say whether it works.

Portable heaters are another case. A plug-in oil-filled radiator is not secondary heating in RdSAP, which includes secondary heating where a "fixed emitter" is present, with electric focal point fires the stated exception (convention 5.01). That the occupant relies on one may still be worth a line under the occupancy appraisal (clause 7.3.2 k)).

### Controls

For the PAS record, list each control, where it is and whether it works. For RdSAP, controls come from SAP 10.2 Table 4e; since specification version 4.0 a smart thermostat is taken from the PCDB (specification section 10.3; Table 31 item 7-7), and TRVs count only when on 50% or more of the radiators (convention 4.13). MER-DEA-005 covers these rules.

### Fuel and tariff

RdSAP records the main heating fuel (Table 31 item 7-2) and the electricity meter (specification section 12; convention 9.13). For the improvement options evaluation, PAS 2035 requires the coordinator to use the actual tariffs applicable to the dwelling if available, and otherwise the national averages in SAP (clause 8.1.3 b)). A recent bill, seen with the occupant's agreement, gives the coordinator that, and RdSAP uses billing evidence too (conventions 9.13 and 4.11).

### Hot water and the cylinder

RdSAP asks for the cylinder size, from the label if possible, insulation type and thickness, immersion type and cylinder thermostat (specification section 10.5; Table 31 items 7-10 to 7-15). Photograph the label, record where the cylinder is (clause 7.3.2 j)), and where there is none, as with a combination boiler, say so. MER-DEA-007 covers the cylinder conventions.

## Section 3: What RdSAP Needs and What the PAS Record Needs

**In short:** Put service history, equipment locations and installation year in your report, because the RdSAP heating items do not ask for them. Identify gas or oil boilers and heat pumps from the PCDB whenever possible; a data plate photograph lets anyone check your match. If a heating source or its emitters cannot be inspected and no documentary evidence is available, the assessment cannot be completed until access is provided, community heating excepted. Ask your scheme which heating documents it accepts.

### Two records from one visit

The RdSAP data file goes to the designer with your report (clause 7.4.1), but it is not the whole record. None of its heating items (specification Table 31 items 7-1 to 7-20) asks for service history, equipment locations or an installation year. Age appears in narrower forms, such as the extra items for gas boilers from 1998 or later not taken from the database (Table 31 item 7-4) and pump age (convention 4.18). The parts of clause 7.3.2 j) that RdSAP does not hold belong in your report.

### Database first

For gas or oil boilers, micro-CHP and heat pumps, the Product Characteristics Database (PCDB) "is to be used whenever possible" (specification section 10.1), and the brand name, model and model qualifier, or index number, are recorded whenever possible (Table 31 item 7-3). You identify the product at data entry; it is not left for the designer. A boiler or heat pump not in the PCDB is entered as a default (convention 4.07). The conventions (Appendix 5) and specification (Table 31 item 12-3) give ncm-pcdb.org.uk only as the address of the pages for Appendix Q technologies; neither states one for the PCDB itself. A data plate photograph lets anyone check your match.

### Where the conventions settle it

These are the rules the old edition got wrong or left out:
- Inaccessible equipment. If a heating source or its emitters cannot be inspected and no documentary evidence is made available, the assessment cannot be completed until access is provided, community heating excepted (convention 4.03).
- Design flow temperature. For condensing boilers and heat pumps it is recorded as unknown unless documentary evidence shows the system was designed and commissioned as a low temperature one (convention 4.01b). If unknown, the calculation takes radiators as over 45°C and underfloor heating as 35°C or below (specification Table 29).
- Radiators and underfloor from one heat source. Unless the source is a heat pump, it is entered as main heating 1 and 2 under the two main systems rules; a heat pump has one entry, with radiators entered when they serve 50% or more of the floor area (convention 4.01a).
- Storage heaters. Convention 4.09 names four types: old large-volume, fan-assisted, integrated storage/direct acting and high heat retention. It names no "standard" type. A heater is high heat retention only if its brand name and model is in the database; otherwise it is entered as fan-assisted (convention 4.09).
- Coil size. It is not an RdSAP data item. Table 29 assumes a cylinder heat exchanger area of 1.0 m² where some database heat pumps require one, unless the data comes from the PCDB (specification Table 29).

### Documentary evidence

Some heating entries depend on documents: design flow temperature (convention 4.01b), MCS installation of a heat pump, entered "yes" only if documentary evidence is available (Table 31 item 7-4), and LPG at mains gas prices (convention 4.11). In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, acceptable evidence includes certificates, warranties, guarantees or any documents verifying that work has been carried out, and the assessor must be confident, and able to demonstrate, that they relate to the property (convention 9.02). Scotland has its own, shorter text under the same convention: it lists certificates, warranties and guarantees, without the wider "any documents verifying that work has been carried out". Neither names heating documents, so ask your scheme what it accepts. A sensible habit is to keep copies with the PAS report.

## Section 4: Heat Pumps, Distressed Replacement and the Medium-Term Plan

**In short:** Heat pump readiness is a design question, and nothing in PAS 2035 asks you to judge it. After a distressed replacement, the whole-dwelling assessment shall be completed as soon as possible, during or after the retrofit process; record the new appliance as installed, with its commissioning paperwork as service history. For a recent heat pump installation, ask the occupant for the design paperwork; whether it satisfies convention 4.01b is for your accreditation scheme.

### Heat pumps: what the standards put where

The PAS 2035:2023 assessment clause says nothing specific about heat pumps or low temperature heating. Its guidance says existing homes should make use of low-carbon heating, considered when the current heating needs replacement and/or after fabric upgrades (clause 5.5.4). Its requirements fall on design and installation: where the design includes LZC technologies or renewable energy systems covered by MCS, it shall specify compliance with the relevant MCS standards (clause 8.2.22), and those systems are installed to the MCS standards instead of PAS 2030 (clause 9.1).

MCS maintains the heat pump design standard, MIS 3005-D "The Heat Pump Standard (Design)", Issue 3.0, 5 December 2025, which applies to MCS contractors designing heat pump systems (MIS 3005-D, section 1). MCS also publishes MIS 3005-D:2025 "Heat Pump: Design Standard", Issue 2.0, 5 December 2025, which its foreword makes mandatory for contractors certified to MCS: 2025. Neither document says it replaces the other, so ask your scheme which applies. The clauses below are from Issue 3.0, which places these with the contractor:
- a heat load calculation for the building, using the standard's design temperatures and otherwise complying with BS EN 12831-1:2017 (clause 5.5.1 a))
- the cylinder heat exchanger specification, following the heat pump and/or cylinder manufacturer's recommendations (clause 5.6.5)
- written information for the customer before installation starts, including room heat losses, the design emitter temperature "based on the worst performing room" and the design flow temperature (clause 5.9.2)

Heat pump readiness is therefore a design question, and nothing in PAS 2035 asks the assessor to judge it. Your record is the designer's starting point; anything more comes back through clause 8.2.5.

For the RdSAP entry of an installed heat pump, see MER-DEA-005. Issue 3.0 requires the customer to be given the design flow temperature in writing before installation starts (clause 5.9.2), so for a recent installation ask the occupant for the design paperwork; an older one may follow an earlier issue, which this course has not read. Whether a document satisfies convention 4.01b is for your accreditation scheme.

### Distressed replacement of a heating appliance

PAS 2035:2023 defines distressed replacement as "fast-paced installation" of a measure; a note adds that this is typically the replacement of heating and hot water in winter, without which the occupant's living standards are severely impacted (clause 3.6). The standard applies to it through a specific process (clause 1):
- The Retrofit Coordinator is involved as soon as possible, and intended outcomes may be agreed later than the outset (clauses 6.1.5 and 6.2.1).
- The whole-dwelling assessment to identify further measures shall be completed as soon as possible, during or after the retrofit process (clause 7.2.1).
- The replacement shall meet clauses 8.2.1, 8.2.2, 8.2.7, 8.2.9, 8.2.19 and 8.2.20 (clause 8.3.1).
- Once a coordinator is employed and the assessment done, all of clause 8 applies; if no further measures follow, the minimum output is the improvement options evaluation and the medium-term improvement plan (clause 8.3.2).

So the assessment may come after the new appliance is in. Record it as installed, with its commissioning paperwork as service history. A sensible habit is to note what it replaced and when, if anyone can tell you.

### The medium-term improvement plan

A medium-term improvement plan shall be prepared for every dwelling (clause 8.1.5) and shall identify, among other things, "an appropriate order" for installing the measures (clause 8.1.7). Your record of the heating's age and condition helps place the heating in that order.

### BS 40104:2025

BS 40104:2025, "Retrofit assessment for domestic dwellings. Code of practice", was published by BSI on 24 September 2025 and is listed as current. BSI says it "builds on the foundation set by PAS 2035 and PAS 2030" (BSI catalogue entry). PAS 2035:2023 does not refer to it, and this course does not teach its content. Ask your scheme or TrustMark whether, and from when, it applies to your work.

## Key Takeaways
- The PAS 2035:2023 heating requirement is clause 7.3.2 j): services and controls, age and service history where available, locations, areas served, and whether the systems work. Annex A is qualifications.
- Clause 7.3.2 does not ask for serial numbers, efficiency, radiator or pipe sizes, or coil size. Extra information the designer needs is requested through the coordinator (clauses 7.4.4 and 8.2.5).
- The report goes with the RdSAP data file and photographs of recorded features (clause 7.4.1). RdSAP holds no service history, locations or installation year, so those go in the report.
- Fitted but not working is still entered in RdSAP; inaccessible heating with no documentary evidence stops the assessment (convention 4.03).
- Secondary heating needs a fixed emitter (convention 5.01). Storage heaters are high heat retention only if in the PCDB (convention 4.09). Design flow temperature is unknown without documentary evidence (convention 4.01b).
- Heat load, emitter and flow temperatures and the cylinder heat exchanger are MCS design work (MIS 3005-D Issue 3.0), and PAS 2035 requires LZC and renewable systems covered by MCS to meet MCS standards (clauses 8.2.22 and 9.1).
- After a distressed replacement, the whole-dwelling assessment is completed as soon as possible, during or after the work (clause 7.2.1).

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** The Retrofit Designer finds your assessment does not give enough heating information to prepare a design. What does PAS 2035:2023 say happens?

A) Nothing; the assessment is final once the RdSAP data file is lodged
B) The designer identifies the missing information and the coordinator arranges for you to collate it into an updated assessment report
C) The installer collects it at the pre-installation building inspection
D) The coordinator fills the gaps from the RdSAP data file

**Correct answer: B.** Clauses 7.4.4 and 8.2.5: the designer identifies the missing information and the coordinator arranges for the Retrofit Assessor to collate it and include it in an updated assessment report.

---

**Q2.** An air source heat pump is installed. The occupant says the installer was MCS certified but has no paperwork. What is entered for MCS installation in RdSAP?

A) Yes, on the occupant's word
B) Yes, if the heat pump is in the PCDB
C) Yes, because all heat pumps are treated as MCS installations
D) No, because the entry is yes only if documentary evidence is available

**Correct answer: D.** Specification Table 31 item 7-4 records MCS installation as yes or no, and yes only if documentary evidence is available.

---

**Q3.** A gas boiler serves radiators in every room. The occupant also uses a plug-in oil-filled radiator in one bedroom. How is it treated in RdSAP?

A) It is not entered as secondary heating, because secondary heating requires a fixed emitter
B) It is entered as secondary heating, because the occupant uses it
C) It is entered as main heating system 2
D) It causes the dwelling to be calculated for portable electric heaters

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 5.01 includes secondary heating where a fixed emitter is present; there is no main system 2 when main system 1 heats all habitable rooms (specification section 10.2), and the calculation is done for portable heaters where no heating system is present (section 10.1), which a gas boiler rules out.

---

**Q4.** A boiler failed in January and was replaced within days, before any Retrofit Coordinator was involved. What does PAS 2035:2023 require of the whole-dwelling assessment?

A) None is needed, because distressed replacement is outside PAS 2035
B) It had to be completed before the boiler was replaced
C) It shall be completed as soon as possible, during or after the retrofit process
D) It is needed only if the client asks for further measures

**Correct answer: C.** Clause 7.2.1 requires the assessment to be completed as soon as possible during or after the retrofit process, and clause 1 says the PAS applies to distressed replacement of heating appliances.

---

**Q5.** Which of these does clause 7.3.2 j) of PAS 2035:2023 require the assessment to include?

A) The boiler's serial number
B) Confirmation that the heating systems are working correctly, or otherwise
C) The height and width of each radiator
D) The coil size of the hot water cylinder

**Correct answer: B.** Clause 7.3.2 j) requires confirmation that the systems are working correctly (or otherwise); it does not mention serial numbers, radiator dimensions or coil size.
