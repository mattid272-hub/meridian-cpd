# UK Energy Policy 2026: What Assessors Need to Know
**Course ID:** MER-ALL-001 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026
*© Meridian CPD 2026. All rights reserved.*

---

## Learning Objectives
- State the minimum energy efficiency standard in law today for private rented property in England and Wales, with the regulation and dates.
- Explain what the A to G band on an RdSAP 10 EPC is based on, and why the grid carbon factor does not move it.
- Describe the 2026 decisions on EPC C for privately and socially rented homes, and separate what is decided from what is legislated.
- Give the current status and timing of EPC reform and of the Future Homes Standard.
- Identify which funding schemes are open, closing or closed as at October 2026, and where PAS 2035:2023 is a scheme requirement.

---

## Introduction

This course is a status report. For each policy it gives the rule, its status (regulations in force, made but not yet in force, a decision not yet legislated, or a proposal), the nation and the dates. It is correct to 12 October 2026; several items will move again, so check the source before quoting a date.

Most of what follows applies to England and Wales, because the EPC and private rented sector regulations are England and Wales instruments. The Future Homes Standard regulations, the social housing decision, Warm Homes: Local Grant and the Home Upgrade Grant apply to England only. ECO is a Great Britain scheme. Scotland appears only for its EPC reform date. Northern Ireland is not covered: no source for it was read.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition said the Future Homes Standard was already operative (its regulations come into force on 24 March 2027), said the lower grid carbon factor and updated fuel prices move RdSAP 10 bands (the band is cost-based and uses the SAP 2012 prices), and gave EPC C for private rentals as an intention with 2028 and 2030 dates (the January 2026 decision is a single date, 1 October 2030). It also left out EPC reform, listed the closed Home Upgrade Grant as current, called PAS 2035:2023 the third edition, taught "risk pathways" and gave the Retrofit Coordinator a ventilation decision that belongs to the Retrofit Designer.

Related courses: MER-ALL-005 (Future Homes Standard), MER-ALL-008 (Renters' Rights Act 2025), MER-ALL-011 (non-domestic minimum standards) and MER-ALL-015 (EPC reform).

## Section 1: What is law today

**In short:** Tell clients that band E is the only legal minimum for letting private rented property in England and Wales until new regulations are made and in force; below it a landlord must not let unless an exception or exemption in the Regulations applies. If you see a band move between RdSAP 9.94 and RdSAP 10 certificates with no work done, the carbon factor is not the reason: the band is cost-based, on the SAP 2012 prices.

### The minimum standard for letting is band E

The Energy Efficiency (Private Rented Property) (England and Wales) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/962) set the minimum level of energy efficiency for domestic and non-domestic private rented property as "an energy performance indicator of band E" (regulation 22(b)). A property is sub-standard if its valid EPC shows a lower rating, and an EPC counts as valid only if it was entered on the register no more than 10 years before the date it is relied on and no other EPC has since been entered for the property (regulation 22(a) and (c)).

A landlord of a sub-standard property must not let it unless one of the exceptions or exemptions in the Regulations applies (regulations 23(1) and 27(1)). The dates are:
- Domestic: new tenancies, extensions and renewals from 1 April 2018, and continuing to let from 1 April 2020 (regulation 23(2)).
- Non-domestic: new tenancies, extensions and renewals from 1 April 2018, and continuing to let from 1 April 2023 (regulation 27(2)).

For domestic lettings, the government's landlord guidance (last updated 5 May 2026) says a landlord is "not currently required to spend more than £3,500 (including VAT)", the maximum fine is £5,000 per property in total, and local authorities enforce the regulations (GOV.UK, Domestic private rented property: minimum energy efficiency standard, landlord guidance). Until new regulations are made and in force, band E is the only legal minimum.

### RdSAP 10 and what the band measures

RdSAP 10 was launched on 15 June 2025, replacing RdSAP 9.94 (Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, Warm Homes: Local Grant policy guidance, section 7.5; ecmk Technical Bulletin, June 2025). The current documents are the RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0 of 7 June 2026 and Conventions v12.2, applicable from 6 August 2026 (Conventions v12.2, cover page).

The specification describes the asset rating as "a numerical indicator of the cost of energy" (RdSAP10 Specification, Introduction), and section 19.1 sets out the SAP rating, "referred to as EER". The government's EPC guide calls the A to G rating "related to running costs" (A guide to energy performance certificates for the marketing, sale and let of dwellings, December 2017, chapter 1). Two points settle most client questions:
- The fuel prices for the SAP rating are "the same as in Table 12 of the SAP 2012 specification", reproduced as Table 32 (specification section 19.1). Mains gas is 3.48 p/kWh and standard tariff electricity 13.19 p/kWh (Table 32).
- The CO2 and primary energy factors are those of SAP 10.2, with grid electricity at 0.136 kg CO2e per kWh. They feed the carbon-based Environmental Impact Rating and the primary energy figure (specification section 19.2 and Table 32).

The running costs and savings printed on the EPC use a different price set, Table 191 of the Product Characteristics Data File, but any SAP rating must use the SAP 2012 prices (specification section 20).

So when a band changes between an RdSAP 9.94 certificate and an RdSAP 10 one with no work done, the carbon factor is not the reason: it does not enter the band. The specification's list of significant changes includes measuring every window, revised brick and stone wall U-values, a new age band M with all U-value tables updated, a revised room in roof and more ventilation options (specification, Introduction). It gives no rule of thumb for which dwellings go up or down.

## Section 2: Rented homes, the decisions of 2026

**In short:** Keep decisions and law apart when you advise. Privately rented homes in England and Wales are to reach EPC C by 1 October 2030, and social rented homes in England C on one metric by 1 April 2030 and a second by 1 April 2039, subject to the caps, exemptions and other conditions below. On the sources read neither is shown in force, so for private lettings the law is still band E.

### Private rented homes: EPC C by 1 October 2030

The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) consulted from 7 February to 2 May 2025 on privately rented homes in England and Wales, and responded on 21 January 2026 (GOV.UK, Improving the energy performance of privately rented homes: 2025 update). It decided:
- A single compliance date. All tenancies in scope must comply by 1 October 2030, and there will not be an earlier date for new tenancies (government response, Summary of final policy).
- A dual-metric standard: fabric performance first, then either a heating system or a smart readiness standard, at the landlord's discretion (consultation outcome).
- A £10,000 cost cap, described as the maximum landlords will be required to invest over a 10-year period. Once the regulations apply, spending on relevant measures installed since 1 October 2025 can be counted towards it (Executive summary; Summary of final policy).
- For properties valued below £100,000, a lower limit of £10,000 or 10% of the property's value, whichever is lower (Executive summary; Summary of final policy).
- A home that scores C or higher on the current Energy Efficiency Rating (EER), shown on an existing or new EPC before 1 October 2029, will be considered compliant until that EPC expires or is replaced (Summary of final policy).
- A maximum fine of £30,000 per property per breach (Executive summary).

This is a government decision, not law. The Government will seek new powers by Act of Parliament, then lay a statutory instrument "with the aim of it coming into force in 2027" (Executive summary). No source read for this course shows that Act or instrument in existence at 12 October 2026. The accurate line to a landlord is: the law is band E, C by 1 October 2030 has been decided, and on the sources read the legislation is still to come.

Landlords whose homes do not score EER C or higher before 1 October 2029 will be required, before taking action, to commission a new EPC showing the new metrics, and another after the works, with the cost counting towards the cap (Summary of final policy; Executive summary).

### Social rented homes in England

The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG) responded on 1 April 2026. Its decision is that social rented homes in England must reach EPC C or higher against any one EPC metric by 1 April 2030, and against a second metric by 1 April 2039, in both cases using reformed EPCs and unless a valid exemption applies, with a £10,000 spend exemption per property for each metric. A home that reaches C on the current EER before 1 April 2030, with a valid EPC showing it, is considered compliant for that EPC's validity period. The standard is to be implemented through Criterion D (thermal comfort) of the revised Decent Homes Standard and regulated by the Regulator of Social Housing (Improving the energy efficiency of socially rented homes in England: government response). The part of the response read for this course does not name the legal instrument that will give it effect, so this course cannot say it is in force.

### Non-domestic rented buildings

DESNZ's interim response of 18 June 2026, for England and Wales, proposes EPC B from 2031, where cost effective, for private rented buildings over 1,000 square metres, and drops the interim EPC C milestone for 2027. It takes effect only after secondary legislation (MEES in the non-domestic private rented sector: interim response). Band E remains the law; MER-ALL-011 has the detail.

## Section 3: EPC reform and the Future Homes Standard

**In short:** Treat EPC reform in England and Wales as policy, not law: the launch has moved to the second half of 2027 with no launch date given, so ask your scheme for the timetable. Scotland's 30 April 2028 date for new-style EPCs is in draft regulations whose approval this course could not confirm. The Future Homes Standard regulations are made but not yet in force: they apply in England from 24 March 2027, subject to the exceptions below.

### EPC reform in England and Wales

MHCLG, supported by DESNZ, published a partial government response on reforms to the Energy Performance of Buildings regime on 21 January 2026. For domestic EPCs it set out these policies:
- Four new headline metrics: "energy cost, fabric performance, heating system and smart readiness" (partial response, section 2.5).
- The legacy EER is retained "to enable comparison with current EPCs" (section 2.5).
- The ten-year validity period is kept for reformed EPCs (section 3.8).
- A DESNZ consultation published alongside it explores producing new-style domestic EPCs with the Home Energy Model and banding the four metrics (section 4, paragraph 73). Its outcome is not in the sources read.

These are policy positions, not law. The response says that, subject to parliamentary approvals, the Government intends to bring forward regulations in 2026 to make the changes (paragraph 1), and no such regulations appear in the sources read. On timing, the January response said the Government was working to deliver new EPCs from October 2026 (section 4, paragraph 74). That date has gone. An update of 9 March 2026 says the Government has "decided to move the launch of the reforms to the second half of 2027" (GOV.UK, Reforms to the Energy Performance of Buildings regime). The page's update history shows nothing later and no launch date; ask your scheme for the timetable.

### Scotland

Scotland has its own regime. The Scottish Government's update of 24 August 2026 says regulations introducing new-style EPC certificates, validity periods and rating systems "will now be amended to come into force on 30 April 2028" (gov.scot, Energy Performance Certificate reform: updates). These are the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2025, otherwise due to come fully into force on 31 October 2026. The change is in draft amending regulations laid on 24 August 2026 under the affirmative procedure, which cannot take effect unless the Scottish Parliament approves them (Clerk's note of 29 September 2026, Economy, Tourism and Energy Committee). This course could not confirm that Parliament has approved them.

### The Future Homes Standard

The Building Regulations etc. (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/335) make "the legislative changes needed to implement the Future Homes and Buildings Standards" (legislation.gov.uk). The position is:
- Status: regulations made but not yet in force. They come into force on 24 March 2027, except that regulations 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 come into force on 24 September 2027 for higher-risk building work and work to existing higher-risk buildings (SI 2026/335, regulation 1(2) and (3)).
- Nation: they apply to buildings and building work in England (regulation 1(4)).
- Transition: where a building notice, initial notice or full plans application was given before 24 March 2027 and the work is commenced before 24 March 2028, the amendments made by regulations 3 and 4 do not apply. This does not extend to higher-risk building work (SI 2026/335, regulation 5(1), (3) and (4)).
- Content: new homes with low-carbon heating, high levels of energy efficiency and, in most cases, solar panels (consultation response, paragraph 1.5). The parts of the response read give no percentage carbon saving, so the old edition's "75 to 80%" is not confirmed for the standard as made.
- Methodology: the government's SAP guidance, updated 8 June 2026, says the Home Energy Model launch has been delayed and SAP 10.3 "is currently available as the sole approved methodology" (GOV.UK, Standard Assessment Procedure).

## Section 4: The Warm Homes Plan and the funding schemes

**In short:** Check a scheme's status before pointing a client to it: as at 12 October 2026, Home Upgrade Grant Phase 2 and the Great British Insulation Scheme are closed, ECO4 runs until 31 December 2026, and Warm Homes: Local Grant is delivered in England to March 2028 under guidance requiring the latest PAS 2035, currently PAS 2035:2023. The Warm Homes Plan text read mentions neither PAS 2035 nor TrustMark, so ask TrustMark or your scheme before saying those arrangements are changing.

### What the plan is

DESNZ published the Warm Homes Plan on 21 January 2026 and corrected it on 18 March 2026 (GOV.UK, Warm Homes Plan). It is a policy document, not legislation. Its headline is "£15 billion of public investment to upgrade up to 5 million homes" (Ministerial foreword), and the Executive summary aims to "help upgrade 5 million homes by 2030".

The plan sets out a Warm Homes Agency: Salix will close, and its functions, some DESNZ functions and relevant Ofgem roles will move into a single executive agency (chapter 4). It says consumer protection must be reformed, but the text read for this course does not mention PAS 2035 or TrustMark. Ask TrustMark or your scheme before telling anyone those arrangements are changing.

### Schemes: open, closing and closed

As at 12 October 2026:
- Home Upgrade Grant Phase 2 is closed to applications. Its funding covered delivery in England "from April 2023 until March 2025" (GOV.UK, Home Upgrade Grant: Phase 2).
- The Great British Insulation Scheme "ended on 31 March 2026" and is closed to new installations (Ofgem, Great British Insulation Scheme).
- ECO4 "will run until 31 December 2026, following a nine-month extension" (Ofgem, Energy Company Obligation), and the plan says ECO "will be coming to an end" (Warm Homes Plan, chapter 2).
- Warm Homes: Local Grant is in its delivery period. Local authorities in England deliver it from April 2025 to March 2028, as the single successor to HUG and LAD, for homes with an EER in bands D to G (Warm Homes: Local Grant policy guidance, cover dated June 2026 and published on GOV.UK on 2 July 2026, sections 1, 1.1, 1.2 and 5.1).
- The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is open to people in England and Wales (GOV.UK, Apply for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme), and the plan says it will continue to give grants of £7,500 towards hydronic heat pumps (Warm Homes Plan, chapter 3).

From 2027/28 the Government intends to integrate the Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund and the Local Grant into a single low-income capital scheme (Warm Homes Plan, chapter 3).

### PAS 2035 in funded work

The Local Grant guidance says all upgrades "must be compliant with the latest PAS 2035 guidance (currently PAS 2035:2023)" (section 7.1), and installers must be TrustMark registered and certified under the latest PAS 2030 (section 1.1, Table 1). Those are that scheme's requirements. Three facts about the standard correct the old edition:
- PAS 2035:2023 is the second edition, published in September 2023. It supersedes PAS 2035:2019+A1:2022, which BSI said would be withdrawn on 30 March 2025 (PAS 2035:2023, publication history and Foreword).
- Its Foreword says the risk assessment process "has been simplified". "Risk pathways" are not a term to teach from the 2023 text.
- The whole-dwelling assessment, including the ventilation assessment, is reported to the Retrofit Designer (clause 7.4.1). Where the package of specified and existing measures falls into category B or C under Approved Document F, or the assessment shows condensation or mould, the Retrofit Designer confirms the adequacy of existing ventilation and, if necessary, specifies an upgrade (clause 8.2.13).

## Key Takeaways
- Band E is the only legal minimum for letting private rented property in England and Wales (SI 2015/962, regulation 22). Continuing to let below E has been prohibited, subject to the exceptions and exemptions in the Regulations, since 1 April 2020 (domestic) and 1 April 2023 (non-domestic).
- The Government decided on 21 January 2026 that privately rented homes in England and Wales must meet a new C standard by 1 October 2030, all tenancies at once, with a £10,000 cap. It still needs an Act and a statutory instrument.
- The Government's social housing decision of 1 April 2026 is that social rented homes in England reach C on one metric by 1 April 2030 and on a second by 1 April 2039, using reformed EPCs. The part of the response read names no legal instrument.
- The RdSAP 10 band is cost-based on the SAP 2012 fuel prices in Table 32 (specification section 19.1). The SAP 10.2 carbon factors feed the Environmental Impact Rating, not the band.
- EPC reform is policy, not yet law: four headline metrics are to replace the single cost metric, and the launch has moved to the second half of 2027.
- The Future Homes Standard regulations (SI 2026/335) are made, apply in England, and come into force on 24 March 2027. The Home Energy Model had not launched by 8 June 2026, when the SAP guidance named SAP 10.3 as the sole approved methodology.
- HUG Phase 2 and GBIS are closed, and ECO4 runs to 31 December 2026. Warm Homes: Local Grant runs to March 2028 and its guidance requires PAS 2035:2023.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** As at 12 October 2026, what is the legal minimum EPC band for letting a domestic private rented property in England and Wales?

A) Band C, because the response of 21 January 2026 changed the law
B) Band E
C) Band D
D) There is no minimum until new regulations are made

**Correct answer: B.** The minimum level is "an energy performance indicator of band E" (SI 2015/962, regulation 22(b)); the January 2026 response is a decision that still needs an Act and a statutory instrument.

---

**Q2.** Under the Government's decision of 21 January 2026, when must privately rented homes in England and Wales meet the new standard?

A) New tenancies from 2028 and all tenancies from 2030
B) New tenancies from 2025 and all tenancies from 2028
C) All tenancies by 1 April 2030
D) All tenancies by 1 October 2030, with no earlier date for new tenancies

**Correct answer: D.** The response sets 1 October 2030 for all tenancies in scope, with no earlier date for new tenancies (PRS government response, Summary of final policy); 1 April 2030 is the social rented date.

---

**Q3.** What is the A to G band on an RdSAP 10 EPC based on?

A) Energy cost, calculated with Table 32 fuel prices, which are the same as the SAP 2012 prices
B) CO2 emissions, calculated with the SAP 10.2 grid electricity factor
C) The occupant's actual fuel bills and tariff
D) Primary energy use per square metre

**Correct answer: A.** The asset rating is an indicator of the cost of energy and the SAP rating uses Table 32 prices, the same as SAP 2012 (RdSAP10 Specification, Introduction and section 19.1); the carbon factors feed the Environmental Impact Rating (section 19.2).

---

**Q4.** When do the Future Homes Standard regulations (SI 2026/335) come into force for building work that is not higher-risk building work?

A) They have been in force since 2025
B) 21 January 2026
C) 24 March 2027
D) 24 March 2028

**Correct answer: C.** The regulations come into force on 24 March 2027 (SI 2026/335, regulation 1(2)); 24 March 2028 is the date before which work must commence to benefit from the transitional provision (regulation 5(1)).

---

**Q5.** What is the published position on the launch of reformed domestic EPCs in England and Wales as at 12 October 2026?

A) They launched in October 2026 as planned
B) The launch has been moved to the second half of 2027
C) They launch on 1 October 2030 alongside the private rented standard
D) The reform has been withdrawn

**Correct answer: B.** The update of 9 March 2026 says the Government "decided to move the launch of the reforms to the second half of 2027", replacing the October 2026 target (GOV.UK, Reforms to the Energy Performance of Buildings regime).
