# Scotland's EPC Reform: HEM, Three Ratings and Five Years, from 30 April 2028
**Course ID:** MER-SCO-002 | **CPD Hours:** 1 | **Published:** October 2026

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- State what the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2025 do, which parts are in force, and how the draft 2026 Amendment Regulations move full commencement to 30 April 2028.
- Name the three domestic ratings and list what the new certificate and property report must contain.
- Apply the five-year validity rule and the transitional rules for certificates issued under the 2008 Regulations.
- Explain where the Home Energy Model sits in the Regulations and in Scottish Building Standards, and what is not yet published.
- Describe the new accreditation scheme, its audit rules, the lodgement fee change and the training the Scottish Government has said assessors will receive.

---

## Introduction

From 30 April 2028, if the draft amending instrument now before the Scottish Parliament is approved and made, the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2008 are revoked and the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2025 (SSI 2025/417) take their place. A domestic EPC will then carry three ratings, one of them today's Energy Efficiency Rating under a new name, in place of today's two, last five years instead of ten, be calculated by a methodology the Scottish Ministers approve (the stated policy is the Home Energy Model), and come with a property report instead of a recommendations report.

As made, the 2025 Regulations were to come fully into force on 31 October 2026 (regulation 1(2)). The draft Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Amendment Regulations 2026, laid on 24 August 2026, move that to 30 April 2028 but keep the lodgement fee rise on 31 October 2026.

### Where this course applies

This course applies in Scotland. The 2025 Regulations extend to Scotland only (regulation 1(4)). The UK Government's reform for England and Wales runs to its own timetable and is not covered here. MER-SCO-001 covers the current Scottish system.

### Status of the law and of this course

This course is correct to 28 October 2026. On that date the 2026 Amendment Regulations were still a draft: the lead committee had recommended approval, but legislation.gov.uk still described the instrument as "a draft item of legislation" that "has not yet been made", and this course found no record of a vote of the whole Parliament or a made SSI number. The draft is designed to come into force on 30 October 2026, and until an amending instrument is made and in force, regulation 1(2) of the 2025 Regulations still reads 31 October 2026. After 30 October 2026, re-check:
- legislation.gov.uk for the made instrument, its SSI number and date, and the 2025 Regulations as amended (regulation 1(2) should then read 30 April 2028);
- that the register fee of £6.00 for a dwelling applies from 31 October 2026;
- the gov.scot "Energy Performance Certificate Reform: updates" page, and your Approved Organisation's notices, for anything later than 24 August 2026.

Every statement is cited to the document it comes from; where a page would not open, or a point appears only in an accreditation body's article, the text says so.

## Section 1: The Instruments and the Dates

### The 2025 Regulations

The draft 2025 Regulations were laid on 10 October 2025 under the affirmative procedure (Local Government, Housing and Planning (LGHP) Committee report, 27 November 2025, paragraph 1). The committee recommended that the draft be approved (paragraph 25), the Parliament agreed, and the Regulations were made on 16 December 2025 as SSI 2025/417 (heading and preamble). Regulation 1(3) brought regulations 1, 2 (interpretation), 11 (approved organisations), 12 (accreditation schemes) and 36 (transitional provisions for approved organisations) into force on 1 January 2026. Regulation 37 and Schedule 2 revoke the 2008 Regulations (SSI 2008/309) and the seven instruments that amended them, subject to the saving and transitional provisions in regulation 38.

### The draft 2026 Amendment Regulations

On 17 March 2026 the Scottish Government said the new-style certificates "will not come into force in October 2026 as planned", citing the UK Government's "delay to the introduction of EPC reforms until the second half of 2027" and the dependence on "a new methodology (the Home Energy Model) and assessor training being finalised across the UK" (Energy Performance Certificate Reform: updates, page dated 17 March 2026). The draft amending instrument was laid on 24 August 2026 (Economy, Tourism and Energy (ETE) Committee paper ETE/S7/26/6/6, 29 September 2026). Its text (legislation.gov.uk) has three regulations:
- regulation 1 brings it into force on 30 October 2026, except regulation 2, which comes into force on 31 October 2026 (regulation 1(2) and (3));
- regulation 2 substitutes new regulations 10 and 10A in the 2008 Regulations so that the register fee still rises on 31 October 2026, as regulation 15 of the 2025 Regulations had planned (regulation 2(2); ETE report, paragraph 7);
- regulation 3 substitutes "30 April 2028" for "31 October 2026" in regulation 1(2) of the 2025 Regulations and moves the transitional dates in regulations 36 and 38(4) to match (regulation 3(2) to (4)).

The ETE Committee recorded that "There is no other change to the substance or content of the 2025 Regulations" (report of 7 October 2026, paragraph 8), and the draft text contains nothing else.

The Scottish Government chose April 2028 "to provide additional contingency within its implementation timetable" (updates, 24 August 2026). The Minister for Europe, External Affairs and Energy told the committee that the UK Government had written on 17 July saying its reform "will be delivered in England and Wales during the second half of 2027" (letter of 24 August 2026). The ETE Committee agreed the motion recommending approval on 29 September 2026 (paragraphs 1, 20 and 21).

### The fee

The register keeper, the Scottish Ministers, may now charge £2.60 to lodge data for a dwelling and £12.10 for other buildings (2008 Regulations, regulation 10A). From 31 October 2026 those become £6.00 and £15.50 (draft regulation 2(2), new regulation 10A(1); updates, 24 August 2026), the same sums that regulation 15 of the 2025 Regulations sets from commencement. The rise depends on regulation 2 of the draft being in force on that date. The Minister told the committee that fees will be reviewed in two years (ETE report, paragraph 18).

### What it means today

The Scottish Government says "the majority of the Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2008 and current EPC system will now remain in place until Spring 2028" (updates, 24 August 2026). The new accreditation scheme is open, but the EPC you lodge today is a 2008 Regulations certificate, and the RdSAP 10 Conventions you work to (v12.2, applicable from 6 August 2026) still carry Scotland-only conventions such as 3.05a and 9.02.

## Section 2: Three Ratings, a Certificate and a Property Report

### The definitions

Regulation 2 defines the three domestic ratings, each assessed and expressed under an approved methodology. The heat retention rating rates "both the current and potential energy performance of the fabric" of the building. The heating system rating rates the current and potential emissions likely from "the normal operation of the main heating system" and that system's efficiency. The energy cost rating rates the "estimated current and potential annual cost of energy used by the normal use" of the building. These are the domestic "energy performance indicators"; the non-domestic set is an energy performance rating, an energy use rating and a direct emissions rating (regulation 2).

### The certificate and the property report

Regulation 8(1) and (2) require a domestic EPC to show the three ratings, a statement that the property report contains information on improvement measures, the address, the report reference number, the date of issue, the type of heating system, and whether the building is in a heat network zone on that date. It must be issued by an energy assessor accredited for that category of building, or produced from register data after a disclosure request (regulation 8(3)), and must not identify any living individual other than the issuer or the issuer's employer (regulation 8(5); for the property report, apart from the address, regulation 9(4)). Advertisements in commercial media for sale or let must state the energy performance indicators (regulation 7).

The property report replaces the recommendations report. Every property report must contain an indicator of primary energy use; information about measures that could improve the indicators or reduce emissions, in particular those from heating, cooling and hot water; the estimated costs of those measures under an approved methodology; and the names of the issuing assessor and their accreditation scheme (regulation 9(2)). A domestic property report must also contain an indicator of the estimated emissions, "both direct and indirectly", from normal use; an indicator of the estimated energy used for heating, hot water, cooling, lighting and ventilation; what alternative main heating system could be installed; and that system's estimated emissions and estimated costs of installation and operation (regulation 9(3)).

### What each rating is meant to show

The LGHP Committee's summary of the policy describes the Energy Cost Rating as an "A-G rating (based on £/m2/yr)" and notes "This is same as the existing Energy Efficiency Rating" (paragraph 5).

The Scottish Government's October 2025 response gives more detail, as "initial high level intentions" to be "further refined during 2025-26" before the methodology is adopted (EPC reform consultations: SG response, 10 October 2025, section 5). It was written before the delay, so it still refers to commencement in autumn 2026.
- Heat Retention Rating: calculated from "the energy (kWh/m2) needed to maintain the temperature of the dwelling's living area at 21°C", shown as a current and potential rating (5.1).
- Heat Retention Rating bands, set "for the purpose of undertaking further modelling": A 0 to 30, B 31 to 90, C 91 to 159, D 160 to 242, E 243 to 292, F 293 to 309 and G 310 and above, in kWh/m²/year (5.2). The bands finally adopted "may see some further adjustment", keeping Band C "broadly equivalent to existing EPC Band C". The stock model research (10 October 2025, Table 4) gives the same "proposed thresholds" and puts 53.68% of modelled homes in bands A to C.
- Heating System Rating: one A to G rating, from "a single calculation of the emissions and the efficiency of the heating system": A heat pump; B heat network, direct electric, hydrogen; C bioenergy and bioenergy/electric hybrid; D fossil fuel/electric hybrid; E gas and LPG; F oil; G coal and peat (5.4). The page says "any fossil fuel heating system with direct greenhouse gas emissions could not be rated C or better", and that "a gas boiler could achieve a rating within E-G depending on the boiler efficiency". Every home gets a potential rating of C or above, and the rating "remains for information-only" (5.4).
- Energy Cost Rating: £/m²/year, following the current Energy Efficiency Rating "as closely as possible" but assuming "a Scottish average climate" instead of a UK average, with "standard energy prices based on what costs are expected to be from 2026" (5.5).

Today's Environmental Impact Rating is "based on carbon dioxide equivalent emissions" (Business and Regulatory Impact Assessment (BRIA), 24 August 2026, section 1). Regulation 8 puts no equivalent rating on the certificate; emissions are assessed in the heating system rating (regulation 2) and reported in the domestic property report's emissions indicator (regulation 9(3)(a)). None of these bands or calculation bases is settled until the Scottish Ministers approve a methodology under regulation 10.

## Section 3: Five Years, and the Transition

### Validity

Under regulation 8(4) an EPC and its property report cease to be valid on the earlier of "the expiry of a period of 5 years from the date on which the energy performance certificate was issued" and the issue of another EPC for the building. That replaces the 10 years in regulation 6(2)(b) of the 2008 Regulations. The Cabinet Secretary told the LGHP Committee she was "open minded to the idea that the validity period could move back to ten years once a heat retention rating C has been achieved, but we will keep that under review" (paragraph 21). Nothing in the Regulations provides for that.

### Certificates issued under the 2008 Regulations

Regulation 38 lets a valid 2008 Regulations EPC and its recommendations report stand in for the new documents during a "relevant period", and lets an advertisement state the single 2008 indicator (regulation 38(1), (3) and (4)). It does not apply to a newly constructed property, meaning one built but not yet occupied (38(2); regulation 2). As made, the sale and let periods ran from 31 October 2026 to 31 October 2027 at the latest. Under the draft amendment each begins on 30 April 2028 (draft regulation 3(4); updates, 24 August 2026):
- for a sale, it ends on the earlier of the first sale after that date and 30 April 2029;
- for a let other than a short-term let, on the earlier of the first letting after that date and 30 April 2029;
- for a short-term let, on 30 April 2029.

The Scottish Government reads this to mean that "from 30 April 2028 onwards, when a building owner commissions a new EPC, assessors will only be able to issue new-style EPCs"; that a still-valid old-style EPC "can be downloaded from the EPC Register and used in a property transaction"; that the year is cut short for properties sold or let within it "in the event that they are sold or let again"; and that "From 30 April 2029, only EPCs issued under the 2025 Regulations, as amended, will be allowed" (updates, 24 August 2026).

### Reading it in practice

From 30 April 2028 a ten-year EPC lodged in 2026 serves a sale until the first sale and a let until the first letting, and never after 30 April 2029. A second sale within the year, or a second letting, needs a new-style EPC and property report on any reading. Because regulation 38(4) sets separate periods for sales and for lets, whether a sale followed by a first letting within the year can still use the old EPC is a point to confirm with your Approved Organisation. A sensible habit is to explain this to clients when you issue a 2008 Regulations EPC from now on.

New buildings are outside the transition. From commencement the developer must give the owner a copy of a valid new-style EPC and property report within seven days of the date a completion certificate is submitted to a verifier (regulation 6).

## Section 4: Methodology, Building Standards and Governance

### The methodology

The Regulations do not name the Home Energy Model. Regulation 10 requires the Scottish Ministers to approve and publish a methodology for the energy performance indicators and the property report's emissions and cost figures, with standardised ways of expressing them, and allows different methodologies for different classes of building. The policy note to SSI 2025/417 refers to "The new Home Energy Model which will replace the Standard Assessment Procedure for domestic EPCs" (footnote 3). The impact assessment says SAP "needs to be replaced by the new UK Home Energy Model" and a cloud-based calculation service, Energy Calculation as a Service, "(expected in 2027)" (BRIA, section 1). The Scottish Government says the shared infrastructure "will be tested and available for the EPC assessor industry to use for training during 2027, in order to be ready for go-live, in Scotland, in April 2028" (updates, 24 August 2026). By 28 October 2026 this course had found no published approval of a methodology under regulation 10.

On the risk of relying on the UK Government to progress HEM, raised by the Existing Homes Alliance (paragraph 18), the Cabinet Secretary told the LGHP Committee that "a Memorandum of Understanding has been agreed between the two governments" to give early sight of any delay (paragraph 20). This course did not find the Memorandum published.

### Building Standards

Regulation 34 amends the Building (Scotland) Regulations 2004. In schedule 5 it re-points paragraph 6.1 (energy demand), and the definition of "nearly zero-energy building", from regulation 7 of the 2008 Regulations to regulation 10 of the 2025 Regulations, and it omits paragraph 6.9 (energy performance certificates). It names no methodology: that depends on what Ministers approve under regulation 10.

The Scottish Government's Stage 2 consultation on a Scottish equivalent to the Passivhaus standard (open 24 July to 16 October 2026) says it now aims "to publish guidance and approved methodologies under standard 6.1 in Autumn 2027". For the design and construction stage compliance statements introduced by the Building (Procedure) (Scotland) Amendment Regulations 2024, which had "a coming into force date of 31 March 2028", it "will amend the implementation date in regulations to Autumn 2029". Its domestic tool is a "Home Energy Model: Scottish Wrapper Consultation Tool", an interim methodology "to be developed further before being adopted as approved methodologies". Elmhurst Energy reported on 28 August 2026 that "SAP may continue to be used for Building Standards compliance while HEM is used for EPC production"; this course found no Scottish Government statement of that. Re-check when the consultation response is published.

### Approved Organisations and the accreditation scheme

Regulation 11 lets the Scottish Ministers approve organisations to operate accreditation schemes, once satisfied that certificates and property reports will be prepared "in a consistent, accurate and independent manner" by fit and proper, suitably qualified people. Regulation 12 requires every energy assessor to belong to an approved scheme, and each scheme to provide for, among other things, a code of conduct, indemnity arrangements, complaints handling and audit of "a specified proportion" of certificates and reports, "including where practicable by means of an on-site inspection" (regulation 12(3)).

As made, regulation 36 keeps approvals of approved organisations under regulation 8 of the 2008 Regulations in effect until 31 October 2026. The draft substitutes "29 April 2028" for "30 October 2026" (the end of the regulation 36 period) and "30 April 2028" for "31 October 2026" (draft regulation 3(3)), so those approvals would continue until 30 April 2028. The scheme's operating requirements were first published on 6 March 2026, and on 17 March the Scottish Government said "The new Scottish EPC Accreditation Scheme has launched" (updates, 17 March 2026). It "remains open for new applicants who will be appointed in advance of the remainder of the 2025 Regulations, as amended, coming into force on 30 April 2028"; existing and potential Approved Organisations face "evaluation and (re)appointment" when the new Regulations come into force (updates, 24 August 2026).

### The operating requirements

The Scottish EPC Accreditation Scheme operating requirements (6 March 2026, updated 14 October 2026) set the audit rules:
- Approved Organisations must check "a minimum of 2%" of members' EPCs for accuracy (4.1.3), and Ministers may raise that minimum (4.1.8);
- each active assessor's output must be checked at least every six months, or every twelve where they produce five or fewer EPCs in six months (4.1.5), and a new member's output within the first month (4.1.6);
- technical failures include the wrong heating system, building typology, address or UPRN, or potential improvements, a change of band, or a rating change outside a +/- 5% tolerance (4.5.2), with the tolerance table in 4.5 set for the new ratings, for example Heat Retention Rating +/- 5% (kWh/m²/yr);
- below-standard EPCs must be replaced within six weeks (4.6.1), and the building owner must be told (4.6.4);
- each Approved Organisation is audited every 12 months by or for the Scottish Ministers (6.3.1).

Every assessor application needs a Level 1 Disclosure, and an existing one is accepted only if produced within three years (2.1.2). Approved Organisations must have procedures for "continuing competence and professional development" (2.2.3), and any training they provide must reflect the 2025-26 review of the National Occupational Standards (2.2.6). For domestic work the minimum cover for each claim is professional indemnity of £50,000, public liability of £1,000,000 for existing dwellings (not applicable to new build) and 10 years' run-off cover (3.1.3). Each Approved Organisation must have a published code of conduct that all members follow (3.2.2). The Scottish Ministers set no minimum CPD time but "reserve the right to introduce this (if deemed necessary) at a later date" (3.4.2); Approved Organisations should ensure members submit an annual CPD record (3.4.4) and must sample-check "at least 5% of their membership per annum" (3.4.5).

### On-site audits

"While onsite audits are not mandated for AOs to undertake", the Scottish Ministers "remain committed to introducing an independent, onsite audit regime" within 12 months of the remainder of the 2025 Regulations coming into force (4.3.2). The Scottish Government is "establishing a new onsite audit and inspection function within the Scottish Government, to be funded through EPC lodgement fees" (updates, 24 August 2026). Elmhurst Energy reported on 28 August 2026 that the Scottish Government aims to launch the regime in the second half of 2027; that date is not in any Scottish Government document this course opened.

### Training, as published

The Scottish Government has committed to ensuring "that all assessors receive training on the new regulations and changes to the certificates and rating systems", to "training on, and testing of, the new calculation software", and to completing the "2nd stage joint review" of the National Occupational Standards for EPC assessors (updates, 24 August 2026). This course found no published syllabus, duration or assessment. Until one appears, a sensible plan is to keep your own CPD record on the reform and watch for your Approved Organisation's (re)appointment and training notices.

## Key Takeaways
- SSI 2025/417 was made on 16 December 2025; regulations 1, 2, 11, 12 and 36 have been in force since 1 January 2026.
- The draft 2026 Amendment Regulations move full commencement to 30 April 2028 with no other change of substance; on 28 October 2026 legislation.gov.uk still showed them as not yet made, so re-check after 30 October 2026.
- The register fee is due to rise on 31 October 2026 from £2.60 to £6.00 for a dwelling and from £12.10 to £15.50 for other buildings, through regulation 2 of the draft.
- A domestic EPC under the 2025 Regulations carries a heat retention rating, a heating system rating and an energy cost rating (regulation 8(1)); the energy cost rating continues the Energy Efficiency Rating under a new name, though the October 2025 response says it will assume a Scottish average climate.
- New certificates last five years (regulation 8(4)); under the draft, from 30 April 2028 a 2008 Regulations EPC serves only until the first sale or letting, and never after 30 April 2029.
- Regulation 10 requires Ministers to approve a methodology; the Home Energy Model is the stated policy, and no approval had been published by 28 October 2026.
- Building Standards run to their own timetable: guidance and approved methodologies under standard 6.1 are due in autumn 2027, and the Passivhaus-equivalent compliance statements are to move to autumn 2029 (Stage 2 consultation).
- The operating requirements set a 2% minimum audit and a six-week replacement period; the Scottish Ministers remain committed to an independent on-site regime within 12 months of commencement.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Under the draft Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Amendment Regulations 2026, on what date do the remaining provisions of the 2025 Regulations come into force?

A) 31 October 2026
B) The second half of 2027
C) 30 April 2028
D) 30 April 2029

**Correct answer: C.** Regulation 3(2) of the draft substitutes 30 April 2028 for 31 October 2026 in regulation 1(2) of the 2025 Regulations (ETE report, paragraph 5); A is the original date and the date of the fee rise, B is the UK Government's planning date for England and Wales, and D is the end of the sale and let transition.

---

**Q2.** Which three ratings must a domestic EPC include under regulation 8(1) of the 2025 Regulations?

A) Heat retention, heating system and energy cost ratings
B) Energy efficiency, environmental impact and primary energy ratings
C) Energy performance, energy use and direct emissions ratings
D) Fabric, services and cost ratings

**Correct answer: A.** Regulation 8(1)(a) names the heat retention rating, heating system rating and energy cost rating for a domestic property; C is the non-domestic set in regulation 8(1)(b), and B and D appear nowhere in regulation 8.

---

**Q3.** Assume the draft 2026 Amendment Regulations are made as laid. A ten-year EPC lodged under the 2008 Regulations in June 2026 is used to sell a house in August 2028, and the buyer puts it back on the market in February 2029. What does the second sale need?

A) Nothing: the 2026 EPC is valid until 2036
B) Nothing: the 2026 EPC can be used until 30 April 2029
C) A new recommendations report only
D) A new-style EPC and property report under the 2025 Regulations

**Correct answer: D.** The relevant period for a sale ends on the first sale after commencement (regulation 38(4)(a)(i)), here August 2028, so the old EPC cannot serve the February 2029 sale; the Scottish Government's update says the year is cut short for properties "sold or let again".

---

**Q4.** What does regulation 10 of the 2025 Regulations require about the calculation method?

A) That the Home Energy Model be used for all domestic EPCs
B) That the Scottish Ministers approve and publish a methodology and standardised ways of expressing the results
C) That RdSAP 10 continue until the UK Government replaces it
D) That the Building (Scotland) Regulations 2004 set the method

**Correct answer: B.** Regulation 10(1) places the duty on the Scottish Ministers to approve and publish the methodology and the standardised ways of expressing its results; the Home Energy Model is the stated policy (policy note, footnote 3), not a term of the Regulations.

---

**Q5.** Under the Scottish EPC Accreditation Scheme operating requirements, what is the minimum proportion of members' EPCs an Approved Organisation must check for accuracy?

A) 2%
B) 1%
C) 5%
D) 10%

**Correct answer: A.** Paragraph 4.1.3 requires "a minimum of 2%" of the EPCs produced by members to be checked, and under 4.1.8 the Scottish Ministers may raise that minimum; 5% is the annual CPD record sample of membership (3.4.5) and the rating tolerance (4.5.2), not the EPC audit minimum.
