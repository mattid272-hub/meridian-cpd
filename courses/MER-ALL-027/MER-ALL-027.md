## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- Recognise what a commissioned mechanical ventilation system should leave behind: the checks, the commissioning sheet and the information pack, as set out in Approved Document F (ADF) Section 4 and Appendix C.
- Explain what a valid air flow measurement needs (device, hood, calibration, set-up and averaging) and why a fan that runs is not a fan that has been measured.
- Read a commissioning sheet against the design flows, using only the comparison rule and the device accuracy that ADF actually gives.
- Set out what changes when the 2026 editions of ADF take effect on 4 March 2027 in Wales and 24 March 2027 in England, including who may commission and when flows in an existing home should be tested.
- Say what an assessor can observe, what they cannot measure, what to record, and who to refer a finding to.

## Introduction

Most ventilation you meet on an assessment was put in by someone else, at some point in the past, to a standard you cannot see. A fan whirs when the light goes on. A ceiling grille sits in the bathroom with a unit humming in the loft. A householder hands you a folder with a sheet of numbers in it, or tells you nobody ever gave them anything. This course is about that folder, those numbers and the gap between "it runs" and "it does what it was designed to do".

Part 1 of the Ventilation Series covered how homes breathe and the rates and areas ADF sets. Part 2 covered the systems as you see them on site and how they fail. Part 3 covers commissioning, flow measurement and the 2026 editions of ADF. It is the most regulatory part of the series, so it is built around what you see, what you can and cannot check, what you record and when you refer.

This course does not train you to commission, measure or adjust a ventilation system. Your job on site is to record what is there, what the paperwork shows and what you could not confirm, and to point the householder or project team to the right person.

Three other Meridian courses are not repeated here: **MER-RA-008** (PAS 2035:2023 clause 7.3.2 f), the ADF Table 3.1 and Diagram 3.1 trigger, clause 8.2.13 and Annex C); **MER-DEA-003** (how each system is entered in RdSAP); and **MER-ALL-018** (relative humidity, instruments and the condensation "twins").

## Section 1: What Commissioning Is, and What a Finished Job Leaves Behind

**In short:** Treat commissioning as the step that turns an installed system into a checked one. Under ADF, a mechanical system should be inspected, run, measured, balanced where continuous, and have its controls tested, with results written on a commissioning sheet. The householder should get a copy and an information pack. On site you should ask for the paperwork, record what it shows and what is missing, and leave judging the figures to the installer or designer.

### Two duties, two notices

Two regulations sit behind the paperwork. ADF reproduces both before its Section 4.

| | Regulation 44: commissioning | Regulation 42: air flow rate testing |
|---|---|---|
| The person doing the work gives building control | A notice that commissioning followed the ADF procedure | The flow test results |
| Deadline | "not more than 30 days after completion of the work", where no other completion notice applies (44(4)(b)) | "not later than five days after the final test" (42(3)(b)) |
| Applies to | Mechanical ventilation work, except where "testing and adjustment is not possible" (44(1)) | England: "the creation of a new dwelling by building work". Wales: also existing dwellings where mechanical ventilation is provided or extended, except Schedule 4 work (42(1)(b), (1A)) |

Regulation 44 as reproduced in ADF 2021; regulation 42 from legislation.gov.uk, England and Wales texts, up to date to 5 October 2026.

ADF 2021 (England) states both plainly. Mechanical ventilation systems "must be commissioned to provide adequate ventilation", with a notice to building control (4.1). "Air flow rates for mechanical ventilation in new dwellings must be measured" (4.2). Both paragraphs apply to intermittent extract as well as continuous systems (note to 4.2).

The 2021 Intention box also says that for "a new or an existing dwelling", regulation 44 is met by following paragraphs 4.1 to 4.12. That range includes 4.9, which says the air flow rate of all mechanical ventilation fans should be tested. So the 2021 commissioning guidance already expects fans put into an existing English home to be tested; what it limits to new dwellings is the separate duty to measure and notify flows under regulation 42. Section 4 shows how the 2026 edition reshapes this.

### The commissioning checklist, as the assessor sees it

ADF 2021 (England) 4.4 says systems should be inspected and tested to paragraphs 4.5 to 4.12. Turned into what a finished job should show:

| Part of the job | What ADF 2021 (England) expects | What you might see on site |
|---|---|---|
| Background ventilators (4.5) | Minimum area from Table 1.7 identified; actual area and location recorded; controls open and close; correctly mounted | Trickle vents with a marked area; one that will not open; one painted shut |
| Intermittent fans (4.6) | The installer checks fans run, respond to controls, and run-on timers are "set to at least 15 minutes" | Fan stops the moment the light goes off; pull-cord broken; isolator off |
| Ducts and terminals (4.7) | Installed to the design; good condition; air flows the right way; no abnormal noise on start-up, background or high rate | Crushed flexible duct in the loft; a grille blowing instead of sucking; a rattling unit |
| Continuous systems (4.8) | Balanced to design flows "at each room terminal" | Terminals with set positions; a unit with speed settings |
| Flow testing (4.9, 4.10) | All fans tested, including cooker hoods, with a calibrated device and hood; flows recorded in l/s | A sheet with measured and design columns filled in |
| Controls (4.11) | Each control function tested where practical; controls labelled | Labelled boost switch; humidistat on the unit; no label anywhere |
| Information (4.13 to 4.17, 4.20) | Design flows, maintenance needs and a copy of the commissioning sheet given to the owner | A folder with the sheet and manuals, or nothing |

### The commissioning sheet

Appendix C of ADF 2021 (England) is a model checklist and sheet (C1): Part 1, system details, address, installer and commissioning engineer; Part 2a, the installer's checklist; Part 2b, a visual or pre-commissioning inspection, including whether background ventilators were left open and whether packaging was removed; and Part 3, the measuring equipment, with its date of last UKAS calibration, and the measured and design flow for each fan or terminal.

C3 says the relevant parts should be signed by a person "competent to install the system and responsible for installing and commissioning the system". A copy goes to building control (C4) and to the owner (4.14). The information pack (4.16) should cover, where relevant, the maker's contact details, that background ventilators "should be left open", controls, cleaning and filters, duct access, sensors and the design flow rates.

### Where PAS 2035 puts the paperwork

On a PAS 2035 project, the retrofit design specifies commissioning for building services measures, including ventilation (10.2.1), and where several need it, that they are "finally commissioned at the same time, not separately" (10.2.2). The design names the records to be supplied through the Retrofit Coordinator (10.3.1), who "shall retain copies" of them (11.1.3). Annex C adds that new mechanical ventilation systems "shall be tested" and, where adjustable, have their flows commissioned (C.3.13).

So on a pre-works visit, an existing system's commissioning sheet is useful evidence for the designer. On a post-works visit, it is something the Retrofit Coordinator should already hold, and its absence is worth recording and raising with them.

### What you do with it

Ask for the commissioning sheet and information pack. Record whether they exist, the date, the system type on Part 1, whether Part 3 has measured flows, and whether the device and calibration date are filled in. Photograph the sheet if the householder agrees. Do not mark or sign it, or comment on whether the figures are acceptable.

Callouts where ADF 2021 Section 4 checks: (1) bedroom trickle vent, "area marked, opens and closes (4.5)"; (2) bathroom intermittent fan, "runs, responds to switch, run-on at least 15 min (4.6)"; (3) cooker hood, "tested, highest setting recorded (4.9, App C Part 3)"; (4) loft duct to a roof terminal, "good condition, right direction, no abnormal noise (4.7)"; (5) continuous extract unit in the loft with ceiling terminals, "balanced at each terminal (4.8)"; (6) labelled boost switch, "controls tested and labelled (4.11)"; (7) a folder on the worktop, "commissioning sheet and information pack (4.14, 4.16)". ## Section 2: Measuring Air Flow: the Device, the Set-up and the Reading

**In short:** A flow reading counts under ADF only if taken with a calibrated device and proprietary hood, accurate to ±5% and calibrated at a UKAS-accredited centre within 12 months, with background ventilators open and doors and windows closed. The 2026 editions add a powered flow hood for continuous fans, a vane anemometer only for intermittent fans, readings in both modes and a 10-second average. Without that kit, you should record what you saw, not a flow figure.

### Why a hood, and why calibrated

A design rate is a volume of air per second through the terminal. To measure it, the whole flow has to be captured and passed through the instrument, which is what the hood does. ADF 2021 (England) 4.10 asks for "a calibrated air flow device with a proprietary hood" at each room terminal, and sets three conditions (4.10(c)): a proprietary hood attachment; an accuracy of ± 5%; and calibration within the last 12 months at a UKAS-accredited calibration centre.

The set-up matters as much as the device (4.10(b)): all intended background ventilators and air transfer devices open, and all internal and external doors and windows closed. Each flow is recorded in litres per second on Part 3 of the sheet (4.10(d)). Where a fan's configuration prevents measurement "in exceptional circumstances", evidence should be given to building control that the flow can be found from the maker's guidance (note to 4.10). That note is for the installer and building control, not a route for an assessor to estimate flows.

### What the 2026 editions add

ADF 2026 (England) 4.24 keeps the hood, the ±5% and the 12-month UKAS calibration, and adds:
- **Design flows from Table 1.1 or Table 1.2**, as appropriate (4.24(a)), so intermittent fans are measured against intermittent rates. (ADF 2021 4.10(a) names Table 1.2 only.)
- **Both modes:** background ventilation and high rate (4.24(b)).
- **Device by fan type.** Continuous extract and supply fans with a "powered flow hood air flow measurement device"; "a vane anemometer may be used as an alternative" for intermittent extract fans only (4.24(d)).
- **Calibration as a pair, across the range.** Device and hood calibrated together, across flows "from at least 4l/s" to the system's maximum design flow (4.24(e)(iii)).
- **Hood centred** over the fan or terminal (4.24(f)).
- **Averaged reading:** "the average value obtained over a minimum 10-second period" (4.24(g)).

ADF 2026 (Wales) has the same device rules at 4.16. ADF 2022 (Wales) 4.10 is close to the 2021 English wording: hood, Table 1.2, ±5% and UKAS calibration within 12 months.

The 2026 English sheet splits Part 3 into 3a (intermittent fans), 3b (decentralised continuous) and 3c (centralised continuous), each asking for the calibration certificate number as well as the date. For cooker hoods, only the highest setting needs recording.

The design flows the sheet is measured against are those in Part 1 of this series: Table 1.1 (intermittent: bathroom 15 l/s, for example), Table 1.2 (continuous high rate: kitchen 13, utility 8, bathroom 8, sanitary accommodation 6 l/s) and Table 1.3 (whole dwelling: 31 l/s for three bedrooms), with the whole dwelling rate also at least 0.3 l/s per m² of internal floor area (ADF 2021 1.24; ADF 2026 1.20). These values are the same in both English editions.

### MVHR: the balance check

ADF 2026 (England) 4.21 adds a system check for MVHR: total supply and extract "should be within 10% when operating in background ventilation mode". ADF 2026 (Wales) has the same at 4.13. No such percentage was found in ADF 2021 (England) Section 4, which asks for balancing at each terminal (4.8). Neither 2026 edition says which total the 10% is taken from.

### What ±5% means, and what it does not

The ±5% is the device's accuracy: how far its reading may sit from the true flow. It is not a tolerance on the design flow. Appendix C paragraph C2 (2021 and 2026, England) compares measured with design: if each measured rate is "equal to or greater than the design value", the system meets the design standard; if any is lower, "an adjustment should be made to correct the system" and all flows remeasured. Neither edition says to add or subtract the device accuracy before comparing.

## Section 3: Reading a Commissioning Sheet: Worked Numbers

**In short:** When a householder gives you a commissioning sheet, you can check whether it is complete and whether each measured flow is at least its design flow, because that is the comparison ADF Appendix C sets. Do not use the ±5% device accuracy as a margin, because ADF does not. For MVHR, the 2026 editions add a 10% balance in background mode. Record what the sheet shows and what is missing, and leave judgement and adjustment to the installer or designer.

### The sample house

The house is invented to show the arithmetic. It is not a design and the flows are not product data. Two-storey semi-detached, three bedrooms, internal floor area 90 m² (both floors added). Wet rooms: kitchen, bathroom, en suite and a separate WC. A new centralised continuous extract system has a ceiling terminal in each wet room and the fan unit in the loft.

**Step 1: whole dwelling rate.** Table 1.3, three bedrooms: 31 l/s. Floor area rule: 0.3 × 90 = 27 l/s. The higher governs: **31 l/s** total continuous extract. (At 120 m², 0.3 × 120 = 36 l/s would govern instead.)

**Step 2: high rates.** Table 1.2: kitchen 13, bathroom 8, en suite 8 (it is a bathroom), WC 6. Total: 13 + 8 + 8 + 6 = **35 l/s**.

**Step 3: design continuous rates.** The split is the designer's choice. Here the design sheet gives kitchen 10, bathroom 8, en suite 7, WC 6: 10 + 8 + 7 + 6 = 31 l/s, equal to the whole dwelling rate.

**Step 4: the commissioning sheet.**

| Terminal | High rate: design | High rate: measured | Continuous: design | Continuous: measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | 13 | 14.1 | 10 | 10.4 |
| Bathroom | 8 | 8.3 | 8 | 8.1 |
| En suite | 8 | 7.6 | 7 | 6.9 |
| WC | 6 | 6.4 | 6 | 6.2 |
| **Total** | **35** | **36.4** | **31** | **31.6** |

Totals: 14.1 + 8.3 + 7.6 + 6.4 = 36.4 l/s; 10.4 + 8.1 + 6.9 + 6.2 = 31.6 l/s.

**Step 5: apply C2.**
- Kitchen: 14.1 ≥ 13 and 10.4 ≥ 10. Meets.
- Bathroom: 8.3 ≥ 8 and 8.1 ≥ 8. Meets.
- WC: 6.4 ≥ 6 and 6.2 ≥ 6. Meets.
- En suite: 7.6 < 8 and 6.9 < 7. **Below design in both modes.**

The continuous total, 31.6 l/s, is above the 31 l/s whole dwelling rate. That does not rescue the en suite. C2 works fan by fan: if "any measured value is lower than the design value", an adjustment should be made and all flows remeasured. A sheet like this, with no adjustment and remeasurement recorded, is incomplete on its own terms.

**Step 6: what the ±5% does and does not change.**
- En suite high rate, 7.6 l/s: 5% of 7.6 = 0.38, so the true flow could lie between 7.22 and 7.98 l/s. Even the top of the range is below 8.
- Bathroom high rate, 8.3 l/s: 5% of 8.3 = 0.415, so the true flow could lie between 7.885 and 8.715 l/s, a band that straddles 8.

This is not a margin anyone may use. C2 compares the recorded reading with the design value, so on the sheet the bathroom meets design and the en suite does not. The arithmetic shows why ADF asks for a calibrated device and a set method: a reading close to design tells you little unless it was taken properly.

**Step 7: an MVHR balance check.** Suppose the house had MVHR and the sheet gives these totals in background mode.
- Case A: supply 33.0, extract 35.5 l/s. Difference 2.5. As a share of extract: 2.5 ÷ 35.5 = 7.0%. Of supply: 2.5 ÷ 33.0 = 7.6%. Within 10% either way.
- Case B: supply 31.0, extract 35.0 l/s. Difference 4.0. Of extract: 4.0 ÷ 35.0 = 11.4%. Of supply: 4.0 ÷ 31.0 = 12.9%. Outside 10% either way.

ADF 2026 4.21 does not say which total to divide by. In both cases the answer is the same either way; where it is not, the reading is for the installer or designer. A sheet completed under ADF 2021 (England) would not be expected to show this check.

**Step 8: what you write down.** Commissioning sheet seen, dated, system named as centralised continuous extract; device and calibration date filled in, or not; flows recorded for every terminal in both modes, or which are missing; en suite flows recorded below the design flows on the sheet, with no remeasurement shown; referred to the Retrofit Coordinator or installer. You would not write "system fails", "en suite fan faulty" or "system compliant". You are reporting what the document says.

The en suite bars carry an amber marker, "below design (C2)". A thin band around the bathroom high rate bar spans 7.885 to 8.715 l/s, labelled "±5% device accuracy: not a margin". Beneath, a totals strip: "Continuous 31.6 l/s against 31 l/s required (Table 1.3, 3 bed; 0.3 × 90 = 27)". ## Section 4: The 2027 Changes: Before and After

**In short:** Expect three practical changes when the 2026 editions take effect. Anyone commissioning anything other than a single intermittent fan in an existing home should belong to a body that checks their competence. In existing homes, flows should be tested for new centralised systems, fans on ducts over 2 m and fans with more than one 90° offset. Measurement is tightened. Work notified before the dates and started in time stays under the old edition.

### The timeline

|---|---|---|

**England.** The 2026 changes do not apply to work on a building where the relevant notice was given before 24 March 2027, provided work on that building starts before 24 March 2028 (Circular Letter 01/2026, para 16). Protection applies "by reference to each individual building" (para 23). The circular "does not give advice on the technical requirements" (para 7).

**Wales.** The 2026 Welsh edition does not apply where a building notice, full plans application or initial notice was submitted before 4 March 2027, provided the work for each building starts before 4 March 2028 (ADF 2026 (Wales), cover).

Until March 2028 you may see ventilation put in under either edition in the same street. A sheet's date and format (Part 3 in one table, or split into 3a, 3b and 3c) is a clue to the edition followed, not proof.

### Wales: already measuring in existing homes

- ADF 2022 (Wales) 4.2: flows must be measured "in new dwellings and notifiable ventilation work in existing dwellings", with a notice to building control. Where an appropriate competent person scheme does notifiable work in an existing dwelling, that notice is not required (note to 4.2).
- Regulation 42 in Wales applies to existing dwellings where mechanical ventilation is provided or extended, except Schedule 4 work.
- ADF 2026 (Wales) keeps the measuring duty (4.3), adds the same competence split as England (4.2(a), (b)), and lists the same three existing-dwelling testing cases as England's 4.26 at 4.18. Its device rules (4.16) and MVHR balance (4.13) match England's.
- No training or membership wording was found in the ADF 2022 (Wales) Section 4 text read.

### Who may commission: what the text says, and does not say

ADF 2026 (England) 4.10 and ADF 2026 (Wales) 4.2 open the same way: those commissioning and testing "must be competent to do so". For a single intermittent extract fan in an existing dwelling, the person "should have had appropriate training". For all other mechanical ventilation, they should be a member of an organisation that independently verifies their competence, for example a relevant competent person scheme.

Neither text names a qualification, course or card, and "appropriate training" is not defined in the paragraphs read. If a householder asks, quote the wording. Do not say a particular qualification is needed, or that it is not.

The gov.uk list of competent person schemes (updated 17 September 2026) names four for "mechanical ventilation and air-conditioning systems" in dwellings: BESCA, Blue Flame Certification, Certsure and NAPIT. The page does not say which nations it covers; a Welsh list was not opened.

### What this means for the assessor

None of these paragraphs gives an assessor a new duty. They change what a well-documented job looks like.
- After the 2026 dates, a new centralised system, or a new fan on a long or bent duct, in an existing home should come with measured flows. A fan on a short straight run through the wall may not have been measured, and the English text does not expect it to be.
- A replacement single fan may have been commissioned by someone with "appropriate training" rather than a scheme member, consistent with 4.10(a).
- A continuous system or MVHR commissioned under the 2026 editions should show readings in both modes, a calibration certificate number and, for MVHR, totals that let the 10% balance be checked.

## Section 5: The Assessor's Toolkit and Its Limits

**In short:** You can see what is fitted, where, whether it runs, how it is switched, whether vents are open and whether paperwork exists. You cannot produce an ADF-grade flow figure without the calibrated kit and method, and PAS 2035 asks that any functional testing follows the national standard and BSRIA procedures. Record observations as observations, and do not let a run check, a tissue or one humidity reading stand in for a measurement.

### What you can observe without instruments

This list draws on what ADF 2021 Section 4 and Appendix C ask installers to check, and on PAS 2035:2023 7.3.2 f) (covered in MER-RA-008). It is a recording aid, not a published checklist.

| What | What to note |
|---|---|
| Fans | Location, type, make and model where visible, or why not |
| Running | Starts; stops when the switch or light goes off; whether a run-on continues, and roughly how long |
| Controls and isolators | Light switch, pull-cord, humidistat, boost; labels; isolator on or off |
| Terminals and ducts | Present in each wet room; clean or blocked; air moving in or out; visible ducts crushed, kinked, disconnected or unlagged in a cold loft |
| Background ventilators and undercuts | Number, room, marked area, open or closed, painted or taped over; door undercuts present |
| Combustion appliances | Any open-flued appliance, and which rooms have fans |
| Paperwork | Commissioning sheet, information pack, installer, date |
| Condensation and mould | Location and severity (PAS 2035 7.3.2 d); MER-ALL-018) |

### Functional testing under PAS 2035

PAS 2035:2023 7.3.2 f) 3) says "any functional testing shall follow the process provided in the relevant devolved nation" ventilation standard, using BSRIA procedures for fan measurement. Flows measured below the minimum "shall be reported as part of the assessment". NOTE 5 says extract fans should be checked to BSRIA guidance so they are "providing adequate air movement". The Commentary on C.2 describes the assessor's job as identifying existing systems and confirming "that they are working correctly".

So the clause says how testing should be done if it is done. In England and Wales the devolved standard is ADF, so a reading reported as a flow should meet the device rules in Section 2. If you do not hold a calibrated device with a hood, record what you observed, state that flows were not measured, and say why.

### What does not count as a measurement

- **A fan that runs.** It proves the motor turns, not the flow at the terminal, which depends on the duct, shutter, grille and setting.
- **A tissue or paper held to a grille.** No source opened for this course describes this as a measurement. If you note it, record it as an observation and do not turn it into a flow.
- **A vane anemometer without a hood, or an uncalibrated device.** Outside ADF's conditions, it is not an ADF reading; and the 2026 editions allow a vane anemometer only for intermittent fans.
- **A single humidity reading.** The government damp and mould guidance, which applies to England, names "consistently high relative humidity, as assessed using a moisture meter or environmental monitors" as a root-cause check (updated 1 April 2026). One reading on one visit is a snapshot, not "consistently". MER-ALL-018 covers instruments.

### What you can and cannot conclude

From observation you can say a fan is present, runs, has no run-on, is isolated, or sits in a room with no trickle vent. You cannot say it achieves or misses its design rate. From a sheet you can say what was recorded, when, with what device, and whether a recorded flow is below its recorded design value. You cannot say the system is or is not adequate; under PAS 2035 that is the Retrofit Designer's call (8.2.13, MER-RA-008).

The same guidance names "damaged, blocked, absent or switched off mechanical ventilation" as a root cause to check, and says it is "totally unreasonable to blame damp and mould in the home on 'lifestyle choices'". Record what is switched off or blocked as a fact about the equipment. An isolator turned off often has a reason the householder can tell you, such as noise or running cost.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Fan not working properly" | "Fan runs; no run-on observed after switch-off" |
| "Extract inadequate" | "Flow not measured: no calibrated device on site" |
| "System fails ADF" | "Sheet shows en suite high rate 7.6 l/s against design 8 l/s; no remeasurement recorded" |
| "Trickle vents closed by tenant" | "Background ventilators in bedrooms 1 and 2 found closed" |

## Section 6: When to Refer, and to Whom

**In short:** Send each finding to the person who can act on it. On a PAS 2035 project, ventilation findings go to the Retrofit Coordinator and through them to the Retrofit Designer, who decides adequacy. Missing or incomplete commissioning records go to the Retrofit Coordinator or the installer. An open-flued appliance in a home with extract fans should be referred promptly to an engineer competent for that appliance. A landlord's duties are covered in MER-ALL-017.

### The people in the route

- **Retrofit Coordinator.** Holds test certificates and commissioning records (PAS 2035:2023 11.1.3) and passes the assessment to the designer. First stop on a PAS 2035 project.
- **Retrofit Designer.** Confirms whether existing ventilation is adequate where 8.2.13 is triggered and, if needed, specifies an upgrade (MER-RA-008).
- **Installer.** Should have commissioned the system and handed over the sheet. Annex C says the retrofit design "shall specify" installation by operatives registered with a relevant competent persons scheme and approved by the system's manufacturer or supplier (C.3.12).
- **Competent person scheme or building control.** The scheme, where the installer was a member; building control, where it signed off the work and holds the notices. Scheme complaint routes were not opened for this course.
- **Gas engineer.** ADF 0.17 warns that fans "might cause combustion gases to spill from open-flued appliances", even with appliance and fan in different rooms. ADF 0.18 says Approved Document J should be followed when installing and testing, and "Combustion appliances must operate safely whether or not fans are running." By law, "only Gas Safe registered engineers should carry out work on gas appliances" (Rhondda Cynon Taf Council, Gas Safe registration guidance). For oil or solid fuel, refer to an engineer competent for that fuel; no source on those routes was opened.

### Which finding goes where

| Finding | Record | Refer to |
|---|---|---|
| No commissioning sheet for a mechanical system on a PAS 2035 project | "No commissioning record seen" | Retrofit Coordinator |
| A measured flow below its design value, no remeasurement; or no device details | The figures, or what is missing | Retrofit Coordinator; installer |
| Fan with no run-on, isolated, or shutter stuck | What you saw | Householder to installer; Retrofit Coordinator on a project |
| Condensation or mould alongside any of the above | Location, severity (7.3.2 d)) | Retrofit Coordinator, for the designer (8.2.13) |
| Open-flued appliance in a home with extract fans | Appliance and fan locations | Householder promptly to a Gas Safe registered engineer, or a competent engineer for other fuels |
| Rented home with ventilation not working and damp or mould | What you saw | Landlord duties: MER-ALL-017 |

### What not to do

- Do not adjust a fan, terminal, speed setting or isolator. ADF 2026 4.20 asks commissioners to lock adjustable terminals so they are not altered.
- Do not tell a householder their system is compliant, safe or adequate, or that it is not.
- Do not quote a flow you did not measure with the kit in Section 2.
- Do not recommend a product, installer or scheme member. Point to the route.
- Do not give a view on whether someone was qualified to commission. Quote ADF 2026 4.10 or ADF 2026 (Wales) 4.2.

The route does not change in March 2027; what you should expect to find does. A new centralised system, or a fan on a long or bent duct, in an existing home under the 2026 editions should have measured flows on its sheet. If not, that is a finding to record and refer, not a breach for you to call.

## Key Takeaways

- Commissioning under ADF means inspected, run, measured, balanced where continuous, controls tested, and written up on a commissioning sheet; the owner should get a copy and an information pack (ADF 2021 4.4 to 4.17).
- A flow reading counts under ADF only with a calibrated device and proprietary hood, ±5% accuracy and UKAS calibration within 12 months, with background ventilators open and doors and windows closed. The 2026 editions add a powered flow hood for continuous fans, both modes, calibration from at least 4 l/s and a 10-second average.
- Appendix C compares each measured flow with its design value. The ±5% is device accuracy, not a margin, and a good total does not make up for one terminal below design.
- For MVHR, the 2026 editions add a 10% supply-to-extract balance in background mode, without saying which total it is taken from.
- From 24 March 2027 in England, flows in existing homes should be tested for new centralised systems, fans on ducts over 2 m and fans with more than one 90° offset (ADF 2026 4.26). Wales already measures on notifiable existing-home work and adds the same list (ADF 2026 (Wales) 4.18).
- Under the 2026 editions, a single intermittent fan in an existing home needs "appropriate training"; everything else needs a member of a body that independently verifies competence. No qualification is named.
- Work notified before the new dates and started before March 2028 (4 March in Wales, 24 March in England) stays under the old edition, building by building.
- Record observations and paperwork, never quote unmeasured flows, and refer: to the Retrofit Coordinator on a project, the installer otherwise, and a Gas Safe registered engineer for an open-flued gas appliance with fans.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** A commissioning sheet shows a bathroom extract terminal at 7.8 l/s measured against a design high rate of 8 l/s, and the total continuous extract for the house is above the whole dwelling rate. What does ADF Appendix C paragraph C2 say?

A) The terminal meets the design standard because 7.8 is within 5% of 8
B) The terminal meets the design standard because the house total is above the whole dwelling rate
C) The terminal is below its design value, so an adjustment should be made and all flows remeasured
D) C2 applies only to new dwellings, so the reading can be ignored in an existing home

**Correct answer: C.** C2 compares each measured value with its design value; if any is lower, an adjustment should be made and all flows remeasured. The ±5% is the device's accuracy, not a margin, and a good total does not cover one terminal (Sections 2 and 3).

---

**Q2.** Under ADF 2026 (England) 4.24, which device should be used to measure the flow of a continuous extract fan?

A) A vane anemometer held at the grille
B) A powered flow hood air flow measurement device
C) Any handheld anemometer accurate to ±10%
D) A smoke pencil and stopwatch

**Correct answer: B.** 4.24(d) says continuous extract and supply fans should be measured with a powered flow hood. A vane anemometer may be used as an alternative for intermittent extract fans only (Section 2).

---

**Q3.** From 24 March 2027, in which case does ADF 2026 (England) 4.26 say air flow rate testing should be carried out for work to an existing dwelling?

A) Only where the householder asks for it
B) For every replacement fan, whatever the duct
C) For a new mechanical ventilation fan connected to ductwork with more than one 90° offset
D) Only for MVHR systems in flats

**Correct answer: C.** 4.26 lists new centralised systems or major changes to existing ones, new fans with ductwork longer than 2 m, and new fans connected to ductwork with more than one 90° offset (Section 4).

---

**Q4.** A householder asks whether the person who replaced a single bathroom extract fan in their existing home was qualified to commission it. What do ADF 2026 (England) 4.10 and ADF 2026 (Wales) 4.2 say?

A) The person must hold a named national qualification in ventilation
B) The person should have had appropriate training; no qualification is named
C) Only a member of a competent person scheme may commission any fan
D) No commissioning is needed for a single fan

**Correct answer: B.** For a single intermittent extract fan in an existing dwelling, the person "should have had appropriate training". Membership of a body that independently verifies competence applies to all other mechanical ventilation. Neither text names a qualification (Section 4).

---

**Q5.** On a pre-works visit you find an open-flued gas boiler in the kitchen, a new extract fan in the adjoining utility room, and no commissioning sheet for the fan. What is the appropriate record and action?

A) Switch off the fan and tell the householder the boiler is unsafe
B) Record the appliance, the fan and the missing paperwork; suggest the householder promptly contacts a Gas Safe registered engineer; pass the findings to the Retrofit Coordinator
C) Record nothing, as fans and boilers are outside an energy assessment
D) Hold a tissue to the fan and record the result as a flow

**Correct answer: B.** ADF 0.17 warns fans might cause combustion gases to spill from open-flued appliances, even in separate rooms, and 0.18 says appliances must operate safely whether or not fans run. Record, refer promptly to a Gas Safe registered engineer, and pass the missing record to the Retrofit Coordinator. Do not adjust anything or judge safety yourself (Sections 5 and 6).
