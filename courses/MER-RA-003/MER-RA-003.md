# Retrofit Assessment: What PAS 2035:2023 Clause 7 Requires You to Record and Report
**Course ID:** MER-RA-003 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- State who may carry out a PAS 2035:2023 retrofit assessment under Annex A, and when it happens.
- Work through clause 7.3.2, items a) to l), and say what each asks of you on site.
- Record existing ventilation under item f), and handle the missing Table C.1 honestly.
- Explain the energy model in clause 7.3.3, how it differs from an EPC, and where the RdSAP 10 Conventions govern your data.
- Prepare a report that meets clause 7.4, including the defect categories and the Annex E significance survey.

## Introduction

This course goes item by item through what PAS 2035:2023, "Retrofitting dwellings for improved energy efficiency. Specification and guidance" (BSI, September 2023), requires a retrofit assessment and its report to contain. It is written from clause 7, Annexes A and E and the parts of clause 8 that use your data, and goes deeper than the overview in MER-RA-001. References are to PAS 2035:2023 unless another document is named.

PAS 2035:2023 came into effect on 30 March 2025, when the 2019 edition was withdrawn (Foreword), and BSI listed it as "Current" in October 2026. It is a PAS, not a British Standard, and its requirements are the sentences using "shall" (Foreword). These bind you where a scheme, contract or funding rule adopts the standard. Here, "requires" means a "shall".

The standard is written for the whole UK: it cites the ventilation standards of England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland (clause 2, references N3 to N6) and accepts qualifications on all four nations' regulated registers (A.1.1). Approved Document F is cited here for England only.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition presented Annex A as a schedule of mandatory, condition-dependent and recommended data fields; Annex A sets out qualifications, and the content of an assessment is in clause 7. Its data categories, moisture-risk indicators, TrustMark rejection reasons, photo pack and rainwater goods rule had no source, and its statement that an EPC must be lodged where none exists is not in the standard and does not match TrustMark's guidance.

The course is correct to 12 October 2026.

## Section 1: Who Assesses, and When

**In short:** Check you meet Annex A, for example as a domestic energy assessor certified and registered by an assessor body or as a RICS registered surveyor, and for a traditionally constructed dwelling also hold the Level 3 older and traditional buildings award or a listed equivalent. Every dwelling to receive energy retrofit work is assessed first, to inform the Designer; after a distressed replacement of a heating appliance, assess as soon as possible during or after the work.

### The Retrofit Assessor

A retrofit assessment is the "survey, inspection and assessment" of a building to collate information for a retrofit design (clause 3.27). A Retrofit Assessor shall be qualified in accordance with Annex A (clause 7.3.1). Annex A is titled "Qualifications"; the other annexes cover quality assurance schemes (B), ventilation (C), interactions between measures (D) and significance for traditional buildings (E). Under A.1.1 the assessor shall be one of these:
- a domestic energy assessor certified and registered by an assessor body;
- someone working towards that through a recognised RPEL process or a course on a regulated qualification register;
- a RICS registered surveyor (AssocRICS, MRICS or FRICS).

For a traditionally constructed dwelling the assessor shall also hold the Level 3 Award in Energy Efficiency for Older and Traditional Buildings or one of the two listed equivalents (A.1.2).

### When the assessment happens

The Retrofit Coordinator shall be involved from inception, before the assessment (clause 6.1.5), and shall consult the client and agree and record the project's intended outcomes at the outset (clause 6.2.1). The Coordinator shall confirm that every dwelling to receive energy retrofit work is first assessed by a Retrofit Assessor, to inform the Retrofit Designer (clause 7.1.1).

Two cases differ:
- Distressed replacement of a heating appliance: the assessment shall be completed as soon as possible during or after the work (clause 7.2.1).
- Large-scale projects: every dwelling shall be assessed before the design package is complete, though a note allows one document per archetype, reporting differences by exception (clause 7.4.5).

### BS 40104:2025

BSI has published BS 40104:2025, "Retrofit assessment for domestic dwellings. Code of practice" (24 September 2025). BSI says it "builds on the foundation set by PAS 2035 and PAS 2030" and describes a "risk-based methodology" for assessing dwellings before retrofit (BSI catalogue page; press release, 28 October 2025). PAS 2035:2023 does not refer to it, and this course does not teach its content; ask your scheme provider whether it applies to your work.

## Section 2: What the Assessment Shall Include

**In short:** All twelve items of clause 7.3.2 are required; its notes are informative. Locate defects, leaks, condensation and mould and record their severity whatever measures are in view, measure the heat loss envelope showing how elements meet at corners, junctions and within openings, and confirm whether services are working correctly or otherwise. If occupants are out, the occupancy appraisal is still required; a sensible habit is to obtain it by a method your scheme accepts and record how and from whom.

Clause 7.3.2 lists twelve items. Notes 1 to 14 among them are informative, not requirements (Foreword), and are mostly written with "might" and "should".

### Setting and planning: items a) to c)

The first three items are:
- a) location, orientation and how the dwelling attaches to other dwellings or buildings;
- b) an appraisal of heritage, architectural features, structure, construction, condition and the installed ventilation, heating, hot water and lighting, in enough detail to establish suitability for improvement;
- c) constraints from the local planning authority, such as planning permission, listing, conservation areas and tree preservation orders.

A sensible habit is to check listing and conservation area status with the local planning authority before the visit.

### Defects: item d)

Item d) asks for the location and severity of any construction or structural defects or leaks, and of any condensation or mould growth. It applies to every assessment, whatever measures are in view. The notes say this might need test equipment or opening up (note 1) and point to BS 5250:2021 Annex C for diagnosing damp (note 4). In practice, a leaking gutter or downpipe wetting a wall is the kind of leak item d) asks you to locate and describe the severity of; designing for rainwater resilience is the Designer's job (clause 8.2.21 b).

### Measures and fabric: items e), g) and h)

The fabric items are:
- e) any energy efficiency measure (EEM) already installed or proposed;
- g) an appraisal of the construction sufficient to establish the main materials, U-values and moisture properties of the exposed floors, walls and roofs;
- h) a measured survey of the heat loss envelope, including basements and attics, the dimensions of all elements and of all window and door openings, showing how elements relate at corners, junctions and within openings.

Note 8 says that for traditional buildings RdSAP default U-values might not be appropriate (see Section 3). Note 9 says a window's position within the wall governs the thermal bridging around it, which matters to the design if it includes wall insulation. Existing measures from item e) count alongside proposed ones when the design is placed in an Approved Document F category (clause 8.2.13, note 2). For item h), a sensible habit is to sketch and photograph eaves, verges, floor edges and reveals, and note where each frame sits in the wall, because a design with fabric measures shall include details for those junctions (clause 8.2.15).

### Site and services: items i) and j)

The next two items are:
- i) site constraints, such as elevation and exposure to sun, wind and rain, major roads and industrial activity, access, party walls, rights of light and adjoining properties;
- j) the installed ventilation, heating, hot water and lighting systems and controls, their age and service history where available, where the equipment is, the areas served, and confirmation that they are working correctly or otherwise.

Item j) does not ask for emitter sizing, which is a design matter (clause 8.2.19), but it does ask whether things work. A sensible habit is to run the heating and hot water, operate controls and fans, and ask for service records.

### Occupancy and other risks: items k) and l)

The last two items are:
- k) an appraisal of occupancy, including the number of occupants and special considerations such as vulnerable people, for example children, elderly people or people with disabilities;
- l) other information that might affect the project, including noise, air pollution and flooding, and climate change risks such as extreme weather, overheating and higher relative humidity.

Item k) does not say how occupancy information is gathered. If the occupants are out, the appraisal is still required; a sensible habit is to obtain it by a method your scheme accepts and record how and from whom. For item l), note 10 says a property in a flood risk area should have an assessment by a property flood resilience surveyor.

## Section 3: Ventilation, the Energy Model and RdSAP Data

**In short:** Record any fan's position and type, with make and model where possible or the reasons if not, and the size and location of any background ventilators. The 2023 text has no Table C.1, so do not invent a checklist: ask your scheme provider which it expects and state in your report which you used. The energy model gives an estimate, not an EPC. In RdSAP 10, overwrite default wall, roof and floor U-values only on documentary evidence meeting convention 3.08.

### Ventilation: item f)

Item f) requires an inspection and review of the existing ventilation following "the checklist provided in Table C.1", and in addition you shall:
- record the position and type of any fans (for example intermittent, continuous mechanical extract, MVHR, single room heat recovery or PIV), with make and model where possible, and if not possible, record the reasons;
- record the size and location of all background ventilators where present;
- in any functional testing, follow the relevant nation's ventilation standard and BSRIA procedures for fan measurement, and report measured air flows below its minimum.

There is a gap in the text. The September 2023 edition contains no Table C.1: its list of tables names only Tables E.1 and E.2. Do not invent a checklist. Ask your scheme provider which one it expects, and state in your report which you used. For England, Approved Document F Volume 1 (2021 edition) has Table D1, a checklist for ventilation provision in existing dwellings, which Annex C itself cites (C.3.1; Approved Document F Volume 1, Appendix D). PAS 2035:2023 cites the 2021 edition; a 2026 edition has been published and takes effect on 24 March 2027 (24 September 2027 for work connected with higher-risk building work), so the 2021 edition is the one in use today.

If the specified and existing measures fall into Category B or C of Approved Document F Diagram 3.1 and Table 3.1, or your assessment found condensation or mould, the Designer shall confirm whether the existing ventilation is adequate and, if needed, specify an upgrade under Annex C (clause 8.2.13). Annex C describes acceptable systems in terms of extract from the wet rooms and, for continuous mechanical extract and for an existing intermittent extract system, correctly sized background ventilators (C.2.2 and C.2.3), which is why their positions and sizes matter.

### The energy model: clause 7.3.3

The data collected under clause 7.3.2 shall be used to estimate annual fuel use, fuel costs and carbon dioxide emissions, under actual occupancy where data is available or otherwise standard occupancy. The Assessor, Coordinator or Designer shall use a recognised model "such as RdSAP, SAP or PHPP", and no RdSAP version is named (clause 7.3.3). The guidance warns that RdSAP makes default assumptions, so its data should be reviewed carefully and preferably exported to full SAP (clause 4.3.2).

That is an energy estimate, not an EPC. The standard's EPC provision comes after handover, when the Coordinator shall recommend a new or updated EPC and, if the client agrees, arrange for a domestic energy assessor to produce it (clause 11.1.4). TrustMark says the Retrofit Assessor uses RdSAP data to create an Energy Report or Energy Performance Report (EPR), lodged with TrustMark, and that an EPC, if required, is a separate exercise by a domestic energy assessor with a second data set, lodged on the National EPC Registers for England and Wales or Scotland (TrustMark, RdSAP Data Production for Retrofit Projects, V1.1). Its EPR Variation Conventions, written for ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme only, say the conventions in that document must not be used in producing an EPC, which must follow the government approved RdSAP conventions, and that EPRs are not to be confused with EPCs (V1.4, opening paragraph and convention 5.01).

### RdSAP data alongside the assessment

Where you use RdSAP 10, the Conventions (v12.2) for RdSAP 10, applicable from 6 August 2026, govern the data set. Three points meet clause 7.3.2:
- Windows: measure and enter the width and height of every relevant window, highly glazed door and roof light in the heat loss envelope, between the internal reveals (convention 2.15; RdSAP10 Specification v4.0, section 3.7.1).
- Measurements: state whether dimensions are internal or external, and measure to 0.01 m or better (conventions 2.01 and 2.02).
- U-values: default wall, roof and floor U-values must not be overwritten without documentary evidence for that property, following BR 443, provided and recorded: building control approval that correctly defines the construction and states the calculated U-value, or a calculation produced or verified by someone with suitable expertise (convention 3.08). For ECO4 and Great British Insulation Scheme EPRs, overridden U-values are not accepted without the scheme provider's prior agreement (TrustMark, EPR Variation Conventions V1.4, convention 2.01).

Where note 8 leads you to calculate or measure a U-value, a sensible approach is to keep the RdSAP data strictly to the conventions and report the other evidence, with its basis, to the Designer under item g).

## Section 4: Traditional Buildings and the Report

**In short:** For a traditionally constructed dwelling, listed or not, assess significance before design starts using the Table E.2 checklist and, where retrofit might affect appearance, tick the box for each significant feature with details and photographs; a protected building's assessment also follows BS 7913. Report to the Designer with any data file and photographs of all recorded features and defects, putting any defects, leaks, condensation or mould into two categories: repair before retrofit work can proceed, or recommended but not essential.

### Traditional and protected buildings

Traditional construction means solid brick, stone or earth external walls of moisture-permeable materials, usually built before 1919, or pre-1919 timber frame (clause 3.39). For a dwelling of traditional construction, listed or not, an assessment of significance shall also be carried out in accordance with BS 7913; a note says Annex E gives a simplified version (clause 7.3.4). For a protected building, meaning listed or in a conservation area (clause 3.23), the assessment shall also follow BS 7913 and include an assessment of significance (clause 7.3.4).

Annex E is normative and is the simplified version for traditional buildings; Table E.1 is described as adapted for unprotected traditional buildings, and a protected building follows BS 7913 as clause 7.3.4 says. For the assessment Annex E requires that:
- significance is assessed before retrofit proposals are formulated and design starts, in proportion to the building's importance (E.1.3);
- the Table E.2 Significance Survey Checklist is used, by an assessor qualified for traditional buildings (E.1.4);
- where retrofit might affect appearance, significant walls, windows and doors, roofs and internal features are analysed (E.1.5);
- where such features are found, the box on Table E.2 is ticked with details and photographs (E.1.6).

Table E.1 describes significance through architectural and aesthetic, communal, evidential and historical values. Table E.2 covers form and context, roof covering, walls, original windows and historic glass, internal features, floor materials, alterations and potential to enhance significance. Later, if a proposed measure affects something identified as significant, a heritage impact assessment to BS 7913 shall be carried out as part of the options appraisal (E.1.2; clause 8.2.33).

### The report: clause 7.4

The assessment, including the ventilation assessment, shall be reported to the Retrofit Designer with any RdSAP, SAP or PHPP data file and a photographic record of all the recorded features of the building and any identified defects (clause 7.4.1). The standard lists no minimum set of photographs; if your scheme sets one, follow it.

Where you have found defects, leaks, condensation or mould, the report shall identify them in two categories (clause 7.4.2):
- defects that need to be repaired before any retrofit work can proceed;
- repairs that are recommended but are not an essential prerequisite to retrofit.

The Coordinator shall confirm with the Designer that the report is sufficient to design from. If it is not, the Designer identifies what is missing and the Coordinator arranges for you to collect it and update the report (clauses 7.4.3, 7.4.4 and 8.2.5). A note says that if the certification body requires it, you can upload the report, data file and photographs to the TrustMark data warehouse (clause 7.4.4, note).

### How clause 8 uses your report

Everything after the assessment draws on it:
- The Coordinator's options evaluation shall use your data file, applying cost rates to the dwelling's areas from the assessment data (clauses 8.1.1 and 8.1.3).
- The Designer shall confirm they hold a clause 7 assessment before designing, and shall take account of the building's condition, including repairs and causes of damp, and of your report and ventilation assessment (clauses 8.2.3 and 8.2.8).
- The Coordinator gives the installer copies of your assessment (clause 8.2.26), and the Retrofit Evaluator's basic evaluation findings come back to you (clause 13.4.2).

## Key Takeaways

The points to carry away:
- Annex A sets qualifications; what an assessment shall include is clause 7.3.2 a) to l), and the report is clause 7.4.
- A Retrofit Assessor is a certified domestic energy assessor (or working towards it) or a RICS registered surveyor, with an extra award for traditional buildings (A.1.1 and A.1.2).
- Defects, leaks, condensation and mould are recorded in every assessment and reported in two categories: repair before retrofit, or recommended but not a prerequisite (clauses 7.3.2 d and 7.4.2).
- Ventilation records cover fans (make and model, or why not), background ventilators and, where you test, flows below the minimum; Table C.1 is missing from the 2023 text, so agree a checklist with your scheme.
- Clause 7.3.3 asks for an energy model estimate, not an EPC; TrustMark treats any EPC as separate from the retrofit RdSAP data.
- RdSAP data follow the RdSAP 10 Conventions v12.2, including the evidence rule for overriding default U-values; report other U-value evidence to the Designer.
- For traditional buildings, the Table E.2 significance survey is done before design starts, with details and photographs of significant features.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Where does PAS 2035:2023 set out what a retrofit assessment shall include?

A) Annex A, as a schedule of mandatory and recommended data fields
B) Annex B, on quality assurance schemes
C) Clause 7.3.2, items a) to l), with the report under clause 7.4
D) Annex D, on interactions between measures

**Correct answer: C.** Clause 7.3.2 lists the twelve items and clause 7.4 sets the report; Annex A is "Qualifications" (PAS 2035:2023, contents, clauses 7.3.2 and 7.4).

---

**Q2.** You cannot read the make and model of a bathroom extract fan without dismantling it. What does clause 7.3.2 f) require?

A) Record its position and type, and include in the record the reasons the make and model could not be given
B) Leave the fan out of the record, since an unidentified fan cannot be assessed
C) Record it as continuous mechanical extract ventilation by default
D) Stop the assessment until the make and model are known

**Correct answer: A.** Item f) asks for the position and type of any fans with make and model where possible, and if not possible the reasons shall be recorded (PAS 2035:2023, clause 7.3.2 f)).

---

**Q3.** A client asks whether your PAS 2035 assessment means an EPC will be lodged for the house. Which statement is correct?

A) Yes: clause 7.3.3 requires an EPC to be lodged where no current one exists
B) Yes: Annex A requires an EPC reference for every assessment
C) No EPC is involved at any stage of a PAS 2035 project
D) Clause 7.3.3 requires an energy model estimate, not an EPC; after handover the Coordinator recommends a new or updated EPC

**Correct answer: D.** Clause 7.3.3 asks for an estimate of fuel use, costs and emissions using a model such as RdSAP, SAP or PHPP, and clause 11.1.4 has the Coordinator recommend an EPC after handover (PAS 2035:2023, clauses 7.3.3 and 11.1.4).

---

**Q4.** You find a broken downpipe saturating a solid wall and mould in the bedroom behind it. How shall the report present these defects?

A) As a single list ranked by repair cost
B) In two categories: defects to be repaired before any retrofit work can proceed, and repairs recommended but not an essential prerequisite
C) Only if insulation of that wall is proposed
D) Only the mould, since external defects are for the Designer to find

**Correct answer: B.** Item d) applies to every assessment, and clause 7.4.2 requires defects, leaks, condensation and mould to be reported in those two categories (PAS 2035:2023, clauses 7.3.2 d and 7.4.2).

---

**Q5.** On an unlisted pre-1919 solid brick terrace you find original sash windows with historic glass. Under Annex E, what shall you do?

A) Nothing, because Annex E applies only to listed buildings
B) Carry out a heritage impact assessment of the windows yourself before a Coordinator is appointed
C) Tick the relevant box on the Table E.2 significance survey checklist and add details and photographs of the feature
D) Record the windows in the RdSAP data set only

**Correct answer: C.** Clause 7.3.4 applies to traditional construction, listed or not, and E.1.6 requires the Table E.2 box ticked with details and photographs; heritage impact assessment comes later, in the options appraisal (PAS 2035:2023, clause 7.3.4, E.1.2 and E.1.6).
