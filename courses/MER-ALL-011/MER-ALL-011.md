# Non-Domestic MEES: The EPC E Minimum in Force and the Proposed EPC B Standard for 2031

## Learning Objectives
- State the minimum standard that is law for let non-domestic property in England and Wales, and where it is set
- Explain the seven-year payback test and the registered exemptions
- Distinguish what the government said on 18 June 2026 about larger buildings from 2031 from what has been legislated
- Identify what that announcement leaves undecided and where the answers will come from
- Apply the non-domestic EPC conventions on defaults, evidence, U-values and measurement to an EPC a landlord will rely on

---

## Introduction

This course covers England and Wales only, not Scotland or Northern Ireland. The minimum energy efficiency standard (MEES) for let non-domestic property is set by the Energy Efficiency (Private Rented Property) (England and Wales) Regulations 2015, SI 2015/962.

This edition replaces the July 2026 edition. That edition taught an interim EPC C step in 2027 or 2028, a rising £3,500 non-domestic cost cap, possible EPC C for smaller buildings, and EPC B by 2031 as a confirmed requirement. In fact the EPC C step was a 2021 proposal dropped on 18 June 2026, the £3,500 cap is a domestic rule, smaller buildings are intended to stay at E, and EPC B from 2031 has not been legislated.

The course is correct to 12 October 2026. On that date the position was:
- Law in force: EPC band E is the minimum for a let non-domestic property of any size (SI 2015/962, regulations 22 and 27).
- Government intention, not law: EPC B from 2031 for private rented buildings over 1,000 square metres, "where cost effective" (DESNZ interim response, 18 June 2026).
- Still to come: the full government response and the secondary legislation. Neither had been published.

Expect a further edition when the full response or draft regulations appear.

## Section 1: The law in force, EPC E under the 2015 Regulations

**In short:** The newest valid EPC decides whether a let non-domestic property in England or Wales is sub-standard: below band E, whatever the building's size, the landlord must not grant, extend or renew a tenancy, or since 1 April 2023 continue letting, unless an exemption applies. Some properties are excluded, as set out below, but do not tell a client that a listed building is automatically outside MEES.

Part 3 of SI 2015/962 sets the non-domestic standard. It is in force; the 2031 standard in Section 3 is not.

### Which properties are caught

A non-domestic private rented (PR) property is one that is situated in England and Wales, is let under a tenancy and is not a dwelling (Energy Act 2011, section 42(1)(b); SI 2015/962, regulation 20(1)). An owner-occupied building is not let under a tenancy, so it is outside. Regulation 20 then excludes:
- property that is not, and is not part of a building that is, required to have an EPC under the Building Regulations 2010 or the EPB Regulations, and was not required to under the earlier 2007 regulations (regulation 20(2))
- property let on a term certain "not exceeding six months", unless the agreement provides for renewal or extension beyond six months or, when the tenancy is granted, the tenant has been in continuous occupation for more than 12 months (regulation 20(3)(a))
- property let on a tenancy granted for a term certain of 99 years or more (regulation 20(3)(b))

Whether a building needs an EPC depends on the Building Regulations 2010 and the Energy Performance of Buildings (England and Wales) Regulations 2012, SI 2012/3118 (the EPB Regulations). Officially protected buildings are excluded only so far as compliance would "unacceptably alter their character or appearance" (regulation 5(1)(a)), and the guidance says listed buildings "will not necessarily be exempt" from needing an EPC (BEIS guidance, February 2017, box after paragraph 35). Do not tell a client that a listed building is automatically outside MEES.

### The standard and the two dates

A property is sub-standard if its valid EPC shows an energy performance indicator below "band E" (regulation 22). An EPC is valid for this purpose if it was entered on the register no more than 10 years before it is relied on and no later EPC for the property has been entered since (regulation 22), so the newest one counts.

A landlord must not let a sub-standard non-domestic PR property unless regulation 29 or one of the Chapter 4 exemptions applies (regulation 27(1)). "Let" has two meanings, each with its own date (regulation 27(2)):
- from 1 April 2018, granting a new tenancy, or extending or renewing an existing one
- from 1 April 2023, continuing to let the property

The standard is the same whatever the size of the building. When this course was checked, legislation.gov.uk showed band E in regulation 22, no outstanding changes to Chapters 1 and 3 of Part 3, and no draft amending regulations. A breach does not affect the validity or enforceability of any provision of the tenancy (regulation 30).

### Enforcement

The enforcement authority is the local weights and measures authority (regulation 34(1)(b)). Penalties under regulation 41:
- letting in breach of regulation 27 for less than three months: up to the greater of £5,000 and 10% of rateable value, capped at £50,000 (regulation 41(2))
- in breach for three months or more: up to the greater of £10,000 and 20% of rateable value, capped at £150,000 (regulation 41(3))
- registering false or misleading information, or failing to comply with a compliance notice: up to £5,000 (regulation 41(4))

Each also carries the publication penalty: details such as the breach, the address and the penalty can be published on the PRS Exemptions Register for at least 12 months (regulation 39).

## Section 2: The seven-year payback test and the exemptions

**In short:** A commercial landlord who quotes you a £3,500 cap is reading the domestic rules: for Table 6 improvements the non-domestic test is a simple payback of seven years or less, with cost taken excluding VAT. The evidence registered for a payback exemption includes three quotations, not your EPC, and a savings calculation is a separate instruction whose scope it is sensible to agree in writing. Exemptions can be relied on only once registered.

### Where the list of improvements comes from

The prohibition turns on "relevant energy efficiency improvements". For non-domestic property an improvement is relevant if it is listed in the Schedule to the Green Deal (Qualifying Energy Improvements) Order 2012 or in Table 6 of Approved Document L2B, and has been recommended for the property in a green deal report, a recommendation report or a report by a surveyor (regulation 28(1)). A recommendation report means an energy assessor's recommendations "for the cost-effective improvement of the energy performance of a building" (SI 2012/3118, regulation 4(1)). Your report identifies measures and notes indicative paybacks; the government guide does not describe it as giving prices (Guide to EPCs for non-dwellings, section 5).

### The payback test

A Table 6 improvement is relevant only if it would achieve "a simple payback of seven years or less" (regulation 28(3)). The BEIS guidance of February 2017 says the Green Deal Order list would apply only where Green Deal finance is used, which was then unavailable for new plans (paragraphs 43 and 45). The guidance applies the test to "a measure, or a package of measures" (BEIS guidance, February 2017, paragraph 47). The calculation compares:
- the savings on energy bills over seven years from completion of the installation, calculated using relevant energy prices (regulation 28(5) and (7))
- the cost of purchasing and installing the improvement "excluding value added tax", multiplied by an interest rate factor based on the Bank of England base rate at the time of the calculation (regulation 28(6))

The improvement passes if the savings are the same as or greater than that repayment cost (regulation 28(4)).

Regulation 28 contains no pound figure and no cost cap. The £3,500 "cost cap" is in regulation 24(4), in the chapter on domestic PR property, and is applied to costs including VAT (regulation 24(3)). A commercial landlord who quotes £3,500 to you is reading the domestic rules.

The evidence for a payback exemption is not your EPC. Where the landlord relies on regulation 29, the registered information includes, for an improvement not made because it fails the payback test, "copies of three quotations" for purchasing and installing it (SI 2015/962, Schedule, paragraph 1(i)(i)); the gov.uk guidance also asks for the cost calculations. The regulations "do not specify who should carry out the savings calculation", and a landlord without in-house capability is likely to need "a competent non-domestic energy assessor" using the landlord's energy prices (BEIS guidance, February 2017, paragraph 49). That is a separate instruction; a sensible habit is to agree its scope in writing.

### The exemptions and how long they last

The gov.uk landlord guidance (DESNZ, last updated 5 May 2026) lists six exemptions:
- seven-year payback: five years
- all improvements made: all relevant improvements have been made, or none can be made; five years from registration (regulation 29)
- wall insulation: a written opinion from a relevant person or an independent installer that the measure is not appropriate because of its potential negative impact on the fabric or structure (regulation 28(2); guidance paragraph 59); five years
- third-party consent: the tenant refused consent, or, despite reasonable efforts by the landlord, a third party's consent was refused or granted on a condition the landlord "cannot reasonably comply" with (regulation 31(1)); five years, but a tenant-refusal exemption ends with that tenant's tenancy (regulation 31(3))
- property devaluation: an independent surveyor's report that the improvement would cut the market value of the property, or its building, by "more than 5%" (regulation 32(2)); five years
- recently becoming a landlord: six months, in circumstances listed in regulation 33, including a new lease created by operation of law or under Part 2 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954, and buying a property already let

Exemptions are registered on the PRS Exemptions Register (regulation 36(2)) and can be relied on only once registered (for example regulation 31(2)). The guidance says registration is on "a self-certification basis" and an exemption applies "from the point at which it is registered". The public can search registered exemptions and penalties through a gov.uk service page published on 5 May 2026.

## Section 3: The 2031 proposal, what was said and what is still open

**In short:** If a client asks whether their building must reach B by 2031, the accurate answer today is no: the law requires E, and the government intends B from 2031 for private rented buildings over 1,000 square metres, where cost effective, which needs secondary legislation. The interim response does not give the start day, how the area is measured or the enforcement model, so do not fill those gaps with the 2021 proposals.

### How we got here

The government consulted in 2019 and again in 2021. The 2021 consultation (England and Wales, 17 March to 9 June 2021) proposed "one interim milestone: EPC C by 2027" and EPC B for all non-domestic rented buildings by 1 April 2030, with compliance windows and "a move away from enforcement at the point of let". It said the government intended "to retain the payback threshold at 7 years", and proposed replacing the "three quotes" system with a calculator giving standardised purchase and installation costs (BEIS consultation document, March 2021, pages 20 to 23 and 38 to 39). These were proposals. None was legislated.

### The interim response of 18 June 2026

DESNZ published an interim response to the 2019 and 2021 consultations on 18 June 2026. The government said it was "confirming its intention to implement" the following:
- from 2031, "it is proposed" that private rented buildings over 1,000 square metres in England and Wales reach EPC B, "where cost effective"
- buildings below 1,000 square metres are intended to stay on the current EPC E minimum (a later sentence gives landlords of smaller properties flexibility to upgrade over time, "with no set deadline for going beyond this level")
- the interim EPC C milestone for 2027 "will not be taken forward"
- existing flexibility mechanisms, "including the 7-year payback test and exemptions", remain, so that only improvements that are "practical, affordable and cost-effective" will be required

The change "will only take effect following the successful passage of secondary legislation through Parliament", and further detail, including on "implementation of the threshold", is to come in the forthcoming government response. The government aims to legislate "at the earliest opportunity" but gives no date.

This is a stated intention, not law. If a client asks whether their building must be at B by 2031, the accurate answer today is no: the law requires E, the government intends B for buildings over 1,000 square metres where cost effective, and the rules are not yet written.

### What the interim response does not tell us

It does not say:
- which day in 2031 the standard would apply from
- how the 1,000 square metres is to be measured, or how it works where only part of a building is let
- whether enforcement stays at the point of letting or moves to compliance windows of the kind proposed in 2021
- whether the three quotes will give way to a calculator

The interim response sits on the gov.uk page for the 2021 consultation, which is the page to watch. Do not fill the gaps with the 2021 proposals; the government has already departed from them.

On scale, the 2021 consultation said commercial and industrial buildings above 1,000 square metres "account for only 7% of all non-domestic buildings" but use over half of the energy used by all non-domestic buildings (BEIS consultation document, March 2021, page 32). That figure is more than five years old and covers all non-domestic buildings, not only rented ones, so it cannot show how many lettings the proposal would affect.

## Section 4: What this means for the assessor's work

**In short:** A prospective or actual tenant can enforce your duty of reasonable care and skill while your EPC is valid, and near a band boundary the conventions on defaults, evidence, U-values and measurement are worth rereading. It is sensible to say that an indicative payback is not the regulation 28 calculation, and for a building near 1,000 square metres, keep your measurement record in good order and tell the client the threshold rule is not yet known.

### Your EPC already carries legal weight

Your EPC decides whether a property is sub-standard (SI 2015/962, regulation 22), and your recommendation report is one source of relevant improvements (regulation 28(1)). Any cost-effective measure you recommend "must be technically feasible" for the building (SI 2012/3118, regulation 4(3)). You "must carry out energy assessments with reasonable care and skill", a duty any prospective or actual tenant can enforce while the certificate is valid (SI 2012/3118, regulation 24). The government guide makes you responsible for the accuracy of the information used and says you must validate it by site inspection (Guide to EPCs for non-dwellings, section 5).

The rating compares the building's carbon emissions with a reference building's, using SBEM or a dynamic simulation model in government approved software (Guide to EPCs for non-dwellings, section 5). The NCM website calls SBEM "primarily a compliance and energy certification procedure and not a design tool", a fair caution for a landlord who wants a guaranteed post-works band.

### The conventions near a boundary

The Non Domestic EPC Conventions for England and Wales, Issue 9 (the document prints no issue date), "must be applied by all accredited NDEAs" (non-domestic energy assessors). Five are worth rereading when a rating sits near a band boundary:
- defaults: use software defaults only in the absence of a convention or more specific information on the building, record evidence of why the default was selected so your accreditation scheme can check it, and avoid defaults where possible (convention 2.02)
- as-built data: it is not acceptable to use design documentation only to produce an EPC (convention 2.06)
- documentary evidence: there must be "a demonstrable link" between what you saw on site and the documents you rely on (convention 2.07)
- U-values: library or inference values apply in the absence of more specific values; entered values only where calculated by a Level 4 qualified assessor or other suitably qualified and accredited person, or taken from an as-built specification, with the documentation provided if called for audit (convention 3.07)
- measurement: measurements entered must be recorded to at least two decimal places in site notes, and calculated values need supporting calculations for audit (convention 4.04)

### Recommendations and floor area

The government guide says the recommendation report groups measures by payback: short term (less than 3 years), medium term (between 3 and 7 years), long term (greater than 7 years), plus other recommendations based on the assessor's knowledge, and that paybacks are "indicative" (Guide to EPCs for non-dwellings, section 5). An indicative payback is not the regulation 28 calculation; a sensible habit is to say so when you send the report.

On floor area, the conventions say horizontal dimensions "generally follow" the RICS definition of Gross Internal Area (Appendix 10.03, on pages with an Issue 8.1 footer, so confirm the current issue with your scheme). Whether the proposed threshold will use the EPC floor area, or the building or the let part, has not been published. For a building near 1,000 square metres, keep your measurement record in good order and tell the client the rule is not yet known.

This course gives no figures for how far a measure moves a rating, because none was found in the sources read.

## Key Takeaways
- At 12 October 2026 the only legal minimum for let non-domestic property in England and Wales is EPC band E, whatever the size (SI 2015/962, regulations 22 and 27)
- EPC B from 2031 for buildings over 1,000 square metres, where cost effective, is a government intention of 18 June 2026 that needs secondary legislation
- The 2027 EPC C milestone was a 2021 proposal and has been dropped; buildings below 1,000 square metres are intended to stay on E
- There is no non-domestic cost cap: the test is a seven-year simple payback, which is being kept; £3,500 is the domestic cap
- Exemptions must be registered before they can be relied on; most last five years, the new landlord exemption six months
- Your recommendation report is one source of relevant improvements; cost evidence comes from three installer quotations and a separate payback calculation
- The conventions require evidence for defaults, as-built documents, a demonstrable link to site observations and documented U-values
- The threshold measurement rule, the start day in 2031 and the enforcement model are unpublished; watch the gov.uk consultation page

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** On 12 October 2026, what minimum EPC band does the law require before a landlord in England may grant a new five-year lease of a 2,500 square metre office?

A) Band B
B) Band E
C) Band C
D) Band D

**Correct answer: B.** The minimum level is band E for every non-domestic PR property (SI 2015/962, regulations 22 and 27); band B for larger buildings is a proposal (DESNZ interim response, 18 June 2026), not law.

---

**Q2.** What did the government's interim response of 18 June 2026 say about the interim EPC C milestone for 2027?

A) It will apply to new leases from 2027 and existing leases from 2028
B) It will apply only to buildings over 1,000 square metres
C) It has been moved back to 2029
D) It will not be taken forward

**Correct answer: D.** The interim response says the previously proposed interim EPC C milestone for 2027 "will not be taken forward" (DESNZ interim response, 18 June 2026); it gives no replacement date.

---

**Q3.** Which cost test applies to improvements to a let non-domestic property under the regulations in force?

A) A simple payback of seven years or less, with cost taken excluding VAT
B) A cost cap of £3,500 including VAT
C) A cost cap of £10,000 excluding VAT
D) A cap of 10% of the property's rateable value

**Correct answer: A.** Regulation 28(3) sets the seven-year simple payback test and regulation 28(6) takes cost excluding VAT; £3,500 is the domestic cap in regulation 24(4), and 10% of rateable value is part of the penalty in regulation 41(2).

---

**Q4.** Under the interim response, what is intended for let non-domestic buildings below 1,000 square metres?

A) EPC C from 2031
B) EPC B from 2035
C) They continue on the current EPC E minimum, with no set deadline for going beyond it
D) They are removed from MEES altogether

**Correct answer: C.** The interim response says buildings below 1,000 square metres are intended to stay on the current EPC E minimum, and gives landlords of smaller properties flexibility to upgrade over time "with no set deadline for going beyond this level" (DESNZ interim response, 18 June 2026).

---

**Q5.** Under convention 2.02 of the Non Domestic EPC Conventions, when may an assessor use a software default value?

A) Only in the absence of a convention or more specific information on the building, with evidence of why the default was selected
B) Whenever it saves survey time, provided the building is rated C or better
C) Always, because defaults are the approved values for existing buildings
D) Only with the landlord's written consent

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 2.02 allows defaults only in the absence of a convention or more specific information, requires evidence of why the default was selected, and says defaults should be avoided where possible (Non Domestic EPC Conventions, Issue 9, convention 2.02).
