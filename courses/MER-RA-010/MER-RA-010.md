# Thermal Bridging in Retrofit Assessment: What Assessors Must Record
**Course ID:** MER-RA-010 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026
*© Meridian CPD 2026. All rights reserved.*

---

## Learning Objectives
- Define a thermal bridge in the terms PAS 2035:2023 uses, and tell its guidance clauses from its requirements.
- State what clause 7.3.2 requires the assessment to capture at corners, junctions and openings, and separate that from good practice.
- Record condensation and mould at cold spots by location and severity, and report them under clause 7.4.2 without overstating the cause.
- Explain what clause 8 requires of the Retrofit Designer at junctions, and why that depends on your record.
- Explain how RdSAP 10 sets the thermal bridging factor y, and what it cannot tell you about a particular dwelling.

---

## Introduction

This course covers what PAS 2035:2023 asks the Retrofit Assessor to record about thermal bridges, what the Retrofit Designer must then do, and how RdSAP 10 treats thermal bridging. It does not teach psi-value or temperature-factor calculation, which belongs to design.

PAS 2035:2023, "Retrofitting dwellings for improved energy efficiency: Specification and guidance", is the second edition, published by BSI in September 2023. It supersedes PAS 2035:2019+A1:2022, which its Foreword says would be withdrawn on 30 March 2025 (PAS 2035:2023, Foreword). It is a Publicly Available Specification, not law. Its "shall" requirements (Foreword) bind you where your scheme, contract or funding rule adopts the PAS; ask your scheme or Retrofit Coordinator which applies. Clause 4 is headed "Guidance about retrofit building physics" and uses "should"; clauses 7 and 8 are headed "Requirements for" assessments and designs.

The PAS is not written for one nation: its normative references include the ventilation guidance of England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland (clause 2, [N3] to [N6]). The RdSAP material comes from the RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0 (BRE, 7 June 2026), whose age bands cover all four nations (specification Table 1). The government damp and mould guidance quoted in Section 3 applies to England. This course is correct to 12 October 2026.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition said RdSAP 10 sets y by construction type and age, accepts calculated junction psi values and uses "conservative" defaults; the specification sets y by the main dwelling's age band, or park home status, with no psi input. It also said y includes repeating bridges, which the specification does not say, taught a PAS ban on assessor recommendations that is not in the text, and used invented figures (lintel heat-loss percentages and a psi example) and Further Reading titles that could not be traced. Please unlearn those points.

## Section 1: Thermal bridges in PAS 2035:2023

### The definition

PAS 2035:2023 defines a thermal bridge as an area of the building envelope where the insulation is discontinuous, thinner or more conductive than the adjacent insulation, or has reduced effectiveness because of the building geometry, "leading to locally increased heat loss and therefore locally reduced internal surface temperature", or in summer a locally increased heat gain (clause 3.36). So a bridge is found by looking at the insulation layer, and shows itself as extra heat loss and a colder inside surface. The PAS separately defines thermal by-pass, external air getting to the warm side of the insulation (clause 3.37); the design requirements deal with both (clause 8.2.16).

### What clause 4 says you should know

Clause 4 is guidance. Everyone involved, including the Retrofit Assessor, should have a working knowledge of building physics "with emphasis on the movement of heat, moisture and air through dwellings" (clause 4.1.1). Clause 4.3.3 lists calculation techniques, among them:
- linear thermal transmittances (psi values) and temperature factors (fRsi) "at the corners, junctions and edges of building envelopes" identified as thermal bridges;
- the y value, the component of a building's overall thermal transmittance "that is due to thermal bridging";
- assessing the risk of surface condensation and mould growth from temperature factors and internal and external temperature and relative humidity.

Retrofit Assessors "should be familiar with" those techniques (clause 4.3.6). Familiarity is not a duty to calculate: nothing in clause 7 asks the assessor to calculate a psi value, an fRsi or a y value.

Thermography, "including the identification of thermal bridges and insulation discontinuities", is a testing technique (clause 4.3.4 f) that Retrofit Coordinators and Retrofit Evaluators should be able to apply (clause 4.3.6). A thermal imaging survey is not a PAS requirement of the assessment, although identifying defects "might require the use of test equipment or invasive assessments" (clause 7.3.2 d), NOTE 1).

### Why junctions get attention

The PAS's "concentrate on the interfaces" guidance says retrofit projects "often go wrong because of poor attention to the corners, junctions and edges of building elements", where insulation and air barriers should be continuous (clause 5.6.1). BRE makes the related point that "As dwellings have become better insulated, the importance of thermal bridging has increased" (BRE, SAP 10 Technical Paper S10TP-05, Issue 1.2, 11 March 2022).

## Section 2: What the designer must do at junctions

### The design requirements

Clause 8 puts junction design on the Retrofit Designer:
- where the design includes fabric measures (insulation, airtightness or replacement windows, for example), it "shall also include construction details" for the corners, junctions and edges of all affected elements and for all junctions with other elements, improved or not (clause 8.2.15);
- those details shall maintain "the continuity of the three-dimensional insulated envelope" and any airtightness barrier, to eliminate thermal by-pass and minimise thermal bridging; published good-practice details can be used, and any other detail must be shown by calculation to IP1/06 to have an fRsi of not less than 0.75 (clause 8.2.16; IP1/06 is the BRE paper listed as normative reference [N8] in clause 2);
- in a stage-by-stage approach, the design must provide for "mitigating the risks relating to cold surfaces, including condensation and mould" (clause 8.2.17);
- the design documentation that the Retrofit Coordinator provides to the Main Contractor or Retrofit Installer includes construction details "for all corners, junctions and edges of the building envelope" (prepared by the Retrofit Designer or obtained from a system designer), or clear identification of the standard details to be used, and where (clause 8.2.25 g).

Annex D repeats the requirement and gives "window reveals" and "around meter boxes" as examples (Annex D, D.1). Annex A describes interacting fabric measures as those that meet "at junctions, corners and edges, for example wall and roof, or wall and floor, or wall and windows" (Annex A, A.3.5, NOTE 4).

### The installer checks the design

The Retrofit Installer must check that the design provides for "minimizing thermal bridging at the corners, junctions and edges of installed measures", and consult the Retrofit Coordinator where it does not (PAS 2030:2023, clause 5.2.3 and Annex A, A.5).

### Where your assessment comes in

The designer must review your report to confirm it is sufficient for a retrofit design (clause 8.2.4), and the design must take account of "the information in the report(s) of the assessment(s)" (clause 8.2.8 c). If the report is not sufficient, the designer identifies what is missing and the Coordinator arranges for you to collate it in an updated report (clauses 7.4.4 and 8.2.5). A designer who must detail every affected junction needs to know what is there now: how the elements meet, where the openings sit, and where any existing insulation stops.

## Section 3: What you record at junctions, openings and cold spots

### The requirement

Clause 7.3.2 lists what "The assessment shall include". These items bear on thermal bridging:
- a) location, orientation and "a description of the attachment of the dwelling to other dwellings or buildings";
- d) "identification of the location and severity" of any existing defects or leaks, "and of any condensation and/or mould growth in the dwelling";
- e) identification of any energy efficiency measure "already installed or proposed";
- h) a measured survey of the heat loss envelope, the elements and "all window and door openings", "to show the relationship of elements of the thermal envelope to one another at corners, junctions and within openings".

NOTE 9 to item h) gives the PAS's own example: "the position of a window within a wall is important, as this governs the thermal bridging around the window and impacts on the retrofit design if it includes wall insulation". The whole assessment is reported to the Retrofit Designer with any RdSAP, SAP or PHPP data file and "a photographic record of all the recorded features of the building and of any identified defects" (clause 7.4.1).

Clause 7 does not use the words lintel, reveal or eaves, and gives no list of junction types to tick. What it requires is that your survey shows how the envelope elements meet.

### Good practice at each junction

How you show those relationships is for you and your scheme. These habits serve item h), but none is a named PAS requirement:
- openings: where the frame sits in the wall depth, the reveal depth and finish, and the lintel type where visible (RdSAP wall thickness "can be measured at door or window reveals", convention 2.22);
- wall to floor: the floor construction at the perimeter and how the internal floor level relates to the ground outside;
- wall to roof: whether loft insulation reaches the wall head, where it is safe to look;
- existing insulation: where any cavity fill or wall insulation stops (item e), and party walls and adjoining buildings (item a);
- penetrations such as meter boxes (Annex D, D.1);
- a labelled photograph of each.

### Condensation and mould at cold spots

A bridge lowers the internal surface temperature (clause 3.36), so mould at a reveal or a wall-floor corner is evidence worth recording precisely. Your duty is "the location and severity" (clause 7.3.2 d). The notes to item d) add:
- "A small amount of condensation in bathrooms around sinks or showers, or around the edge of windows in extreme weather conditions, is normal" (NOTE 2);
- guidance for diagnosing dampness "is given in BS 5250:2021, Annex C" (NOTE 4), which we do not have and do not teach.

The government's damp and mould guidance for England says condensation damp forms on "colder parts of the buildings (for example window frames, corners and low points on walls behind sofas or wardrobes)", and that cold spots "can occur when there are gaps in insulation" (MHCLG, DHSC and UKHSA, Understanding and addressing the health risks of damp and mould in the home, updated 1 April 2026). It puts corners and window frames alongside walls behind furniture without saying why each is colder, so where mould sits does not, on its own, prove a thermal bridge. A sensible habit is to record room, wall, height and extent, the surrounding construction and the ventilation you found under item f), and to say plainly when the cause is not confirmed.

### Reporting it

Where you find condensation or mould, the report must place it in one of two categories: "defects that need to be repaired before any retrofit work can proceed" or "defects that are recommended but not an essential prerequisite to retrofit" (clause 7.4.2). Evidence of condensation or mould in your assessment also obliges the designer to confirm the adequacy of the existing ventilation and, if necessary, specify an upgrade under Annex C (clause 8.2.13 b; Annex C, C.2.1 b). MER-RA-002 covers the wider moisture duties.

### Recommending and specifying

No clause of the PAS forbids the assessor to recommend; clause 7.4.2 b) itself uses "recommended". The PAS gives design and specification to the Retrofit Designer (clause 8.2.8), and the improvement options evaluation and recommended package to the Retrofit Coordinator (clauses 8.1.1 and 8.1.4). A sensible habit is to describe each junction, classify any defect under clause 7.4.2, and leave the choice of detail to the designer.

## Section 4: How RdSAP 10 treats thermal bridging

### One factor from one table

"The thermal bridging factor, y, as defined in Appendix K is taken from Table 21" (RdSAP10 Specification v4.0, section 5.15). Table 21 gives y in W/m2K:
- age bands A to I: 0.15;
- age band J: 0.11;
- age bands K, L and M: 0.08;
- park homes: 0.15 in every age band.

y "is determined according to the age band of the main dwelling and applied to the all the exposed area including main dwelling, extensions, and non-separated conservatory" (section 5.15). The same text appears in version 3.0 (9 June 2025). Appendix K is SAP's; the specification's references to SAP mean SAP 10.2 (specification, Introduction).

### Where the age bands fall

Age band years differ by nation (specification Table 1), though Table 21 does not. Band J (0.11) is 2003 to 2006 in England and Wales and 2003 to 2007 in Scotland; band K (0.08) starts in 2007 and 2008 respectively. Northern Ireland has no band J: band I runs to 2006 and band K starts in 2007.

Two consequences follow:
- An extension does not get its own y. A 2016 extension (band L) on a 1930s house in England (band C) takes the main dwelling's value, 0.15, across its exposed area (Table 1; section 5.15). Its U-values, by contrast, are obtained separately for the main part and each extension (section 5.3).
- The age band you enter for the main dwelling sets y. For a conversion of an unheated building such as a barn, the conventions use the conversion date as the date of construction (convention 3.05b); for a conversion of a heated building or a subdivided house, outside Scotland, the original construction date applies (convention 3.05a).

### What RdSAP 10 does not do

Section 5.15 is the specification's only provision on thermal bridging, and Table 31 (data to be collected) has no item for junctions, psi values or y. So:
- nothing you observe at a junction changes the RdSAP result;
- there is no route to enter a calculated psi value or y, and departing from the specification "invalidates the calculation and therefore cannot be used for generating EPC" (specification, Introduction);
- nothing in section 5.15 links y to insulation added since construction; only the main dwelling's age band and park home status count.

The specification does not say whether y covers repeating bridges such as wall ties or joists. It says a U-value "is that of the whole element, including any added insulation" (section 5.3), that its roof U-values "take account of" joists (section 5.11, notes to Tables 16 and 18), and defers to SAP Appendix K for the definition of y, which this course does not reproduce.

The specification does not call the values conservative. BRE's technical paper says that for existing buildings, where junction details are unknown, it proposed to continue "a generic y-value approach based on the age of the property", and that "the y-values for existing buildings are intended to be averages (rather than pessimistic defaults used for new dwellings) since there is no alternative but to use them" (S10TP-05, section 2.1). The paper predates RdSAP 10 and refers to the RdSAP 2012 table and "in due course" its RdSAP 10 equivalent. An average means a particular dwelling's junctions may be better or worse.

### What this means for your assessment

PAS 2035:2023 notes that RdSAP "makes default assumptions about some aspects of existing dwellings", and that RdSAP data supporting a retrofit assessment should be reviewed carefully "and preferably exported to Full SAP software" (clause 4.3.2). The y-value is such a default, not a description of this dwelling's junctions. How a full SAP or PHPP model uses junction information is outside this course; ask whoever runs the model.

## Key Takeaways
- PAS 2035:2023 defines a thermal bridge by what happens to the insulation layer, giving locally higher heat loss and a colder inside surface (clause 3.36).
- Clause 7.3.2 h) requires a measured survey showing how envelope elements relate at corners, junctions and within openings (window position is the PAS's example, NOTE 9), with the photographic record of clause 7.4.1.
- Recording lintels, reveals and eaves is good practice under item h), not a named PAS item; thermography is not an assessment requirement.
- Record condensation and mould by location and severity (clause 7.3.2 d) and report them in the two categories of clause 7.4.2; where mould sits does not prove a thermal bridge.
- The Retrofit Designer must detail every affected corner, junction and edge, using good-practice details or details shown to have an fRsi of not less than 0.75 (clauses 8.2.15 and 8.2.16).
- RdSAP 10 takes y from Table 21 by the main dwelling's age band only: 0.15 for bands A to I, 0.11 for J, 0.08 for K to M, and 0.15 for park homes (specification section 5.15).
- RdSAP 10 has no psi or y input, so junction observations do not change its result; BRE says existing-dwelling y-values are intended to be averages.
- The PAS gives design and specification to the designer (clause 8.2.8) but does not forbid the assessor to recommend; clause 7.4.2 b) itself uses "recommended".

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Under the RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0, what determines the thermal bridging factor y for a dwelling that is not a park home?

A) The wall construction type and the age band of each building part
B) Calculated psi values for the junctions the assessor has surveyed
C) The age band of the main dwelling, through Table 21
D) The thickness of insulation added since construction

**Correct answer: C.** Section 5.15 takes y from Table 21 according to "the age band of the main dwelling"; construction type, psi values and added insulation play no part.

---

**Q2.** A 1930s semi-detached house in England (age band C) has a rear extension built in 2016 (age band L). What y does RdSAP 10 apply to the extension's exposed area?

A) 0.15 W/m2K
B) 0.08 W/m2K
C) 0.11 W/m2K
D) None, because the extension was built to modern standards

**Correct answer: A.** y follows the main dwelling's age band, here C, giving 0.15, applied to all exposed area including extensions (specification section 5.15 and Table 21).

---

**Q3.** Why does PAS 2035:2023 single out the position of a window within a wall?

A) It sets the dwelling's age band in RdSAP
B) It changes the RdSAP y-value for that window
C) The assessor must calculate a psi value for each reveal
D) It governs the thermal bridging around the window and affects a retrofit design that includes wall insulation

**Correct answer: D.** Clause 7.3.2 h), NOTE 9, gives exactly that reason; RdSAP's y depends only on the main dwelling's age band (specification section 5.15), and clause 7 asks for no psi calculation.

---

**Q4.** In a ground-floor bedroom you find black mould along the junction of the external wall and the floor, and on the soffit of a window lintel. What does PAS 2035:2023 require of your assessment?

A) Diagnose thermal bridging as the cause and enter a calculated psi value in RdSAP
B) Identify the location and severity, photograph it, and report it in one of the two categories of clause 7.4.2
C) Nothing, because a small amount of condensation around windows is normal
D) Specify insulated reveal and perimeter details for the designer to use

**Correct answer: B.** Clauses 7.3.2 d), 7.4.1 and 7.4.2 require location, severity, photographs and the two categories; NOTE 2 covers only small amounts of condensation in bathrooms or at window edges in extreme weather, and specification is the designer's (clause 8.2.8).

---

**Q5.** Which statement about recommendations and junction details matches PAS 2035:2023?

A) Clause 7 forbids the Retrofit Assessor to make any recommendation
B) The Retrofit Assessor prepares the junction construction details required by clause 8.2.15
C) The assessor's report sorts defects, condensation and mould into those to repair before any retrofit work and those "recommended but not an essential prerequisite", while design and specification are the Retrofit Designer's
D) The Retrofit Installer writes the junction details during the pre-installation inspection

**Correct answer: C.** Clause 7.4.2 sets the two categories and clause 8.2.8 gives design and specification to the designer; no clause bans assessor recommendations, and PAS 2030:2023 clause 5.2.3 has the installer check the design, not write it.
