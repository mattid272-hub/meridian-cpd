# EPC Reform and the Future Home Buying Process: What Assessors Must Know
**Course ID:** MER-ALL-015 | **CPD Hours:** 1 | **Published:** October 2026

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- State when an EPC must be commissioned, obtained and given to a buyer or tenant, and when its rating must appear in adverts, under the 2012 Regulations as they stand today.
- Explain what the January 2026 partial government response on the Energy Performance of Buildings regime decided on metrics, validity and the marketing trigger, and why none of it is yet law.
- Describe what the June 2026 home buying and selling reform roadmap commits to on sales packs, its legislative route, and where the EPC appears in it.
- Identify the current legal basis for estate agents' material information duties, and why the old Trading Standards Parts A, B and C guidance should not be relied on.
- Separate the Home Energy Model and RdSAP 10 stream from both reforms, and advise clients without presenting proposals as requirements.

## Introduction

Three separate government work streams touch the EPC on sale and letting, and they are easily confused. The first is reform of the Energy Performance of Buildings (EPB) regime, led by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG) with the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ), covering what reformed EPCs will measure and when they will be required (EPB partial government response, paragraph 1). The second is MHCLG's home buying and selling reform, which aims to make sellers prepare a "sales pack" before listing (Home buying and selling reform roadmap, chapter 4). The third is DESNZ's Home Energy Model (HEM), which is to replace SAP and RdSAP as the calculation behind new EPCs (DESNZ HEM EPC consultation).

This course covers England and Wales. The 2012 Regulations extend to England and Wales (SI 2012/3118, regulation 7, extent E+W), and the EPB reform and HEM consultation both apply to England and Wales. The roadmap expects most of its measures to apply in England, Wales and Northern Ireland but not Scotland, with final extent settled through the legislative process (roadmap, chapter 1). Scotland has its own Energy Performance of Buildings (Scotland) Regulations 2025, coming fully into force on 31 October 2026 (SSI 2025/417, regulation 1(2) and (4)); Northern Ireland also has its own regulations. Neither is covered here.

Each point below is labelled as law in force, intention, consultation or commitment to legislate. No regulations amending the 2012 Regulations to deliver the EPB reform, made or in draft, appear on legislation.gov.uk, and sales pack legislation is promised only "when parliamentary time allows". This course is correct to 14 October 2026.

This edition replaces the September 2026 edition. That edition presented intentions as law: it said a sales pack and an EPC "lodged and verified" before listing were confirmed requirements, when the first is a commitment to legislate in future and the second an intention from a different reform, with regulation 7 unchanged. It also said EPC validity was undecided (in January 2026 the government said it intends to keep ten years), relied on the revoked Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 and the old Parts A, B and C guidance, and described BASPI data fields and portal data feeds as confirmed, which no government source I read supports.

## Section 1: The Law Today on Sale and Letting

**In short:** You must make sure the EPC data is on the register before you give it to the person who asked for it, and buyers and tenants can already enforce your duty of reasonable care and skill while the EPC is valid. Where there is no valid EPC, the seller or prospective landlord must secure that one is commissioned before marketing, then use all reasonable efforts to obtain it within 7 days, plus a further 21 days if that proves impossible.

The duties are in Part 2 of the Energy Performance of Buildings (England and Wales) Regulations 2012 (SI 2012/3118). On legislation.gov.uk the regulations below show "no known outstanding effects", and the latest amending instruments listed date from 2024.

### Commissioning before marketing

Regulation 7 applies where a building is to be sold or rented out and has no valid EPC. Before the building is put on the market, the relevant person must secure that an EPC is "commissioned" (regulation 7(2)). The relevant person is the seller, or for a letting the prospective landlord (regulation 2(1)). Anyone acting on their behalf, usually the agent, must be satisfied that an EPC has been commissioned before marketing (regulation 7(3)).

Both trigger words are defined. An EPC is commissioned when a request is "properly addressed" to an energy assessor accredited for that category of building, in the usual form and with the usual information and payment, or an undertaking to pay (regulation 7(6)(d)). A building is put on the market when the fact that it is or may become available for sale or rent is, with the intention of marketing the building, first made public in England and Wales by or on behalf of the relevant person (regulation 7(6)(b)).

### Obtaining within 7 days plus 21 days

Once the building is on the market, the relevant person and anyone acting for them must use all reasonable efforts to secure that a valid EPC is obtained within 7 days (regulation 7(4)), with a further 21 days if that proves impossible (regulation 7(5)). So the law today allows marketing to start with an EPC commissioned but not yet produced. The government itself describes the current rules as allowing "up to 28 days following marketing" (EPB partial government response, paragraph 47).

### Giving the EPC, and the rating in adverts

The relevant person must make a valid EPC available free of charge to any prospective buyer or tenant at the earliest opportunity, and no later than when written information is first provided on request or the person views the building (regulation 6(2)), and must make sure the eventual buyer or tenant is given one (regulation 6(5)). Where a building with a valid EPC is offered for sale or rent, the energy performance rating must be stated in any advertisement in commercial media (regulation 11(2)); for a dwelling that rating runs from A to G (regulation 11(3)).

### Validity and enforcement

An EPC is valid only if it was entered on the register no more than 10 years before the date it is made available and no later EPC for the building has since been entered (regulation 9(2)). Every local weights and measures authority is an enforcement authority (regulation 34(1)). For a dwelling, the penalty charge for breaching regulations 6(2), 6(5) or 7(2) to 7(5) is £200 (regulation 38(1)(a)(i)), and £200 for breaching regulation 11(2) (regulation 38(1)(e)).

### Your own duties

You must make sure the data used to produce the EPC is entered on the register before you give it to the person who asked for it (regulation 27(2)). You must carry out energy assessments with reasonable care and skill, and for an EPC that duty can be enforced by the relevant person and by any prospective or actual buyer or tenant during the certificate's validity (regulation 24(1) and (2)). That reliance exists now, without any reform.

## Section 2: EPB Reform: Decisions, Intentions and Timetable

**In short:** Treat everything here as government intention with no made or draft instrument behind it: four headline metrics, ten-year validity kept for reformed and existing EPCs, and an EPC required at the point of marketing. Until amending regulations are made, regulation 7 and its 7 plus 21 days remain the law. The launch has moved to the second half of 2027, and no published launch date was found, so check the consultation page and ask your scheme.

The consultation "Reforms to the Energy Performance of Buildings regime" ran from 4 December 2024 to 26 February 2025, from MHCLG and DESNZ, applying to England and Wales. A partial government response was added on 21 January 2026 and an outcome update on 9 March 2026 (gov.uk consultation page). Everything in this section is a stated government intention with no made or draft instrument behind it.

### What reformed EPCs will measure

The single cost metric on domestic EPCs is to be replaced by four headline metrics: energy cost, fabric performance, heating system and smart readiness (partial response, paragraph 25). A secondary energy demand metric based on delivered energy will be added and a secondary carbon metric kept (paragraph 30). The legacy Energy Efficiency Rating will be retained for comparison with current EPCs and for existing regulatory compliance "until it is no longer required" (paragraph 32).

### Validity stays at ten years

The government intends to keep the ten-year validity period for reformed EPCs, and existing EPCs keep their ten-year validity (paragraph 64). Among respondents who wanted a shorter period, the preferred option was five years (paragraph 37); that was not adopted. Homeowners can still commission an EPC voluntarily, for example to show the effect of upgrades (paragraph 64). Whether a new EPC is needed when one expires in a private rented building, and whether one must be held throughout a private tenancy, are still open (paragraphs 65 and 72).

### The marketing trigger

The consultation asked about requiring a valid EPC before a property is marketed, replacing the current window of up to 28 days (paragraph 47). The government intends to update regulations so that an EPC is required "at the point of marketing a property" rather than the point of sale or rent (paragraph 67). This intention comes from the EPB reform, not the home buying roadmap. Until amending regulations are made, regulation 7 and its 7 plus 21 days remain the law.

### Scope changes and open questions

The government also intends to require a valid EPC for a whole house in multiple occupation when a single room is let, to include short-term rental properties irrespective of who pays the bills, and to remove heritage exemptions (paragraph 68). A further response is promised in 2026 on Display Energy Certificates, EPC and DEC data, managing EPC quality and air conditioning reports (paragraph 72).

### Timetable

The response said that, subject to parliamentary approvals, the government intended to bring forward regulations in 2026 (paragraph 1), and that it was working to deliver new EPCs from October 2026 (paragraph 74). The 9 March 2026 update moved the launch "to the second half of 2027", with a new launch date and shared implementation plan to be agreed with industry and the devolved administrations by the summer (consultation page). The page has not been updated since, and I found no published launch date, implementation plan or final response. Check that page, and ask your accreditation scheme.

## Section 3: The Home Buying and Selling Reform Roadmap

**In short:** Remember that requiring a sales pack before listing is a commitment to legislate when parliamentary time allows; it is not law. "EPC rating" is one item the government anticipates a pack will include, with details subject to change, and the roadmap does not say how EPC information would reach a pack or mention the new metrics or the HEM. Keep this separate from the EPB intention to require an EPC at the point of marketing.

MHCLG consulted from 6 October to 29 December 2025 on "Home buying and selling reform" and "Material information in property listings". Its response to both is the "Home buying and selling reform roadmap", added on 19 June 2026, covering reform "over the remainder of this parliament" (gov.uk consultation pages).

### The three phases

The roadmap's timeline has three phases (roadmap, chapter 1, "Timeline for reform"):
- Now (in 2026): non-statutory guidance on property listing information, a non-statutory Code of Practice for agents, and identifying sales pack information that can be provided voluntarily, upfront, immediately.
- Next (2027 to 2028): an advisory Charter for property professionals, a consultation on mandatory qualifications for agents, and considering which homeownership schemes should require digital logbooks and sales packs.
- Future (by end of Parliament): when parliamentary time allows, legislation to require sales packs prior to listing, including searches and a property condition report, and later binding conditional contracts once sales packs are embedded.

### What a sales pack would be

Sellers would keep overall responsibility for preparing the pack, with relevant professionals gathering and verifying information. Guidance will define what a standardised pack contains and when data needs refreshing, and the government will carry out a thorough impact assessment ahead of any legislation (roadmap, chapter 4, government response for questions 7 to 10).

### Where the EPC appears

Annex B lists information the government anticipates a sales pack will include. "EPC rating" is one item, alongside tenure, Council Tax band, title information, building safety information, searches, a property questionnaire, a property condition assessment report and a floor plan. Details "are subject to change and will be confirmed prior to any legislation" (roadmap, Annex B).

In the roadmap text I read, that is the only reference to the EPC. The roadmap does not say how EPC information would reach a pack, does not mention the new metrics or the HEM, and does not say whether sales packs or new-style EPCs come first.

### Status

The sales pack is a commitment to legislate in future; it is not law. It is also a different proposal from the EPB intention to require an EPC at the point of marketing, which would be delivered by updating regulations (EPB partial response, paragraph 67), whereas sales packs await legislation promised "when parliamentary time allows".

## Section 4: Material Information, the Home Energy Model and Your Practice

**In short:** Do not sell an EPC as "sales pack ready", and check your own scheme's indemnity terms. Agents' material information duties now rest on the DMCCA 2024, not the revoked 2008 Regulations behind the old Parts A, B and C guidance. RdSAP 10 applies now; a DESNZ consultation, with no outcome published, says the HEM will replace it when new EPCs launch in the second half of 2027, exact date not set.

### Material information law has changed

The Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 were revoked by section 251(1) of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCCA), in force from 6 April 2025 (DMCCA 2024, section 251, commencement note). Chapter 1 of Part 4, "protection from unfair trading", came into force the same day, except sections 232, 234 and 235 (SI 2025/272, regulation 2). A commercial practice involves a misleading omission if, among other things, it omits material information, meaning information the average consumer needs to take an informed transactional decision (DMCCA 2024, section 227(1) and (2)).

### The old Parts A, B and C guidance

National Trading Standards announced the publication of Parts B and C of its material information guidance on 30 November 2023 (National Trading Standards news item of that date). That guidance was framed around the Consumer Protection Regulations, the 2008 Regulations since revoked. The National Trading Standards material information page no longer sets it out; its body is now one sentence pointing to MHCLG's June 2026 reforms. MHCLG says "no sector-specific guidance has been produced since the DMCCA" came into effect (material information consultation). No source I read names a document that has already replaced it.

The roadmap commits MHCLG to publish non-statutory guidance on agents' existing DMCCA responsibilities, with a standardised form for gathering material information, "later this year" (roadmap, chapter 12 and Annex A). I could not confirm whether it had been published by 14 October 2026; check gov.uk. None of this alters the separate duty to state the EPC rating in adverts under regulation 11.

### The Home Energy Model is a separate stream

RdSAP is the UK approved methodology for assessing energy performance and generating EPCs for existing dwellings; the current specification is RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0, dated 7 June 2026 (RdSAP10 Specification v4.0, Introduction and version history).

DESNZ consulted from 21 January to 18 March 2026, for England and Wales, on using the HEM for EPCs. The consultation says that when new EPCs launch, domestic EPC assessments will be carried out using the HEM, which "will replace" SAP and RdSAP; an update dated 10 March 2026 moves the launch to the second half of 2027 (HEM EPC consultation document, chapter 1 and update note). No outcome has been published; the page says feedback is being analysed. The EPB partial response says the HEM consultation explores how new-style EPCs will be produced and how the four headline metrics will be banded (paragraph 73).

So: RdSAP 10 now; HEM-based EPCs from a launch in the second half of 2027, date not fixed; sales pack legislation planned by the end of this Parliament, when parliamentary time allows. No source I read links the last two.

### What this means for your practice

Buyers and tenants can already enforce your duty of care under regulation 24. Professional indemnity is a scheme matter: for example, the ecmk Quality Plan requires members to hold appropriate professional indemnity insurance, with minimum terms set by MHCLG in the Scheme Standards (ecmk Quality Plan ACC-001, section 6). Check your own scheme's terms.

Some sensible habits while the reforms are pending:
- If an agent tells a seller the law already requires the EPC in hand before listing, the accurate position is commissioning before marketing, then 7 plus 21 days to obtain it; an EPC at the point of marketing is an intention.
- Do not sell an EPC as "sales pack ready". No sales pack requirement exists, and future pack contents are to be set in guidance.
- Tell clients that the government intends existing EPCs to keep their ten-year validity.
- Watch the EPB consultation page and legislation.gov.uk, and ask your scheme about lodgement, audit or software changes.

## Key Takeaways

The points to carry away:
- Today an EPC must be commissioned before marketing and obtained within 7 days plus a further 21 days if needed (SI 2012/3118, regulation 7).
- The January 2026 partial response keeps ten-year validity for reformed and existing EPCs and plans four headline metrics: energy cost, fabric performance, heating system and smart readiness.
- An EPC at the point of marketing is an EPB reform intention (paragraph 67), not part of the home buying roadmap and not yet law.
- The launch of reformed EPCs has moved to the second half of 2027; EPC data and quality management await a further response.
- The roadmap commits to legislate, when parliamentary time allows, for sales packs before listing; "EPC rating" is one anticipated item.
- Material information duties now rest on the DMCCA 2024, in force from 6 April 2025; do not rely on the old Parts A, B and C guidance.
- RdSAP 10 remains the approved methodology for existing dwellings; DESNZ says the HEM will replace it when new EPCs launch.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Under regulation 7 as currently in force, what must happen before a dwelling with no valid EPC is put on the market for sale?

A) A valid EPC must already be lodged on the register
B) The EPC rating must be included in a sales pack
C) The seller must secure that an EPC is commissioned
D) Nothing, until an offer has been accepted

**Correct answer: C.** Regulation 7(2) requires an EPC to be commissioned before marketing, and regulation 7(4) and (5) then allow 7 days plus 21 days to obtain it; there is no sales pack requirement (SI 2012/3118, regulation 7).

---

**Q2.** What did the January 2026 partial government response say about EPC validity?

A) Ten-year validity will be kept for reformed EPCs, and existing EPCs keep theirs
B) Validity will be cut to five years for all new EPCs
C) Validity will end automatically when a home is sold
D) Validity will be decided in the home buying roadmap

**Correct answer: A.** Paragraph 64 states the intention to keep ten-year validity for reformed and existing EPCs; five years was only the preference of respondents who wanted a shorter period (partial response, paragraphs 37 and 64).

---

**Q3.** What is the status of a requirement for sellers to prepare a sales pack before listing, as at 14 October 2026?

A) In force since 19 June 2026
B) In force under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024
C) Made regulations due in force in the second half of 2027
D) A government commitment to legislate when parliamentary time allows

**Correct answer: D.** The roadmap places sales pack legislation in its "Future (by end of Parliament)" phase, "when parliamentary time allows", after an impact assessment (roadmap, chapters 1 and 4).

---

**Q4.** What is the current legal basis for an estate agent's duty not to omit material information?

A) The Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 and the Parts A, B and C guidance
B) Chapter 1 of Part 4 of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024
C) Regulation 11 of the Energy Performance of Buildings (England and Wales) Regulations 2012
D) Annex B of the home buying and selling reform roadmap

**Correct answer: B.** Section 227 of the DMCCA 2024 defines misleading omissions and material information, and section 251(1) revoked the 2008 Regulations from 6 April 2025 (SI 2025/272, regulation 2); the roadmap is policy, not law.

---

**Q5.** Which statement about the Home Energy Model is correct as at October 2026?

A) HEM-based EPCs have been produced since October 2026
B) The roadmap makes HEM-based EPCs a condition of sales packs
C) RdSAP 10 remains the approved methodology for existing dwellings, and HEM will replace it when new EPCs launch
D) The HEM removes the need for EPCs on sale and letting

**Correct answer: C.** RdSAP is the approved methodology for existing dwellings (RdSAP10 Specification v4.0, Introduction), and DESNZ says HEM will replace it when new EPCs launch, now the second half of 2027 (HEM EPC consultation, update of 10 March 2026).
