# Heating Systems Secondary: Storage Heaters, Direct Electric and Room Heaters in RdSAP 10

## Learning Objectives
- Use the habitable and heated habitable room counts to separate main from secondary heating (conventions 2.04a and 2.04b).
- Enter storage heaters correctly, including mixed types, high heat retention models and single rate meters (conventions 4.02, 4.09 and 4.20).
- Record electric underfloor mats, infra-red heaters, electric CPSUs and electric combi boilers (conventions 4.14, 4.15 and 4.22).
- Select the secondary heater and its fuel, including open fires and dwellings heated only by room heaters (conventions 4.09 and 5.01 to 5.03).
- Record the electricity meter and explain how it sets the tariff and when Addendum 6 is needed (convention 9.13, specification section 12).

## Introduction

This October 2026 revision replaces the March 2026 edition. It has been rebuilt from the Conventions (v12.2) for RdSAP 10, applicable from 6 August 2026, and the RdSAP10 Specification version 4.0 (7 June 2026). Each rule is followed by the convention or specification section it comes from, so you can check it. Where the two documents conflict, the conventions state that the convention takes precedence.

Some things you might expect are deliberately missing. The specification does not reproduce the SAP 10.2 tables of heating system types, efficiencies and controls. For those it points to the entries marked "rd" in SAP 10.2 Tables 4a, 4b and 4e (specification sections 10.1 and 10.3). This course therefore gives no efficiency or responsiveness figures and does not describe controls or software screens.

Four v12.2 changes dated 13 July 2026 are covered in this course. Convention 4.09 now covers a high heat retention storage heater that is not in the database. Convention 4.20 now refers to the sole source of heat, where v12.1 said the primary source. Convention 4.22 on electric combi boilers is new. Convention 4.03 now covers hot water systems as well as heating.

## Section 1: Main or Secondary, Room Counts and Missing Systems

**In short:** When counting habitable rooms, leave out rooms used solely as a kitchen, utility rooms, bathrooms, hallways, stairs, landings and rooms without access to natural daylight, among others. Main systems cannot be room heaters unless the heating consists solely of room heaters. A system fitted but not working, or condemned, still counts when deciding main and secondary heating; one present but not accessible, with no documentary evidence, means the assessment cannot be completed until access is provided, except for community heating.

### Habitable rooms

Habitable rooms include any living room, sitting room, dining room, kitchen/diner, bedroom, study and similar. A non-separated conservatory adds to the count if it has an internal quality door between it and the dwelling, but one open to the rest of the dwelling is not counted as a separate room (convention 2.04a). The same convention excludes the following from the count:
- any room used solely as a kitchen, and any utility room, bathroom, cloakroom, or en-suite bathroom, shower room or toilet
- any hallway, stairs or landing
- any room without access to natural daylight

So a storage heater in a hallway, or a fixed heater in a bathroom, is in a non-habitable room.

### Heated habitable rooms

The heated habitable room count includes all habitable rooms heated by the main heating system or systems, or by fixed secondary heating (convention 2.04b). The specification describes a heated habitable room as one with a fixed heat emitter in the room, and notes that the count is used to define which heating is main and which is secondary in inadequately heated dwellings (specification Table 31, item 1-7). Bedrooms with only open fireplaces are disregarded from the count when identifying the heating systems (convention 2.04b).

The specification leaves the treatment of unheated habitable rooms to SAP 10.2 Appendix A (specification section 10.1), so ask your scheme if you need that detail.

### What can be a main system

The main heating system is the one that heats the largest proportion of the dwelling, and it is not usually based on individual room heaters unless only room heaters are present (specification section 10.1). RdSAP allows two main systems, but main systems 1 and 2 cannot be room heaters except where the dwelling's heating consists solely of room heaters (convention 4.09, specification section 10.2). If main system 1 heats all habitable rooms there is no main system 2, unless it serves hot water only (specification section 10.2).

The secondary heating system is a room heater type (specification section 10.4), and a second main system is not to be confused with a secondary heater (convention 4.09). So a gas fire in a house with a boiler is a candidate for secondary heating, not main system 2.

### Not working, not installed, not accessible

Convention 4.03 separates three situations:
- Fitted but not working, or condemned: the system should still be taken into account when deciding the main and secondary heating.
- Not fitted or installed: enter no heating system, or do another survey when the system is installed.
- Present but not accessible for inspection, with no documentary evidence: the assessment cannot be completed until access is provided (community heating excluded).

Where no space heating system is present, the calculation is done for portable electric heaters with no controls in all habitable rooms (specification section 10.1).

## Section 2: Storage Heaters and Other Electric Main Heating

**In short:** Record a storage heater as high heat retention only if you locate its brand name and model in the database; one identified as high heat retention but not in the PCDB is entered as fan-assisted. More than one type means two main systems, with system 1 always heating the living area. Storage heaters as main heating on a single rate meter are entered as panel heaters with Addendum 6. Treat electric underfloor mats and infra-red heaters as panel heaters.

### Types, and the high heat retention rule

Convention 4.09 names four types of storage heater: old large-volume, fan-assisted, integrated storage/direct acting, and high heat retention. Neither document describes how to tell them apart on site, so use your scheme's guidance for identification.

One rule is stated outright. A storage heater can be classified as high heat retention only if the brand name and model is located in the database. If you identify a high heat retention storage heater that is not in the Product Characteristics Database (PCDB), enter it as a fan-assisted storage heater (convention 4.09).

The specification asks for brand name, model and model qualifier whenever possible for storage heaters and, for high heat retention types only, the index number of each heater (specification Table 31, item 7-3).

### More than one type of storage heater

If there is more than one type of storage heater, treat them as two main systems. Then, if either main system has more than one type, choose the most prevalent (convention 4.09). The two-system rules in the same convention then apply:
- System 1 always heats the living area.
- When both systems heat the living area, system 1 is the one that heats the most habitable rooms.
- When both heat the same number of habitable rooms, system 1 is the one that provides water heating.
- When neither or both heat water, system 1 is the cheapest to run, taken as the fuel cost from specification Table 32 divided by the efficiency of the system.

Where the systems serve different spaces, record the heating proportion from the floor area served by each (convention 4.09). The specification asks for the percentage of heated floor area served by the second system, estimated to the nearest 10% (specification Table 31, item 7-5). When there are two main systems and a heating system upgrade is recommended, include Addendum 9 (convention 4.09).

The convention does not say how "most prevalent" is judged, or how three or four types are divided between two systems. For a dwelling that mixed, check with your scheme.

### Worked example: a flat with mixed storage heaters

A two-bedroom flat with a separate kitchen has a dual meter and storage heaters in the living room and both bedrooms. The living room and bedroom 1 heaters are a model you locate in the PCDB as high heat retention. The bedroom 2 heater is old large-volume.
- There is more than one type, so there are two main systems (convention 4.09).
- System 1 always heats the living area, so the high heat retention heaters are main system 1, with the index number of each heater recorded (convention 4.09, specification Table 31, item 7-3).
- The old large-volume heater is main system 2. Its proportion is the share of heated floor area it serves, to the nearest 10% (convention 4.09, specification Table 31, item 7-5).

If that model could not be found in the PCDB, the living room and bedroom 1 heaters would be entered as fan-assisted storage heaters (convention 4.09). There would still be two types, so still two main systems.

### Non-habitable rooms and single rate meters

Where the sole source of heat is a storage heater in a non-habitable room, include it as main heating and count it as one heated habitable room (convention 4.20). A flat whose only heater is a storage heater in the hallway is the obvious case.

If storage heaters are present as main heating but there is only a single rate meter, enter them as panel heaters and include Addendum 6 (convention 4.02, specification section 12).

### Underfloor mats, infra-red heaters and electric combi boilers

Treat electric underfloor heating mats and infra-red heaters as electric panel heaters (convention 4.14). Specification section 12 separately lists underfloor heating (index numbers 421 or 422, but not 424) among the systems that need an off-peak tariff. The sources do not say what those numbers cover, so for electric underfloor heating that is not plainly a mat, check with your scheme.

Space heating from an electric combi boiler is recorded manually as a standard boiler, direct acting, with electricity as the fuel (convention 4.22). For its hot water, specify the water heater as instantaneous at point of use (convention 6.08).

The sources do not list the other direct electric heating types, which sit in SAP 10.2 Table 4a (specification section 10.1), so check your scheme's guidance for anything not covered here.

## Section 3: Secondary Heating, Open Fires and Room Heaters

**In short:** Include secondary heating wherever a fixed emitter is present, even if the main system heats every room: a fixed heater in a non-habitable room counts, as does a plug-in electric focal point fire. Ask your scheme about borderline appliances. Where there are several, choose the one heating the most habitable rooms, then cheapest fuel, then lowest efficiency. Consider an open fire if the fireplace can support one, with a grate suitable for holding fuel, even with no fuel present.

### When to include secondary heating

Include secondary heating if a fixed emitter is present, regardless of whether the main system or systems heat all rooms (convention 5.01). The same convention adds three points:
- Electric focal point fires are included even if not wired by a fixed spur.
- A fixed heater in a non-habitable room is still counted as a secondary heater (also specification section 10.4).
- A solid fuel or oil range cooker with no boiler is treated as a closed room heater.

Apart from the focal point fire rule, the sources do not define "fixed" further, so for a borderline appliance ask your scheme. If there is no secondary heating system the entry is none (specification Table 31, item 7-8).

### Choosing between several secondary heaters

Where there is more than one, select one in the order given by convention 5.01:
- Select the device that heats the greatest number of habitable rooms.
- If that is the same, choose the cheapest fuel. The conventions tell you to use specification Table 32 for fuel prices.
- If the fuel is the same, select the device with the lowest efficiency.

### Worked example: three candidates

A house has a mains gas boiler with radiators in every room and a single rate electricity meter. The lounge has a mains gas fire, the dining room a plug-in electric focal point fire, and the bathroom a fixed electric wall heater.
- All three are candidates. The boiler heating every room does not rule them out, the focal point fire needs no fixed spur, and the bathroom heater counts although the room is non-habitable (convention 5.01).
- Habitable rooms heated: each fire heats one and the bathroom heater heats none, because a bathroom is not a habitable room (convention 2.04a). The bathroom heater drops out and the two fires tie.
- Cheapest fuel: the unit price is 3.48 p/kWh for mains gas and 13.19 p/kWh for standard tariff electricity (specification Table 32). The gas fire is the secondary heater.

Had two mains gas fires of different kinds tied, the one with the lower efficiency would be chosen (convention 5.01). Those efficiencies are in SAP 10.2 Table 4a, which the specification does not reproduce (specification Table 31, item 7-8).

### Open fires

An open fire is considered in the heating assessment if the fireplace is capable of supporting an open fire, which includes having a grate suitable for holding fuel, even if no fuel is present (convention 5.02). The number of open fireplaces is also used in the calculation as the number of open chimneys for ventilation (convention 5.02).

### Fuel for solid fuel fires and room heaters

Convention 5.03 sets the fuel to record:
- If the appliance can burn only one fuel, specify that fuel. This includes exempted appliances burning wood in Smoke Control Areas.
- Otherwise, in a smoke control area, an open fire is recorded with smokeless fuel and a closed heater with anthracite.
- Otherwise, outside a smoke control area, an open fire is recorded as dual fuel and a closed heater with wood logs if capable, or anthracite if not.

Neither document says how to establish whether an address is in a smoke control area, so follow your scheme's guidance.

### Dwellings heated only by room heaters

When the heating consists solely of room heaters, they can be main systems (convention 4.09). If there is more than one type, for example a gas fire and an electric fire, the convention gives two outcomes:
- Both heat habitable rooms: apply the two main system rules from Section 2, so the one heating the living area is system 1.
- One heats a habitable room and the other is in a non-habitable room: treat them as main and secondary heating respectively.

If there are more than two main heating systems, use the same rules to settle systems 1 and 2 and disregard the third (convention 4.09).

## Section 4: Meters and Tariffs

**In short:** Two single-reading meters for on-peak and off-peak are a dual meter, and unless you are choosing a particular tariff on additional information such as a recent bill, treat a tele-switch as single and a time-switch as dual. With storage heaters as main heating or a dual immersion on a single meter, enter panel heaters or a single immersion with Addendum 6. An electric CPSU on a single tariff is a direct-acting electric boiler; ask your scheme about Addendum 6.

### Recording the meter

Older properties may have two single-reading meters recording on-peak and off-peak readings, which is recorded as a dual meter (convention 9.13). If you are choosing a particular tariff, gather additional information such as a recent electricity bill. Otherwise, if a tele-switch is present treat the meter as single, and if a time-switch is present treat it as dual (convention 9.13).

The specification records the meter as single, dual (two separate readings), dual 18-hour, dual 24-hour, or unknown if inaccessible (specification section 12).

Smart meters communicate wirelessly with the meter network and may have a variable tariff. They do not have to be functioning at the time of the visit, but the evidence must be either a smart meter unit or a portable display that shows meter readings (convention 9.13). Their presence does not affect the calculation (specification Table 31, item 1-17). The conventions give no separate rule for deciding whether a smart meter is single or dual, so follow your scheme's guidance.

### From meter to tariff

Specification section 12 links the meter to the tariff used in the calculation:
- Single meter: the standard tariff.
- Dual 18-hour or dual 24-hour meter: the 18-hour or 24-hour tariff.
- Any other dual meter where the main heating is an electric CPSU: the 10-hour tariff.
- Otherwise, where either main system is electric storage heaters, an electric dry core or water storage boiler, or electric underfloor heating (index numbers 421 or 422): the 7-hour tariff.
- Failing that, where either main system is a direct-acting electric boiler, a heat pump or electric room heaters: the 10-hour tariff.
- If none of these applies, including where electric heaters are assumed because there is no heating system: the 7-hour tariff.

If the meter is unknown, it is treated as single except where the main heating or water heating is one of the off-peak systems below, or the main heating is a ground source or water source heat pump (specification section 12).

### Off-peak systems on a single meter

The specification lists the systems that need an off-peak tariff: electric storage heaters, the underfloor heating noted in Section 2, electric dry core or water storage boilers, electric CPSUs and dual electric immersions. If the meter is single when any of these is present, the heating is entered as panel heaters and/or the immersion as single, with Addendum 6, which explains on the EPC that a single-rate appliance has been used for the assessment (specification section 12 and Table 30). The conventions say the same for storage heaters (convention 4.02) and for a dual immersion, entered as a single immersion with Addendum 6 (convention 6.03).

### Electric CPSU: where the documents differ

Convention 4.15 says an electric CPSU uses a 10-hour or 18-hour tariff. On a 7-hour tariff, treat it as a water storage boiler. On a single tariff, record it as a direct-acting electric boiler. Specification section 12 would instead enter panel heaters for a CPSU on a single meter. The convention takes precedence, so record a direct-acting electric boiler. Convention 4.15 does not mention Addendum 6 here, so ask your scheme whether to include it.

## Key Takeaways
- Main systems cannot be room heaters unless the heating consists solely of room heaters (convention 4.09).
- A storage heater is high heat retention only if its brand name and model is located in the database. One identified as high heat retention but not in the PCDB is entered as fan-assisted. More than one type means two main systems (convention 4.09).
- Storage heaters on a single rate meter are entered as panel heaters, and a dual immersion as a single immersion, each with Addendum 6 (conventions 4.02 and 6.03).
- A storage heater in a non-habitable room that is the sole source of heat is main heating and counts as one heated habitable room (convention 4.20).
- Secondary heating is included wherever a fixed emitter is present. Choose between several by habitable rooms heated, then cheapest fuel, then lowest efficiency (convention 5.01).
- Unless you are choosing a particular tariff on additional information such as a recent bill, a tele-switch is treated as a single meter and a time-switch as dual (convention 9.13).

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** A flat with a dual meter has storage heaters that appear to be high heat retention, but you cannot locate the brand name and model in the PCDB. How are they entered?

A) As high heat retention storage heaters
B) As old large-volume storage heaters
C) As fan-assisted storage heaters
D) As panel heaters with Addendum 6

**Correct answer: C.** Convention 4.09 allows high heat retention only if the brand name and model is located in the database, and says one that is not in the PCDB is entered as a fan-assisted storage heater.

---

**Q2.** Storage heaters are the main heating in a bungalow, but there is only a single rate electricity meter. What is the correct entry?

A) Panel heaters, with Addendum 6 included
B) Storage heaters on the 7-hour tariff
C) Storage heaters on the standard tariff, with no addendum
D) Storage heaters, with the meter recorded as unknown

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 4.02 says storage heaters present as main heating with only a single rate meter are entered as panel heaters with Addendum 6.

---

**Q3.** A boiler and radiators heat every room of a house. The only other heater is a fixed electric heater in the bathroom. What is recorded for secondary heating?

A) None, because the main system heats all rooms
B) None, because a bathroom is not a habitable room
C) Portable electric heaters
D) The fixed bathroom heater

**Correct answer: D.** Convention 5.01 includes a fixed emitter regardless of whether the main system heats all rooms, and says a fixed heater in a non-habitable room is still counted as a secondary heater.

---

**Q4.** You have no tariff information such as a recent bill, and the electricity meter has a time-switch. How is the meter treated?

A) As single
B) As dual
C) As unknown
D) As dual 24-hour

**Correct answer: B.** Convention 9.13 says that, unless a particular tariff is chosen on additional information, a tele-switch is treated as single and a time-switch as dual.

---

**Q5.** The secondary heater is a closed solid fuel room heater that can burn wood or mineral fuel. The dwelling is not in a smoke control area. Which fuel is recorded?

A) Anthracite
B) Dual fuel
C) Wood logs
D) Smokeless fuel

**Correct answer: C.** Convention 5.03 says that outside a smoke control area a closed heater is recorded with wood logs if capable, otherwise anthracite, and that dual fuel is the entry for an open fire.
