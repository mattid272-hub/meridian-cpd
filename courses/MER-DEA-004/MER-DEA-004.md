# Room in Roof: Type 1 and Type 2 in RdSAP 10
**Course ID:** MER-DEA-004 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** March 2026, revised October 2026 (Conventions v12.2)

## Learning Objectives
- Decide whether a space in the roof is a roof room, a separate storey, a mansard storey or a storey wholly within the roof (conventions 2.06, 2.07 and 2.08).
- Tell Type 1 from Type 2 by the common wall, and apply the 1.8 m rule to a Type 2 common wall.
- Decide when the detailed assessment must be used, and list the measurements each method needs.
- Record gable types and dormers correctly, including the hipped roof, 25% connected gable and 20% dormer rules.
- Record roof room age band, insulation and U-values from evidence, and explain what the deletion of Appendix 4 means.

---

## Introduction

RdSAP 10 recognises two types of roof room and two ways of assessing each. The rules are in convention 2.06 of the Conventions (v12.2) for RdSAP 10, applicable from 6 August 2026, with illustrations in Appendix 2 and Appendix 3, and in sections 1.15, 3.9 and 3.10 of the RdSAP10 Specification of 9 June 2025. Where a convention conflicts with the specification, the convention takes precedence (Conventions v12.2, opening statement).

This course follows the decisions in site order: is it a roof room, which type, which method, what to measure, and how to record age band, insulation and U-values. Each statement cites its source. Where the sources are silent or disagree, the course says so and points you to your accreditation scheme.

Correction: the March 2026 edition of this course defined Type 1 and Type 2 by whether a separating ceiling exists between the roof room and the storey below. That is not the definition in the conventions or the specification. The types are defined by the common wall, as set out in Section 2.

Convention 2.06 was amended on 13 July 2026 for v12.2, and Appendix 4 was deleted.

## Section 1: Is it a roof room? Inclusion, separate storeys and special cases

### The inclusion test

A roof room is included in the assessment when it is accessed via a permanent fixed staircase such that one is able to walk downwards facing forwards (convention 2.06). A space that passes this test is still not a roof room if it fails the common wall test in Section 2. It is then a separate storey (convention 2.06).

### Mansard roofs

A storey with non-vertical walls of at least 70 degrees pitch is a separate storey and is not treated as roof rooms. Use alternative wall if appropriate (convention 2.07).

### A whole dwelling or building part within the roof

When the property, or a building part of it, is a single storey entirely located within a roof, convention 2.08 says to model it as:
- lowest occupied level
- timber frame construction of the appropriate age band
- room height entered as 2.2 m
- area and perimeter measured as for a normal storey
- roof entered as pitched roof.

Non-timber gables are treated as alternative wall. If there are two storeys within the roof, enter the lower storey as above and the upper storey as rooms in roof (convention 2.08).

### Building parts, vertical extensions and bungalows

A roof room belongs to the building part beneath it. Roof rooms are defined only when a building part consisting of normal storeys has been defined, and the procedure is applied to the main dwelling and separately to any extension with roof rooms (specification section 3.9). A roof room cannot be a vertical extension in its own right (convention 2.11). A roof room built later than the storeys below is therefore handled through its own age band (convention 3.13), not by creating a vertical extension.

The specification also says that a roof room built over a garage attached to the dwelling is treated as an extension (specification section 3.9). It does not explain how that extension is then modelled, so ask your scheme if you meet one.

A bungalow has all of its habitable accommodation at the ground floor only. Chalet bungalows and bungalows with rooms in the roof are treated as houses (convention 1.02b).

## Section 2: Type 1 and Type 2: the common wall and the 1.8 m rule

### The two types

The common wall is a vertical continuation of the external wall of the storey below (convention 2.06).

Type 1 is a roof room fully within the roof (convention 2.06). Appendix 2 calls it the true Room in Roof and shows two cases:
- a roof room with no common walls
- a roof room built into a roof space where the roof room stud walls are built inside the common walls, leaving the common walls outside the boundary of the accessible floor area.

Type 1 is always treated as a roof room regardless of the height of the stud walls, and the 1.8 m rule does not apply to it (Appendix 2; specification section 1.15).

Type 2 is a room in roof that is not fully within the roof, where an accessible common wall is part of the room in roof (convention 2.06).

Neither source uses a separating ceiling, the staircase arrangement or the use of the room to decide the type. The question is whether the external wall of the storey below continues above the roof room floor and is accessible as part of the room.

### Applying the 1.8 m rule

The common wall height is measured from the floor to the point where the roof plane intersects with the head of the common wall (Appendix 2; specification section 1.15.2). Gable ends and party walls are left out of the test (convention 2.06). Lengths are measured to 0.01 m or better (convention 2.02).

If the common wall is equal to or less than 1.8 m high for 50% or more of its length, the space is a roof room. Otherwise it is a separate storey (convention 2.06).

Worked example. An upper floor has 18.00 m of accessible common wall, excluding the gables. 12.00 m of it is 1.40 m high and 6.00 m is 2.10 m high. 12.00 divided by 18.00 is 67%, so the wall is below 1.8 m for more than half its length and this is a Type 2 roof room (convention 2.06).

### Where the sources do not agree

The body of convention 2.06 says "equal to or less than 1.8 m" for "50% or more". Appendix 2 describes Type 2 as "less than 1.8 m for at least 50%" and a separate storey as "1.8 m or more for at least 50%", and specification section 1.15.2 also says "less than 1.8 m". A common wall of exactly 1.80 m is therefore a roof room under the body of the convention but a separate storey under its own appendix. A wall split exactly half and half is a roof room under the body, but meets both Appendix 2 descriptions. The conventions do not say which governs. Record the measurements and ask your accreditation scheme.

## Section 3: Simplified or detailed: methods, measurements, gables and dormers

### Which method

There are two assessment methods for both types (convention 2.06). If any element of the roof room (ceiling, slope, stud wall, gable or common wall) has known insulation levels, the detailed method must be used and all elements must be measured (convention 2.06). The simplified method is therefore for a roof room where no element has known insulation.

The specification words the trigger more narrowly, as elements with differing insulation levels where each U-value is known (specification section 3.10). Follow the convention.

### Dimensions

The floor area of rooms in roof is always measured internally, whatever basis is used for other storeys (specification section 3.1). Convention 2.18 says not to mix internal and external measurements, and that if a roof room is included it is likely that internal dimensions will be used throughout the dwelling.

### Simplified assessment

For Type 1, measure the roof room floor length and width and the length of the gable or gables (convention 2.06; specification section 3.9.1). Gable heights are not measured. The software assumes 2.45 m, which is a 2.2 m room height plus 0.25 m between the roof room and the storey below (specification section 3.9.1).

For Type 2, measure:
- the roof room floor length and width
- the length and height of each gable, with the height taken at the highest point
- the length and height of each accessible common wall (convention 2.06; specification section 3.9.2).

The software adds the 0.25 m. The assessor does not (specification section 3.9.2).

### Detailed assessment

Appendix 3 illustrates the parts of a roof room when detailed measurements are made. The specification names them as flat ceiling, sloping ceiling, stud wall and gable wall, and notes that instead of a stud wall and residual ceiling there can be a common wall (specification section 3.10, Figure 4). There can be up to two of each, and a U-value must be acquired for each non-zero heat loss area (specification section 3.10).

Convention 2.06 says that where more than two elements are present you merge the areas together "and x 1". It does not explain the entry further, so check with your scheme how a merged area should be entered.

### Dormers

In the simplified method there is no explicit allowance for dormer windows except to include them in the floor area of the roof rooms (convention 2.06).

In the detailed method, compare the floor area of the parts of the dormers protruding beyond the roof line with the floor area of the roof room. If it is less than 20%, measure the roof room elements as if the dormers were not there. Otherwise, total the vertical elements of all dormers in that building part and enter them as stud wall, and enter the flat ceiling elements as flat ceiling (convention 2.06).

### Gable types

For each roof room type the gable type must be recorded as one of four (convention 2.06):
- Exposed: open to external air.
- Party wall: adjacent to heated space, for example a neighbouring property where the space appears to be an original room in the roof or a verified loft conversion, or where dormers are visibly present from outside.
- Sheltered wall: adjacent to unheated space, for example a neighbouring property's loft space.
- Connected: adjacent to another building part of the same dwelling.

The type changes the U-value: 0.25 W/m2K for a party gable and zero for a connected gable, with a sheltering factor applied to a sheltered gable (specification Table 4).

For roof rooms with no exposed gable wall, such as under a hipped roof, assume the stud above the gable walls of the lower floors is the exposed gable wall and measure it in the same way (convention 2.06).

Where a connected gable is not connected for its full area, because of stepped arrangements or a smaller adjoining building part: if less than 25% of the total area is connected, disregard it and assume the gable is exposed, and if over 25% is connected, enter the gable as connected (convention 2.06). The convention does not say what to do at exactly 25%.

## Section 4: Age band, insulation evidence and U-values

### Age band and defaults

The age band for a roof room is the same as the building part unless evidence proves otherwise. Evidence includes documentary evidence such as planning applications, and dated photographs of the property that validate the date of construction. A photograph in which the roof room is absent might establish the earliest possible date (convention 3.13).

Where the roof room insulation is as built or unknown, the default U-value comes from the "Room-in-roof, all elements" column of specification Table 18 for the age band of the roof room (specification section 3.10.1). The defaults are 2.3 W/m2K for age bands A to D, 1.5 for E, 0.80 for F, 0.50 for G, 0.35 for H and I, 0.30 for J, 0.25 for K (0.20 in Scotland), 0.18 for L and 0.15 for M (specification Table 18). Table 18 has a separate column for a room in roof under a thatched roof.

### No access and no evidence

Assume as built where there is no evidence of retrofitted insulation. The convention lists roof rooms where there is no access and no documentary evidence as one such case (convention 3.03a). "Unknown" is for exceptional circumstances only, such as conflicting evidence of added insulation. It automatically suppresses the insulation recommendation and must be explained in site notes (convention 3.03b).

### Known insulation

Convention 2.06 refers to convention 3.07 for known insulation levels. Under convention 3.07:
- multi-foil insulation, with at least three layers of foil-type material, is entered at twice its actual thickness
- thickness can be doubled, except for insulation on solid brick and stone walls of age bands A to E, if there is documentary evidence of the insulation type and manufacturer's information that its thermal conductivity is 0.025 W/mK or less
- thickness is halved if the thermal conductivity is more than 0.08 W/mK
- these rules apply only where a thickness is specified, not where a U-value is calculated.

For roof rooms the specification offers two insulation types, mineral wool or EPS and PUR or PIR (specification Table 31, item 5-11), and Table 17 gives separate U-values for sloping ceiling, flat ceiling and stud wall by thickness (specification Table 17). The sources do not say whether the convention 3.07 doubling also applies when PUR or PIR is selected for a roof room, so ask your scheme before doing both.

Where a stud wall has no observed insulation but the adjoining rafters or slopes are insulated, with the insulation continuing to the eaves like a warm roof, record the stud wall as insulated to the same level and thickness as the slope. This applies only where visual or physical evidence confirms that the insulation extends to or abuts the stud and eaves junction without a significant uninsulated gap (convention 2.06).

Documentary evidence, such as certificates, warranties and guarantees, must relate to the actual property with nothing to contradict it. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, intent to install shown in planning documents, together with Building Control oversight, lets you treat the element as upgraded as the documents indicate. With only one of those two, and a visible upgrade, use the minimum selectable upgrade. In Scotland, evidence of intent to install does not qualify (convention 9.02).

### Entering a U-value

Default U-values must not be overwritten without documentary evidence for the property: either building control approval that correctly defines the construction and states the calculated U-value, or a U-value calculation produced or verified by a person with suitable expertise and experience (convention 3.08).

Convention 2.06 adds one specific route. If the roof room has a common wall or a gable wall, its U-value may be inferred from the "Room-in-roof, all elements" column of Table 18 and entered directly in the detailed assessment. The specification differs: it infers the common wall U-value from the main wall in the building part below (specification section 3.9.2). The convention takes precedence, but it says "may", not "must", so ask your scheme if unsure.

### Appendix 4 has been deleted

In v12.2 Appendix 4, which held U-values applicable to room in roof insulation, is marked "Deleted 31 March 2026", and the revision history lists it as deleted in version 12.2. In v12.1 it was a table of U-values by insulation thickness for slopes, flat ceilings and stud walls. That table is no longer part of the conventions. The side note to convention 2.06 still says "For U-values see Appendix 4", and the conventions do not say what replaces it. The current sources contain Tables 17 and 18 of the specification and the Table 18 route in convention 2.06. If your scheme guidance still refers to Appendix 4 values, ask your scheme.

## Key Takeaways
- A roof room is included when it is reached by a permanent fixed staircase that you can walk down facing forwards (convention 2.06).
- Type 1 is fully within the roof. Type 2 has an accessible common wall, a vertical continuation of the external wall of the storey below, as part of the room. A separating ceiling plays no part.
- The 1.8 m rule applies to the Type 2 common wall only, over at least half its length and excluding gables and party walls. Fail it and the space is a separate storey.
- If any element has known insulation, the detailed method must be used and all elements measured. The simplified method needs gable lengths for Type 1, and gable and common wall lengths and heights for Type 2.
- Record each gable as exposed, party, sheltered or connected. A gable less than 25% connected is treated as exposed.
- In the detailed method, dormers protruding less than 20% of the roof room floor area are ignored.
- With no access and no documentary evidence, record the roof room as built, with the age band of the building part unless evidence proves otherwise.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** What decides whether a roof room is Type 1 or Type 2 in RdSAP 10?

A) Whether a ceiling separates the roof room from the storey below
B) Whether the stud walls are more or less than 1.8 m high
C) Whether an accessible common wall, a vertical continuation of the external wall of the storey below, is part of the roof room
D) Whether the roof room has dormer windows

**Correct answer: C.** Convention 2.06 defines Type 1 as fully within the roof and Type 2 as not fully within the roof, where an accessible common wall is part of the room in roof.

---

**Q2.** An upper floor has 18.00 m of accessible common wall, excluding gables. 12.00 m is 1.40 m high and 6.00 m is 2.10 m high. How is it treated?

A) As a Type 2 roof room
B) As a separate storey, because part of the common wall is over 1.8 m high
C) As a Type 1 roof room
D) As a mansard storey under convention 2.07

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 2.06 requires the common wall to be equal to or less than 1.8 m for 50% or more of its length, and here 67% is 1.40 m high.

---

**Q3.** You can measure the insulation above the flat ceiling of a roof room. No other insulation can be seen and there is no documentary evidence. Which method applies?

A) Simplified, with the flat ceiling thickness mentioned in site notes
B) Simplified, with all insulation recorded as unknown
C) Either method, at the assessor's discretion
D) Detailed, with all elements measured

**Correct answer: D.** Convention 2.06 says that if any element has known insulation levels, the detailed method must be used and all elements measured.

---

**Q4.** In a detailed assessment, the parts of the dormers protruding beyond the roof line have a floor area equal to 25% of the roof room floor area. What do you do?

A) Measure the roof room elements as if the dormers were not there
B) Enter the vertical elements of all dormers in that building part as stud wall and their flat ceilings as flat ceiling
C) Enter each dormer as a separate extension
D) Change to the simplified assessment

**Correct answer: B.** Convention 2.06 lets you ignore dormers only where the protruding floor area is less than 20% of the roof room floor area.

---

**Q5.** There is no access to inspect any roof room insulation and no documentary evidence of any kind. How are insulation and age band recorded?

A) Insulation as built, and the age band of the building part
B) Insulation unknown, and the age band of the most recent extension
C) No insulation, and the age band of the building part
D) Insulation as built, and one age band newer than the building part

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 3.03a says to assume as built for roof rooms with no access and no documentary evidence, and convention 3.13 gives the age band of the building part unless evidence proves otherwise.
