# Solid Wall Insulation: IWI vs EWI, Risks, Evidence and Assessment
**Course ID:** MER-RA-005 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026
*© Meridian CPD 2026. All rights reserved.*

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- Explain who chooses between internal wall insulation (IWI) and external wall insulation (EWI) after the assessment, and what in the assessment informs the choice.
- Record a solid wall under PAS 2035:2023 clause 7.3.2, including heritage significance under Annex E for traditional buildings.
- Describe how PAS 2035:2023 handles interstitial condensation, through BS 5250, and thermal bridging at junctions.
- Recognise the installer's pre-installation checks for EWI and IWI in PAS 2030:2023.
- Enter existing internal and external wall insulation in RdSAP 10 under conventions 3.06 to 3.08.

---

## Introduction

This course covers what a Retrofit Assessor records about a solid wall so that the choice between insulating it inside or outside can be made, and what the installer checks before either goes on.

PAS 2035:2023, "Retrofitting dwellings for improved energy efficiency. Specification and guidance", is the second edition, September 2023. It came into effect on 30 March 2025, superseding PAS 2035:2019+A1:2022, and is to be read with PAS 2030:2023, the installer's specification (Foreword). Its requirements are its "shall" sentences (Foreword), which bind you where your scheme, contract or funding rules adopt it.

The PAS and RdSAP documents are used across the UK. The planning law and Approved Documents cited apply to England only; elsewhere, ask the local planning authority and building control. Planning and fire are covered in MER-RA-009, moisture in MER-RA-002, ventilation in MER-RA-008 and the standard overall in MER-RA-001.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition was built on risk "Path A, B and C", which are not in PAS 2035:2023 (its Foreword says the risk assessment process "has been simplified"), and taught that EWI in a conservation area depends on an Article 4 direction, which is wrong for England. It also called its survey lists "mandatory" and said the assessor must not judge suitability, when the standard asks the assessment to establish suitability for improvement and has the assessor categorise defects.

This course is correct to 12 October 2026.

## Section 1: Who Chooses IWI or EWI, and What the Assessment Contributes

**In short:** You establish what the wall is, its condition and constraints, and whether the dwelling is fit for improvement; the IWI or EWI choice comes later, in the options evaluation and design. If you note that a wall looks unsuitable for a measure, a sensible habit is to give the evidence. In England, on article 2(3) land, which includes conservation areas, Class A permitted development excludes cladding in render or the other materials it lists, Article 4 direction or not.

### Where the choice is made

A retrofit design "determines the unique combination of EEM systems, products, and materials" for a building (PAS 2035:2023, clause 3.30). The Retrofit Designer prepares it taking into account the building's condition, including the need to repair structural defects and "address causes of damp or water ingress" first; the agreed scope and budget; the assessment report; planning or building control conditions; and the existing ventilation (clause 8.2.8 a) to e)). The choice comes after your assessment: the Retrofit Coordinator's improvement options evaluation identifies a recommended package of measures for the medium-term improvement plan, which also identifies the building's constraints (clauses 8.1.1, 8.1.4 and 8.1.7 a); the Retrofit Designer agrees the measures with the client (clause 8.2.6), and the design settles the system and details.

Where a traditional building's significance or fabric is likely to be affected, the designer must be an A.3.7 professional who also holds the older and traditional buildings award or a conservation accreditation (Annex A, A.3.8).

### What the assessment establishes

Clause 7.3.2 b) requires an appraisal of heritage, structure, construction, condition and services "in sufficient detail to establish the suitability of the dwelling for improvement". Defects, leaks, condensation and mould are reported as those "that need to be repaired before any retrofit work can proceed" and those recommended but "not an essential prerequisite" (clause 7.4.2). The report goes to the Retrofit Designer with the data file and photographs (clause 7.4.1); if it is not enough to design from, the Coordinator arranges for you to add what is missing (clause 7.4.4).

So you establish what the wall is, its condition and constraints, and whether the dwelling is fit for improvement; the measure, system and details are chosen later. Nothing in clause 7 stops you noting that a wall looks unsuitable for a measure; a sensible habit, if you do, is to give the evidence.

### Constraints that bear on the choice

Several 7.3.2 items point towards one side of the wall:
- Planning constraints (7.3.2 c). In England, on "article 2(3) land", which includes conservation areas, Class A does not permit "the cladding of any part of the exterior" with "stone, artificial stone, pebble dash, render, timber, plastic or tiles" (SI 2015/596, Schedule 2, Part 1, Class A, paragraph A.2(a); Schedule 1, Part 1). The government's householder technical guidance says such cladding "requires an application for planning permission". No Article 4 direction is needed. Listing, flats and the other nations are in MER-RA-009.
- Heritage significance (7.3.2 b and 7.3.4; Section 2). The guidance says heritage considerations can "limit or rule out many insulation options, some of which are irreversible" (clause 5.5.5).
- Site constraints, including "access, party walls, rights of light" and adjoining properties (7.3.2 i).

### Fire and BS 40104

The design must "verify that the fire safety of the building is not compromised" (clause 8.2.21 e); clause 7.3.2 asks the assessor for no fire risk assessment. For IWI, Approved Document B Volume 1 (England) treats a concealed space left behind internal insulation or a new lining as a cavity (paragraph 5.16), and pipes or cables taken through a party wall or compartment floor make openings that should be fire-stopped (paragraph 9.24); whether cavity barriers or other details are needed is a design decision (MER-ALL-004). EWI fire rules are in MER-RA-009.

BS 40104:2025, "Retrofit assessment for domestic dwellings. Code of practice" (BSI, 24 September 2025), is not referred to by PAS 2035:2023; we do not teach it, so ask your scheme whether it applies to you.

## Section 2: Recording a Solid Wall

**In short:** Check a "solid" wall is solid, since stretcher bond with a wall thickness of 220 to 250 mm indicates a narrow cavity, and measure thickness for each building part, with evidence. Record the location and severity of defects, leaks, condensation and mould; a sensible habit is to record ground levels against the damp proof course, each elevation's finishes and what you could not see. For traditional construction, a qualified assessor completes the Table E.2 significance checklist before design starts.

PAS 2035:2023 has no list of "mandatory IWI data" or "mandatory EWI data". Points marked as the IWI guide's come from "Retrofit internal wall insulation: guide to best practice" (BEIS, September 2021), one of the PAS's listed insulation guides (clause 15.1.3). It says the risk areas in its Table 1 "should also be included in the retrofit assessment" (paragraph 22): advice, not a PAS requirement.

### Construction and thickness

Traditional construction means solid brick, stone or earth walls of "moisture-permeable materials and usually built prior to 1919", or pre-1919 timber frame (clause 3.39). Check that a "solid" wall is solid: "A narrow cavity is indicated by a stretcher bond brick pattern with wall thickness 220 to 250 mm" (convention 9.10). PAS 2030:2023 says EWI "shall not be installed" on walls with unfilled cavities unless the cavities are sealed to the designer's details, because of thermal by-pass (Table B.4, B4-l1). For traditional buildings, RdSAP default U-values might not be appropriate (clause 7.3.2, NOTE 8).

Measure thickness for each building part and alternative wall, with evidence, at reveals or by comparing internal and external dimensions (convention 2.22). For stone walls, check each elevation and storey and use an alternative wall if thickness varies by more than 100 mm (convention 2.13). Thickness sets the uninsulated U-value of stone and solid brick in age bands A to E (RdSAP10 Specification, sections 5.6 and 5.7).

### Condition, moisture and exposure

Record the location and severity of defects, leaks, condensation and mould (clause 7.3.2 d). The fabric first guidance starts with dealing with "water penetration and damp (so that the building fabric is properly dried out)" and "poor pointing of masonry" (clause 5.5.1 a). The IWI guide says damp sections "should be allowed to dry thoroughly prior to installation of IWI" (paragraph 23) and asks whether "the external ground level [is] higher than the internal floor finish level" (Table 1). For EWI, the installer confirms the design provides for ground and paths 150 mm below the damp proof course (PAS 2030:2023, Table B.4, B4-l1). A sensible habit is to record ground levels against the damp proof course and floor levels.

Clause 7.3.2 i) asks for "elevation and exposure (to sun, wind and rain ...)", with no zone field or method. The IWI guide asks "Are external wall finishes suitable for the severity of wind-driven rain exposure?" (Table 1). A sensible habit is to record each elevation's finishes and rainwater goods, and what you could not see.

### Services and fittings

Clause 7.3.2 j) covers installed services and their locations. The IWI guide says to ensure that services ("plug sockets, light switches, pipes, radiators, etc.") and fittings ("skirtings, covings, shelves, cabinets, fitted wardrobes, etc.") on the wall to be insulated are considered (paragraph 65). A sensible habit is to photograph flues, vents, meter boxes and fixings outside, and radiators, sockets and fitted furniture on external walls inside.

### Heritage significance (Annex E)

For traditional construction "an assessment of significance shall also be carried out in accordance with BS 7913", with a simplified version in Annex E (clause 7.3.4 and NOTE), by an assessor holding an older and traditional buildings award (A.1.2). A protected building, listed or in a conservation area (clause 3.23), is also assessed to BS 7913 including significance (clause 7.3.4). Annex E requires:
- significance assessed before design starts, proportionate to importance; "many traditional buildings need only a brief assessment" (E.1.3);
- the Table E.2 checklist, completed by a qualified assessor (E.1.4);
- where retrofit might affect appearance, analysis of walls, windows and doors, roofs and internal features (E.1.5), ticked on Table E.2 with details and photographs (E.1.6).

Table E.2 asks "Is the exterior wall surface significant?", "Are any features visible?" and "Are there any internal features of significance on external walls?". The notes warn that "ceiling cornices are likely to be obscured by internal wall insulation" (E.1.5, NOTE 4). Your record reaches the design through clause 8.2.33: significance is taken into account, and a measure that might affect it needs a BS 7913 heritage impact assessment.

## Section 3: Interstitial Condensation and Thermal Bridging

**In short:** Clause 7.3.2 asks you for no condensation calculation; the design provides for managing moisture in line with BS 5250. Your part is the inputs: materials and moisture properties, finishes, exposure, occupancy and ventilation. Your measured survey must show how elements meet at corners, junctions and within openings, and a sensible habit is to record reveal depths, frame positions, eaves overhang, floor and joist junctions, the damp proof course line, meter boxes and flues.

### Interstitial condensation

An interstitial condensation assessment to BS EN ISO 13788 "might not be sufficient" as the basis of a BS 5250 moisture risk assessment for some walls, "e.g. solid walls with internal wall insulation (IWI) proposed in exposed locations" (clause 4.3.3, NOTE 1). The requirement sits in the design, which "shall include provision for the management of moisture within the construction, in line with BS 5250" (clause 8.2.10). BS 5250 is cited undated, so its latest edition applies (clause 2); we do not teach it. The note to 8.2.10 says materials "should match the moisture strategy of the existing building", with vapour permeable and capillary active materials "most suited" to keeping a dynamic moisture equilibrium.

The IWI guide advises hygrothermal modelling "as advised in BS5250" (paragraph 42) and, because a vapour barrier on solid walls "may cause more harm than good", recommends a moisture open build-up or a moisture closed system with a ventilated cavity of at least 25 mm (paragraph 45).

PAS 2030:2023 has the installer confirm before starting that the design has made provision for verifying that, in porous solid walls, moisture movement "will not be impeded or altered to such an extent that moisture may begin to accumulate within fabric" (Table B.8, B8-l1), and that interface details avoid moisture risk at "embedded timbers (e.g. timber joist ends)" (Tables B.4 and B.8, B4-l2 and B8-l2).

Clause 7.3.2 asks you for no condensation calculation. Your part is the inputs: materials and moisture properties (7.3.2 g), finishes, exposure (7.3.2 i), occupancy (7.3.2 k) and ventilation (7.3.2 f; MER-RA-008).

### Thermal bridging at junctions

A thermal bridge is where insulation is discontinuous, thinner or more conductive than next to it, or weakened by geometry (clause 3.36). The design must:
- include construction details for the corners, junctions and edges of affected elements and their junctions with other elements, improved or not (clause 8.2.15);
- keep the insulated envelope continuous; details other than published good practice ones must be shown by IP1/06 calculation to have a temperature factor (fRsi) "not less than 0.75" (clause 8.2.16; Annex D, D.1, which names window reveals and meter boxes);
- not "inhibit the insulation of other elements in due course" (clause 8.2.18).

Your measured survey must "show the relationship of elements of the thermal envelope to one another at corners, junctions and within openings" (clause 7.3.2 h); a window's position in the wall governs thermal bridging around it and affects any wall insulation design (NOTE 9). A sensible habit is to record reveal depths, frame positions, eaves overhang, floor and joist junctions, the damp proof course line, meter boxes and flues.

For ventilation, Approved Document F Volume 1 (2021 edition, England) counts EWI or IWI to more than 50% of the external wall area as a major measure (Table 3.1), feeding clause 8.2.13 (see MER-RA-008).

## Section 4: The Installer's Checks and the RdSAP Record

**In short:** The installer checks that the design includes your assessment report identifying repairs, and confirms at its pre-installation inspection that they are done. In RdSAP, wall insulation is as built unless added insulation is evidenced; for stone, solid brick and cavity walls in bands A to E, dry lining alone does not confirm insulation. With both internal and external insulation, add the thicknesses together and enter as external, and where the conventions conflict with the specification, the convention takes precedence.

### What the installer checks

The installer must hold a PAS 2035 design before starting (PAS 2030:2023, clause 5.1), check that it includes "a copy of the assessment report" identifying repairs (clause 5.2.2), and confirm to the Coordinator at the pre-installation building inspection that the identified repairs have been done (clause 7.2.1). If the design lacks construction details for corners, junctions and edges, the installer shall consult the Coordinator about whether this was intended (clause 5.2.2); the informative Annex A puts it as obtaining "confirmation from the Retrofit Coordinator that this was intentional" (A.5).

For EWI the installer confirms the design provides for fixings sized for calculated wind loads, safeguarded flues and ventilation openings, and repositioned services, and attends to the base detail "including below dpc", eaves and verges, reveals, cills, penetrations, and vents, flues and meters (Table B.4, B4-l1). For IWI the list includes floor and ceiling details, reveals, "junctions of internal solid wall insulation with intermediate floors and suspended ground floors" and places where insulation is thinner, and ventilation must be "no worse" afterwards (Table B.8, B8-l1). For both, the installer confirms the substrate is suitable and refers potential non-compliance to the Coordinator (B4-l2 and B8-l2).

Invasive tests such as pull-out tests for fixings are something "the retrofit design can specify" (PAS 2035:2023, clause 7.3.2, NOTE 11). IWI and EWI are not on the list of measures the Coordinator may evidence remotely, so they need an in-person site inspection (clause 9.2).

### Recording existing IWI and EWI in RdSAP

Wall insulation is "as built" unless internal or external insulation was "added subsequent to original construction and evidence exists" (RdSAP10 Specification, Table 31, item 4-4). The conventions say:
- 3.06: plasterboard on dabs or battens and lath and plaster are linings with an airspace; use a tap test for plasterboard, treat an inconclusive result as not dry-lined, and remember "Dry lining alone does not confirm the presence of insulation". This applies only to stone, solid brick and cavity walls in age bands A to E.
- 3.07: with both internal and external insulation, "add the insulation thicknesses together and enter as external". For solid brick and stone in bands A to E, where documentary evidence confirms the conductivity, select 0.040, 0.030 or 0.025 W/m·K, rounding to the closest; where visual or documentary evidence confirms insulation but not its conductivity, mineral wool, rock wool, fibre glass or EPS is 0.040, XPS 0.030, and PUR, PIR or phenolic foam 0.025.
- 3.08: default U-values stand unless documentary evidence of conductivity following BR 443 is provided; where only part of an element is insulated, use an alternative wall or an extension.

Those material defaults conflict with the specification, which takes a conductivity "Only if documentary evidence is available" and otherwise says to assume 0.04 W/m·K (Table 31, item 4-6): identified PIR with no data sheet is 0.040 by the specification and 0.025 by the convention. The conventions settle it: "where any Convention is in conflict with the published SAP specification, the Convention takes precedence" (front page).

The specification then computes U = 1 / (1/U0 + R) (section 5.8, Table 14). For a 220 mm solid brick wall in bands A to E, U0 is 1.7 (Table 13); 50 mm at 0.025 W/m·K gives R of 2.25, so U = 1 / (0.588 + 2.25), about 0.35 W/m²K.

## Key Takeaways

To carry away:
- PAS 2035:2023 has no risk paths; IWI or EWI is chosen after your assessment (clauses 8.1.4 and 8.2.6) and designed from your report (clauses 7.4.1 and 8.2.8).
- Your assessment establishes suitability for improvement (7.3.2 b) and categorises defects (7.4.2); it does not select the system.
- In England, EWI rendered or clad in the materials listed in A.2(a) on article 2(3) land, including conservation areas, is not Class A permitted development, Article 4 direction or not (SI 2015/596, A.2(a)).
- Traditional construction needs a BS 7913 significance assessment using Annex E's Table E.2 (clause 7.3.4; E.1.4).
- Moisture is managed in the design in line with BS 5250 (clause 8.2.10); for IWI on solid walls in exposed locations, BS EN ISO 13788 alone "might not be sufficient" (clause 4.3.3, NOTE 1).
- Thermal bridging is handled by details at every corner, junction and edge (clauses 8.2.15 and 8.2.16); your survey shows how elements meet (7.3.2 h).
- PAS 2030:2023 has the installer check the design, substrate, junctions and repairs before work starts (clauses 5.2.2 and 7.2.1; Tables B.4 and B.8).
- In RdSAP, internal and external thicknesses are added and entered as external, and the convention's conductivity defaults prevail over the specification's (convention 3.07; Conventions front page).

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Which statement describes the Retrofit Assessor's part in an IWI or EWI decision under PAS 2035:2023?

A) The assessor assigns the project to risk Path B or Path C
B) The assessment establishes suitability for improvement and categorises defects, and the report goes to the Retrofit Designer, who prepares the design
C) The assessor selects the insulation system and its thickness
D) PAS 2035:2023 explicitly forbids the assessor to comment on suitability

**Correct answer: B.** Clause 7.3.2 b) asks for an appraisal sufficient "to establish the suitability of the dwelling for improvement", 7.4.2 has you categorise defects, and the designer designs from your report (clauses 7.4.1 and 8.2.8); there are no risk paths.

---

**Q2.** A householder in a conservation area in England wants rendered EWI. There is no Article 4 direction. Which statement is correct?

A) It is permitted development, because there is no Article 4 direction
B) It is permitted development if the insulation projects no more than 16 cm
C) It is permitted development if the render looks similar to the existing walls
D) It is not permitted by Class A, because on article 2(3) land Class A does not permit cladding with render

**Correct answer: D.** A conservation area is article 2(3) land, where paragraph A.2(a) excludes render cladding whatever its projection or appearance, with no direction needed (SI 2015/596, Schedule 1, Part 1; Class A, A.2(a)).

---

**Q3.** A solid brick wall has had 50 mm of internal insulation and 75 mm of external insulation added since it was built, both evidenced. How does convention 3.07 have you enter it?

A) As 125 mm, entered as external
B) As 75 mm external, ignoring the internal layer
C) As 50 mm internal, with the external layer as an alternative wall
D) As built, because RdSAP cannot model two layers

**Correct answer: A.** Convention 3.07: "add the insulation thicknesses together and enter as external"; Table 31 item 4-4 allows added insulation where evidence exists.

---

**Q4.** What does PAS 2035:2023 say about interstitial condensation for solid walls with IWI in exposed locations?

A) The Retrofit Assessor must carry out a BS EN ISO 13788 calculation under clause 7.3.2
B) IWI is not permitted on solid walls in exposed locations
C) An assessment to BS EN ISO 13788 might not be sufficient as the basis for a moisture risk assessment conforming to BS 5250
D) BS 5250 does not apply to IWI

**Correct answer: C.** Clause 4.3.3, NOTE 1; clause 7.3.2 asks for no condensation calculation, nothing bars IWI in exposed locations, and the design follows BS 5250 (clause 8.2.10).

---

**Q5.** Under PAS 2030:2023, what must the installer do if the retrofit design has no construction details for minimising thermal bridging?

A) Devise details on site and proceed
B) Obtain confirmation from the Retrofit Coordinator that the omission was intentional
C) Ask the Retrofit Assessor to add details to the assessment report
D) Proceed, because junction details are optional

**Correct answer: B.** Clause 5.2.2 has the installer consult the Retrofit Coordinator about missing items, and the informative Annex A, A.5, says to "obtain confirmation from the Retrofit Coordinator that this was intentional"; details are the designer's job (PAS 2035:2023, clause 8.2.15).
