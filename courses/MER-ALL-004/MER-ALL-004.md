# Fire Safety in Retrofit: What Every Assessor Must Know
**Course ID:** MER-ALL-004 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course you should be able to:
- Tell the Building Regulations 2010 (England) from the statutory guidance in Approved Document B Volume 1, and what is in force from what changes in 2029.
- Explain how retrofit measures can affect compartmentation, cavities, fire-stopping and means of escape.
- Apply the 18 m threshold in regulation 7(4) and the 11 m guidance in Section 10 correctly, and read a reaction to fire class.
- Record what PAS 2035:2023 clause 7.3.2 asks, note existing fire precautions, and report defects.
- Say who decides on fire safety: the Retrofit Designer (clause 8.2.21 e), the installer under PAS 2030:2023, and building control.

## Introduction

This course covers fire safety across retrofit measures and the fire precautions you may see on survey. External wall insulation is summarised; MER-RA-009 has the detail.

The law and guidance taught here apply in England only: legislation.gov.uk marks the regulation 6 and 7 texts used as applying "to England only", and Approved Document B is "for use in England" (cover). Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland are not covered.

The Building Regulations 2010 (SI 2010/2214) are law. Approved Document B is statutory guidance: it sets out "one way to comply", and following it "does not guarantee" compliance (Approved Document B Volume 1, Introduction). PAS 2035:2023 and PAS 2030:2023 are BSI specifications that bind where your scheme, contract or funding adopts them.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition said a 2020 amendment lowered the regulation 7 ban to 11 m, when the law is still 18 m and 11 m is Approved Document B guidance in effect from 1 December 2022. It also put the PAS 2035 fire duty on the Retrofit Coordinator and the assessment instead of the retrofit design, cited a superseded Approved Document B edition with wrong sections and tables, reversed BS 8414 and BR 135, treated a missing cavity barrier at a masonry party wall as a defect, and cited regulation 3(2) for the duty in regulation 4(3). The Building Safety Regulator, which it placed inside HSE, has been a separate body since 27 January 2026 (SI 2026/20, regulation 3(1)).

This course is correct to 13 October 2026.

## Section 1: The Law, the Guidance and the Classes

**In short:** The 18 m test in regulation 7(4) is law in England: in a relevant building, external wall materials are to be class A2-s1, d0 or A1, with exemptions, and the 7 storey figure belongs to a different regime. Whether a job is a material alteration is for the Retrofit Designer and building control. When you read a class for a product other than flooring, A1 is highest and F lowest, and untested products cannot be classified.

### The requirements

Part B of Schedule 1 is law. Retrofit touches these most (Approved Document B Volume 1, requirement boxes):
- B1: early warning of fire and means of escape.
- B3(2): a wall common to two buildings adequately resists fire spread between them, a terraced or semi-detached house counting as a separate building.
- B3(4): the unseen spread of fire and smoke within concealed spaces is inhibited.
- B4(1): external walls adequately resist fire spread over them and between buildings.

### When retrofit work is caught

Inserting insulation into a cavity wall is building work (Building Regulations 2010, regulation 3(1)(e)), as is work required by regulation 23 on thermal elements (regulation 3(1)(h)). Thermal element work that is not a material alteration "need only comply" with Part L, in addition to regulation 7 (regulation 4(2)).

An alteration is material if the work would at any stage result in the building not complying with a relevant requirement where previously it did or, if it did not comply, being more unsatisfactory (regulation 3(2)). Relevant requirements include B1, B3, B4 and B5, but not B2 (regulation 3(3)). The no-worsening duty is regulation 4(3): after a material alteration the building complies or "is no more unsatisfactory" than before. On a material change of use of the whole building, regulation 6(1) requires the work needed to meet listed requirements including B1, B2, B3, B4(2) and B5 in all cases, and B4(1) for "a building exceeding eleven metres in height" (regulation 6(1)(c), which does not say how height is measured; ask building control). Whether a job is a material alteration is for the Retrofit Designer and building control.

Regulation 7 applies to all building work, requiring materials "appropriate for the circumstances" (regulation 7(1)). In a relevant building, materials becoming part of an external wall or specified attachment are to be class A2-s1, d0 or A1, with exemptions (regulation 7(2), (3)). A relevant building has a storey, ignoring roof-top plant, at least 18 metres above ground and contains dwellings, an institution or a room for residential purposes; height runs from the lowest adjoining ground level to the top of the storey's floor surface (regulation 7(4)). Paragraphs (2) to (4) were inserted for England from 21 December 2018 (SI 2018/1230) and paragraph (2) substituted from 1 December 2022 (SI 2022/603); no pending change listed affects them. "At least 7 storeys" comes from the Building Safety Act 2022 definition of a higher-risk building (section 65), a different test for a different regime.

### Approved Document B: which version

The current document is Volume 1: Dwellings, "2019 edition incorporating 2020, 2022, 2025 and 2026 amendments", with the 2029 changes collated (gov.uk, updated 16 September 2026). Its front pages and amendment booklets give:
- 2020: sprinkler trigger height in blocks of flats cut from 30 m to 11 m, and signage for storeys over 11 m; neither concerns external walls.
- 2022, in effect 1 December 2022: updated Section 10 provisions for residential buildings with a storey 11 m or more.
- 2025, in effect 2 March 2025 with transitional provisions: national classes for reaction to fire removed; Class 0 no longer appears in the text.
- 2026, in effect 30 September 2026 with transitional provisions: second stairs and evacuation lifts in blocks of flats.
- 2029, taking effect 2 September 2029 with transitional provisions: national classes for fire resistance removed. Until then Table B1 keeps BS 476 alternatives (Appendix B, paragraph B24).

### Reading the classes

Products other than floorings are classed A1, A2, B, C, D, E or F to BS EN 13501-1, A1 highest and F lowest. Class F "is assigned when a product fails to attain class E", and untested products "cannot be classified" (Appendix B, paragraph B6). Suffixes s1 to s3 cover smoke and d0 to d2 flaming droplets, with s1 and d0 the lowest production (paragraph B7). Test evidence should be checked against the intended use, as small differences in fixing, joints, insulation or air gaps "might significantly affect the performance" (paragraph B2).

Fire resistance is given as R (loadbearing), E (integrity) and I (insulation) in minutes (paragraphs B18, B22); Table B2's notes raise compartment walls separating buildings to at least 60 minutes.

## Section 2: Compartments, Cavities and Fire-Stopping

**In short:** In a brick or concrete cavity wall that meets Diagram 5.3, the guidance does not call for a cavity barrier where the cavity meets the party wall; timber-framed walls, including timber frame with brick cladding, lack the diagram's two masonry leaves, so paragraph 5.18 applies. On survey, look for a compartment wall between buildings that continues through the roof space to the underside of the roof, and for fire-stopping wherever services pass through a fire-separating element.

### Compartment walls and floors

Semi-detached and terraced houses are treated as separate buildings, and every wall separating them should be a compartment wall (Approved Document B Volume 1, paragraph 5.5). In blocks of flats, any floor not within a flat, and any wall separating a flat from another part of the building, should be a compartment floor or wall (paragraph 7.1). Both should form "a complete barrier to fire" (paragraphs 5.8, 7.5). A compartment wall between buildings should run the full height, continue through any roof space to the underside of the roof, meet the roof covering with fire-stopping, and continue across any eaves (paragraphs 5.10, 5.11).

### Cavities and cavity barriers

Approved Document B counts "any concealed space" as a cavity (paragraph 5.16). Cavity barriers divide cavities and close their edges, and "should not be confused with fire-stopping" (paragraph 5.17). Paragraph 5.18 (8.3 for flats) puts them around openings and other cavity edges and where an external cavity wall meets every compartment floor and wall, then says this "does not apply where a wall meets the conditions of Diagram 5.3" (Diagram 8.2 for flats).

Diagram 5.3, "Cavity walls excluded from provisions for cavity barriers", shows two leaves of brick or concrete each at least 75 mm thick, with the cavity closed at the top of the wall, unless totally filled with insulation, and around openings. Its notes say the closing materials need no particular fire resistance and that materials of class B-s3, d2 or worse "may be placed within the cavity". The exclusion sentence follows all three of paragraph 5.18's locations, so in a brick or concrete cavity wall meeting the diagram, the guidance does not call for a cavity barrier where the cavity meets the party wall.

Timber-framed walls, including timber frame with brick cladding, which PAS 2035:2023 counts as system-built (clause 3.35), do not have the diagram's two masonry leaves, so paragraph 5.18 applies. Cavity barriers should give at least E 30 and I 15 (paragraph 5.20).

### Fire-stopping and services

Every joint, imperfect fit and opening for services through a fire-separating element, such as a compartment wall or floor or a cavity barrier (Appendix A), should be fire-stopped (paragraph 9.1). Openings for pipes, ducts, conduits or cables should be as few as possible, as small as practicable and fire-stopped (paragraph 9.24). A flue wall passing through a compartment wall or floor should have at least half that element's fire resistance (paragraph 9.23).

Where ducts pass through fire-separating elements, dampers, fire resisting enclosures or fire resisting ductwork should maintain their performance (paragraph 9.12). In a house with a floor more than 4.5 m above ground, an air circulation system should have no transfer grilles in the stair enclosure, and any duct through it should be rigid steel with fire-stopped joints (paragraph 2.9).

## Section 3: Measure by Measure

**In short:** For each measure, look out for concealed spaces left by internal insulation or new linings, which Approved Document B treats as cavities; openings for pipes or cables through a party wall or compartment floor, which should be fire-stopped; and, in a house, escape windows, whose replacements should keep at least the same potential for escape, subject to the conditions below. Whether cavity barriers or other details are needed is a design decision.

### Cavity wall insulation

Cavity wall insulation (CWI) is building work (regulation 3(1)(e)). Where walls meet Diagram 5.3, its notes let materials of class B-s3, d2 or worse be placed in the cavity. In residential buildings with a storey 11 m or more, the A2-s1, d0 recommendation for insulation does not apply to masonry cavity walls complying with Diagram 8.2 (paragraph 10.7). In a relevant building regulation 7(2) prevails (paragraph 10.7), and its exemptions cover cavity trays between two leaves of masonry, but insulation only below ground or up to 300 mm above it (regulation 7(3)(a), (e)).

PAS 2030:2023 requires the installer, before installing CWI, including in party walls, to determine whether the work results in non-compliance with the Building Regulations, fire safety included (Table B.1, B1-l2).

### Internal wall insulation

Where internal insulation or a new lining leaves a concealed space, Approved Document B treats it as a cavity (paragraph 5.16). Taking pipes or cables through a party wall or compartment floor makes openings that should be fire-stopped (paragraph 9.24). Whether cavity barriers or other details are needed is a design decision.

### External wall insulation, in summary

For houses and flats (regulation 7; Approved Document B Volume 1, Section 10):
- Relevant buildings: by law, A2-s1, d0 or A1 apart from the regulation 7(3) exemptions, with no BR 135 alternative (regulation 7(2); paragraph 10.3).
- Other residential buildings over 11 m: external surfaces A2-s1, d0 or better (Table 10.1) and, with a storey 11 m or more, insulation the same (paragraph 10.7); or the BR 135 criteria using BS 8414-1 or BS 8414-2 full-scale test data (paragraph 10.3).
- Residential 11 m or less: B-s3, d2 or better for surfaces less than 1000 mm from the relevant boundary, otherwise "no provisions" (Table 10.1), though its notes say paragraph 10.4 should always be followed. Cavity barriers follow Sections 5 and 8 (paragraph 10.9).

Regulation 7(4) and paragraph 10.7 measure to the top storey's floor surface (Diagram D6); Table 10.1 uses building height (Diagram D4).

### Loft and roof insulation

PAS 2030:2023 has the loft installer identify electrical wiring that shall not be covered by insulation, "because of the risk of overheating", for relocation above it, and recessed downlighters, to be boxed (Table B.9, B9-l1). For residential buildings up to 15 m high, Diagram 5.2 notes that thermoplastic insulation should not be carried over the compartment wall at roof level.

### Windows, doors and ventilation

In a house, a replacement for a window that would be an escape window in a new house, and is big enough for escape, should keep at least the same potential for escape or, if the old one was larger than needed, the paragraph 2.10 minimum (paragraph 2.19); cavity barriers around the opening may be needed (paragraph 2.20). The installer's pre-installation inspection includes a "measurement check, include fire egress" (PAS 2030:2023, Table B.3, B3-l2). Flat entrance doors are fire doorsets that should have self-closing devices (Appendix C, paragraph C5).

For mechanical ventilation with heat recovery, the installer checks Building Regulations compliance, fire safety included, and the duct routing (PAS 2030:2023, Table C.7, C7-l2).

## Section 4: What the Assessor Records and Who Decides

**In short:** Record and photograph anything that looks like a fire safety defect, such as a breached party wall or unsealed opening, and, as practice rather than a PAS 2035 requirement, note existing precautions such as alarms and the stair. Don't guess a class or rating, and check your scheme's wording on reporting a fire risk. The fire safety verification sits in the Retrofit Designer's design; telling the Retrofit Coordinator too is a sensible habit.

### The assessment

Every dwelling is assessed "to provide information about the dwelling for use by a Retrofit Designer" (PAS 2035:2023, clause 7.1.1). Clause 7.3.2 has no fire item, and the standard's one fire safety provision is clause 8.2.21 e), but 7.3.2 asks for much that fire decisions depend on: attachment to other dwellings (a), construction and condition (b), existing defects (d), measures already installed (e), main materials (g), window and door openings (h), party walls among site constraints (i), and vulnerable occupants (k).

The assessment goes to the Retrofit Designer with "a photographic record of all the recorded features of the building and of any identified defects" (clause 7.4.1), defects being split into those to repair before retrofit and those recommended (clause 7.4.2). If the designer finds information missing, you may be asked to collect it (clause 7.4.4).

One scheme's Retrofit Assessments Standard Guide (version 2.4) says the assessor is "not required to undertake a fire risk assessment" but is "professionally bound to let someone know" of a fire risk seen. Check your own scheme's wording.

### Existing fire precautions worth noting

As practice rather than a PAS 2035 requirement, note what you see:
- Alarms, and whether mains powered. The guidance is at least Grade D2 Category LD3 to BS 5839-6 (paragraph 1.1); for a specified tenancy, the landlord must ensure a smoke alarm on each storey with living accommodation (Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm (England) Regulations 2015, regulation 4(1)(a)(i)).
- The stair: a house with a storey more than 4.5 m up should have a protected stairway or alternative escape route (paragraph 2.5), so note removed doors or open-plan alterations.
- The party wall in the loft, which should reach the underside of the roof (paragraph 5.10), and gaps around joists, pipes and cables.
- In blocks of flats, sprinklers (recommended since the 2020 amendments where the top storey is more than 11 m up, paragraph 7.4) and flat entrance doors.

Do not guess a class or rating, which needs evidence such as a test (paragraphs B1, B6). Record anything that looks like a fire safety defect, such as a breached party wall or unsealed opening, with photographs (clauses 7.4.1, 7.4.2). A sensible habit is to tell the Retrofit Coordinator too, who oversees the project from assessment to installation (clause 6.1.3).

### Who decides

The roles split as follows:
- The Retrofit Coordinator carries out the improvement options evaluation and identifies the package for the medium-term improvement plan (clauses 8.1.1, 8.1.4).
- The Retrofit Designer prepares the design (clause 8.2.8), which "shall also" verify that fire safety "is not compromised by the installation of EEMs" and, if necessary, include an updated fire safety strategy, with a note pointing to Approved Document B and BS 7974 (clause 8.2.21 e and note 2).
- The installer checks that the design provides "protection of the dwelling against the risk of fire" (PAS 2030:2023, clause 5.2.3), and its pre-installation inspection takes "the fire safety of the dwelling" into account (clause 7.2).
- Anyone meeting a requirement another way should seek to agree it with building control early (Approved Document B Volume 1, Introduction).

None of these documents gives a named product's fire class; for that, or for how to put a defect right, ask building control or your scheme.

## Key Takeaways

The points to keep are these:
- Regulation 7(2) is law: in a building with dwellings and a storey at least 18 m up, external wall materials are to be A2-s1, d0 or A1, with listed exemptions; regulation 7 has no 11 m figure.
- The 11 m external wall provisions are Approved Document B guidance, in effect from 1 December 2022; the 2020 amendments' 11 m concerned sprinklers and signage in flats.
- No worsening is regulation 4(3); regulation 3(2) defines a material alteration.
- National reaction to fire classes left Approved Document B on 2 March 2025, and Class 0 no longer appears in it; of the European classes, F is the lowest, and untested products cannot be classified (paragraph B6).
- A masonry cavity wall meeting Diagram 5.3 is excluded from the cavity barrier provisions; timber frame is not. Service openings through compartment walls and floors should be fire-stopped (paragraph 9.24).
- PAS 2035:2023 puts fire safety verification in the retrofit design (clause 8.2.21 e); the assessment supplies the facts it needs (clause 7.3.2).
- Record and photograph fire precautions and defects without guessing classes, and report them.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Which of these is a "relevant building" under regulation 7(4) of the Building Regulations 2010 (England)?

A) A block of flats whose top storey floor is 12 m above ground
B) A block of flats with a storey whose floor surface is 18.5 m above the lowest adjoining ground
C) Any building of seven storeys or more, whatever its use
D) A three-storey house with a wall less than 1000 mm from the relevant boundary

**Correct answer: B.** A relevant building has a storey at least 18 m above the lowest adjoining ground, measured to the floor surface, and contains dwellings; there is no storey count (regulation 7(4)).

---

**Q2.** A semi-detached house has cavity walls of two brick or concrete block leaves, each at least 75 mm thick, with the cavity closed at the top and around openings, and no cavity barrier where the cavity meets the party wall. What does Approved Document B Volume 1 say?

A) Paragraph 5.18 requires a cavity barrier there, so the wall falls short of the guidance
B) Insulation in the cavity must be class A2-s1, d0 under regulation 7(2)
C) Approved Document B has no guidance on cavities in dwellinghouses
D) Paragraph 5.18 does not apply where a wall meets Diagram 5.3, which excludes such masonry cavity walls from the cavity barrier provisions

**Correct answer: D.** The wall meets Diagram 5.3, whose exclusion covers all of paragraph 5.18, and regulation 7(2) applies only to relevant buildings (Approved Document B Volume 1, paragraph 5.18, Diagram 5.3).

---

**Q3.** Where does PAS 2035:2023 require verification that "the fire safety of the building is not compromised by the installation of EEMs"?

A) In the retrofit assessment, clause 7.3.2
B) In the medium-term improvement plan, clause 8.1.7
C) In the retrofit design, clause 8.2.21 e), prepared by the Retrofit Designer
D) In the description of the Retrofit Coordinator's role, clause 6.1.3

**Correct answer: C.** The requirement is clause 8.2.21 e), and the Retrofit Designer prepares the design (clause 8.2.8); clauses 6.1.3, 7.3.2 and 8.1.7 contain no fire wording (PAS 2035:2023).

---

**Q4.** Which statement about reaction to fire classes matches Approved Document B Volume 1 at October 2026?

A) Class F is the lowest class and is assigned when a product fails to attain class E; untested products cannot be classified
B) Class E is the lowest class, and Class F means the product has not been tested
C) Class 0 is still accepted as an alternative to class B
D) The suffix d0 means the most flaming droplets

**Correct answer: A.** Paragraph B6 gives F as lowest and says untested products cannot be classified; d0 is the lowest droplet production (paragraph B7); national reaction to fire classes went on 2 March 2025 and Class 0 no longer appears (Appendix B; 2025 amendments).

---

**Q5.** On survey you find an unsealed hole around a new pipe where it passes through the compartment floor between two flats. What fits the sources?

A) Carry out a fire risk assessment, because PAS 2035:2023 clause 7.3.2 requires one
B) Record and photograph it as a defect for the report to the Retrofit Designer, and tell the Retrofit Coordinator
C) Leave it out, because fire is outside a retrofit assessment
D) Specify a proprietary seal in your report, because PAS 2035:2023 makes the assessor responsible for fire-stopping details

**Correct answer: B.** Such openings should be fire-stopped (Approved Document B Volume 1, paragraph 9.24); the assessment records defects with photographs (PAS 2035:2023, clauses 7.3.2 d, 7.4.1), and the design verifies fire safety (clause 8.2.21 e).
