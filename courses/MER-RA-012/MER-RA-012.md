# TrustMark Lodgement: What the Retrofit Assessment Must Contain and Who Lodges It
**Course ID:** MER-RA-012 | **CPD Hours:** 1.0 | **Published:** October 2026
*© Meridian CPD 2026. All rights reserved.*

---

## Learning Objectives
- State what PAS 2035:2023 requires a retrofit assessment and its report to contain, and who receives the report.
- Separate what PAS 2035:2023 says about TrustMark's data warehouse from what the ECO4 and Warm Homes: Local Grant guidance expect.
- Identify who lodges which records in ECO4 and Warm Homes: Local Grant projects, as Ofgem and DESNZ describe it.
- Explain how the pre-retrofit RdSAP assessment and the assessment report decide scores, repair funding and exemptions under ECO4.
- Apply Ofgem's documentation standards to your own evidence, and know where to find the record retention period that applies to you.

---

## Introduction

This course follows your retrofit assessment data: what PAS 2035:2023 requires, what the standard and two funding schemes say about lodging it with TrustMark, who lodges, and what the data later decides.

PAS 2035:2023, "Retrofitting dwellings for improved energy efficiency: Specification and guidance", is the second edition, published by BSI in September 2023 (PAS 2035:2023, publication history). It is a Publicly Available Specification, "not to be regarded as a British Standard", and complying with it "cannot confer immunity from legal obligations" (Foreword). Its requirements are the sentences that use "shall" (Foreword, presentational conventions). It binds you where a scheme, contract or funding rule adopts it. Ofgem says users of the TrustMark scheme "are required to comply" with it (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, paragraph 4.11), and Warm Homes: Local Grant requires upgrades to comply with the latest PAS 2035, currently the 2023 edition (Warm Homes: Local Grant Policy Guidance, section 7.1).

ECO4 operates in Great Britain (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, paragraph 1.1). Warm Homes: Local Grant applies in England only (Policy Guidance, section 1.1). PAS 2035:2023 is not tied to one nation: its Retrofit Assessor qualification rule recognises the qualification registers of England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales (Annex A.1.1). This course does not cover Scottish, Welsh or Northern Ireland government schemes; ask your scheme provider about those.

TrustMark's own documents, including its Framework Operating Requirements and lodgement guidance, could not be opened while this edition was written. This course therefore gives no TrustMark field list, screen layout or rejection code. Where it says what TrustMark's system holds or does, the source is PAS 2035:2023, Ofgem or DESNZ describing it. Before relying on any lodgement detail, read the current TrustMark documents on TrustMark's website or ask your scheme provider. This course is correct to 14 October 2026.

This edition replaces the March 2026 edition. That edition listed "mandatory" TrustMark fields (an EPC reference under 24 months old, six "moisture indicators", occupant age bands, health flags), ranked rejection reasons and a "UPRN_INVALID" code, none traceable to a published source. It also taught the PAS 2035:2019 risk paths, cited Annex A (which is Qualifications) as the assessment content, applied a six-year retention rule to assessment records, said installers lodge measures and listed closed or replaced programmes as live. Please do not rely on any of those.

## Section 1: What PAS 2035:2023 requires of the assessment

### Who assesses

PAS 2035:2023 defines a retrofit assessment as the "survey, inspection and assessment of a building to collate information for a retrofit design" (clause 3.27). A Retrofit Assessor shall be trained and qualified in accordance with Annex A (clause 7.3.1): a domestic energy assessor certified and registered by an assessor body (or working towards it by a recognised route), or a RICS registered surveyor (Annex A.1.1), with a traditional buildings award as well where the dwelling is traditionally constructed (Annex A.1.2). The Retrofit Coordinator shall confirm that every dwelling due for energy retrofit work is first assessed by a Retrofit Assessor (clause 7.1.1).

### What the assessment shall include

Clause 7.3.2 lists the content, items a) to l):
- location, orientation and attachment to other buildings (a);
- an appraisal of heritage, architectural features, structure, construction, condition and installed services (b);
- local planning authority constraints, such as listing or conservation areas (c);
- the location and severity of construction or structural defects, leaks, and "any condensation and/or mould growth" (d);
- energy efficiency measures already installed or proposed (e);
- an inspection and review of existing ventilation: the position, type, make and model of any fans where possible (with reasons if not), the size and location of background ventilators, and any functional tests, with flow rates below the devolved nation minimums reported (f);
- the construction, in enough detail to establish main materials, U-values and moisture properties of the main elements (g);
- a measured survey showing how elements relate at corners, junctions and openings (h);
- site constraints, including elevation and exposure to sun, wind and rain (i);
- installed building services, their age, service history where available, locations and whether they work (j);
- occupancy, including the number of occupants and special considerations such as vulnerable persons (k);
- other information that might affect the project, including flooding, overheating and other climate risks (l).

Item f) refers to "the checklist provided in Table C.1", but the published text contains no Table C.1; its only tables are E.1 and E.2. Ask your scheme provider which ventilation checklist it expects. There is no list of "six moisture indicators" in the standard; in clause 7.3.2, moisture falls under items d) and g).

The data collected shall be used to estimate annual fuel use, fuel costs and carbon dioxide emissions with a recognised energy model such as RdSAP, SAP or PHPP (clause 7.3.3). For a traditionally constructed dwelling, an assessment of significance shall also be carried out in accordance with BS 7913 (clause 7.3.4). Annex E gives a simplified version, and its Significance Survey Checklist in Table E.2 "shall be used" by a suitably qualified assessor (Annex E.1.4), with features described and photographed (Annex E.1.6).

### What the report shall contain, and who receives it

The whole-dwelling assessment, including the ventilation assessment, shall be recorded and reported to the Retrofit Designer, including any RdSAP, SAP or PHPP data file and a photographic record of all recorded features and any identified defects (clause 7.4.1). Defects, leaks, condensation or mould found shall be reported in two categories: those that need repair before any retrofit work can proceed, and those recommended but not an essential prerequisite (clause 7.4.2).

The Retrofit Coordinator confirms with the Retrofit Designer that the report is sufficient for a design (clause 7.4.3). If not, the Designer identifies what is missing and the Coordinator arranges for you to collate it into an updated report (clause 7.4.4). On large-scale projects every dwelling shall be assessed, although a NOTE allows one document per archetype with reporting by exception (clause 7.4.5).

So the submission PAS 2035:2023 itself requires of you is a complete report, data file and photographic record for the Retrofit Designer.

## Section 2: TrustMark and the Data Warehouse: what each source says

### What PAS 2035:2023 says

The standard records that the quality mark recommended by the Each Home Counts review has been "established as the TrustMark quality scheme" (Introduction). It mentions TrustMark's data warehouse twice, both times in a NOTE:
- if required by the certification body, the Retrofit Assessor "can upload" the final assessment report, the data file and the photographic records to the TrustMark "data warehouse" for quality assurance purposes (clause 7.4.4, NOTE);
- if required by the TrustMark quality scheme, the Retrofit Coordinator "should upload" the improvement option evaluation summary, the medium-term improvement plan and the retrofit design (clause 8.2.38, NOTE).

Neither is a "shall". The PAS also names TrustMark as an applicable quality assurance regime to which a Coordinator escalates unrectified non-compliance (clause 9.3.2, NOTE 2), and notes that Retrofit Assessors "can be registered with TrustMark" (Annex A.1.1, NOTE). The duty to lodge, and who does it, comes from scheme and funding rules.

### What ECO4 expects

ECO4 is established by the Electricity and Gas (Energy Company Obligation) Order 2022, as amended. Ofgem's Delivery guidance sets out how Ofgem, as administrator, runs it (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, opening page and paragraph 1.2); it is administrative guidance, not legislation. Version 4.0, dated 26 March 2026, is current on Ofgem's guidance page. ECO4 ends on 31 December 2026 (paragraph 1.4; DESNZ, Extending the ECO4 end date: government response).

For PAS projects, Ofgem expects:
- compliance with PAS to be evidenced through TrustMark's Quality Assurance Framework, with a certificate of lodgement for each measure (paragraph 4.19);
- the starting SAP rating to be determined by a pre-installation RdSAP assessment carried out as part of PAS 2035 (paragraphs 4.15 and 6.17), lodged in TrustMark's Data Warehouse (paragraph 6.18);
- TrustMark's "Energy Performance Report (EPR) Conventions for use in ECO" to be followed for the pre and post retrofit RdSAP assessments (paragraph 5.24);
- new pre-retrofit RdSAP assessments made on or after 29 January 2026 to use RdSAP 10 (paragraph 4.28), with the post-retrofit assessment of the same type and version (paragraphs 4.29 and 6.39).

Paragraph 6.18 says the assessment should be lodged "as PAS requires", but the PAS puts that upload in a NOTE, so for ECO4 the expectation rests on Ofgem's guidance. Whether TrustMark's own rules require it was not confirmed for this edition. Ofgem does not say who lodges the assessment itself; ask your scheme provider or Retrofit Coordinator.

Ofgem does name the Retrofit Coordinator as lodging several items: off-gas information collected through the RdSAP assessment, in the field "MainsGas" (paragraph 5.106); building fabric repair spend (paragraph 4.78); and the smart meter pledge, by tick box at project creation (paragraph 5.249). The Coordinator also confirms smart meter advice by declaration when lodging the project (paragraph 5.253). Customer contributions go in a "Customer Contribution" field in the TrustMark Retrofit Portal lodgement process (paragraph 4.33).

### What Warm Homes: Local Grant expects

DESNZ's Warm Homes: Local Grant Policy Guidance for Local Authorities (updated June 2026), which is scheme guidance rather than legislation, states that all measures must be lodged onto the TrustMark data warehouse "by a retrofit coordinator", and that a TrustMark PAS 2035 Project must be created for each accepted home within 4 weeks of batch approval (section 7.6). Retrofit assessments converted from PAS 2035:2019 can be used if TrustMark's guidance is followed (section 7.6). Pre-retrofit assessments after 15 June 2025 must use RdSAP 10, and questions about retrofit assessments go to your retrofit assessment scheme provider (section 7.5).

### Programmes that have closed or been replaced

The Great British Insulation Scheme closed on 31 March 2026 (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, paragraph 3.177). The Social Housing Decarbonisation Fund was replaced by the Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund, its GOV.UK collection withdrawn on 23 September 2024. LAD Phase 3 closed in September 2023 (GOV.UK statistics, February 2024).

### TrustMark's own rules

Ofgem refers to TrustMark's Framework Operating Requirements (paragraph 5.263) and to the EPR Conventions that ECO4 assessments should follow (paragraph 5.24). Neither could be opened for this edition; read the current versions, and your scheme provider's requirements, before lodging.

## Section 3: What your data decides in an ECO4 project

### Starting point and the minimum requirement

The pre-retrofit assessment "determines the starting SAP rating", needed to evidence eligibility and calculate scores (paragraph 4.15). For a full project score, unless an exemption or consumer circumstance applies, a project must lift a starting band F or G home to at least band D, and a D or E home to at least band C (paragraphs 4.36 and 4.41 to 4.43); homes starting at band C or above are not eligible, except in-fill (paragraph 4.37). The floor area should not change between the pre and post retrofit assessments (paragraph 6.13).

### Entries Ofgem reads directly

Several rules turn on your entries:
- overridden U-values may be used only with the scheme provider's prior agreement on suitable evidence (paragraphs 5.24 and 5.85);
- the pre-main heat source is set from the pre-retrofit RdSAP assessment, following the RdSAP conventions and TrustMark's EPR Conventions (paragraph 6.49);
- where the pre-installation RdSAP assessment records a full set of working heating controls, the standard heating controls measures cannot be claimed (paragraph 5.163);
- extractions of existing insulation carried out to support a scheme measure must be lodged with TrustMark following its EPR conventions, with evidence and photographs obtained before the pre-retrofit RdSAP assessment is finalised (paragraph 4.92).

### Building fabric repairs

ECO4 allows a building fabric repair (BFR) increase for owner-occupied homes starting in band E, F or G (paragraph 4.74). BFR homes must be evidenced using the PAS 2035:2023 pre-installation assessment as a minimum, including the clause 7.3.2 d) identification of defects, leaks, condensation or mould (paragraph 4.77), and the issues must be reported as defects that need repair before any retrofit work can proceed, in the clause 7.4.2 sense (paragraph 4.79).

### Exemptions

Where local conditions such as regular driving rain mean a measure could never be installed appropriately, or the structure or access prevents it, Ofgem's listed evidence is the PAS retrofit assessment, improvement options evaluation and medium-term improvement plan (Table 7). For projects lodged with TrustMark, exempt measures are validated against Data Warehouse data (paragraph 4.44). Your clause 7.3.2 i) record of exposure and site constraints is part of that evidence.

### The smart meter conversation

Ofgem's guidance says the Retrofit Assessor will discuss the benefits of a smart meter and ask whether the consumer wants to sign the voluntary pledge, which the Coordinator then lodges (paragraph 5.249). Ofgem links the pledge to Article 31(1)(a) of the ECO4 Order (paragraph 5.248, footnote 76). A sensible habit is to note that you had the conversation and what the consumer chose.

## Section 4: Evidence quality, audit and records

### TrustMark's quality assurance in ECO4

Measures lodged on the TrustMark Data Warehouse are not subject to Ofgem technical monitoring but to TrustMark's Quality Assurance framework (paragraph 8.2). Measures successfully lodged receive a TrustMark certificate of lodgement, and Ofgem refers measures to its rejections process where that certificate has been voided (paragraph 4.55). If a supplier makes representations, Ofgem sends them to TrustMark, which decides whether to reinstate the certificate (paragraph 4.56). Even "a minor administrative error", such as missing key information on mandatory documentation, is a compliance failure that must be rectified (paragraph 4.53).

### Ofgem's documentation standards

Ofgem sets minimum standards for completing ECO documentation and lodging ECO information, and work that falls short "may be considered for rejection" (paragraph 8.22). Those that touch an assessor's file:
- complete, with all pages present (paragraph 8.24);
- legible, with comments detailed rather than one-word answers (paragraph 8.25);
- consistent across documents (paragraph 8.26);
- errors scored through with a single line, corrected, signed and dated, with no correction fluid (paragraph 8.28);
- photos date and geo-stamped, with details not tampered with (paragraph 8.30);
- no auto-populating of premises-specific fields such as dates or signatures (paragraph 8.31), and later changes made only in a new, re-signed version (paragraph 8.33).

In practice, your report, data file and photographs should agree on address, dates, dimensions and findings.

### Property identifiers

GOV.UK's open standard describes UPRNs as "the unique identifiers for every addressable location in Great Britain", managed by GeoPlace (Identifying property and street information, updated 29 January 2026). How TrustMark's lodgement uses the UPRN could not be confirmed for this edition; ask your scheme provider.

### How long to keep records

PAS 2035:2023 sets no retention period for assessment records. Its only retention rule, clause 11.1.3, covers the Coordinator's handover documents (test certificates, commissioning records, manuals, warranties and similar) and sets six years or the length of the warranties, whichever is longer. Ofgem does not require suppliers to hold audit documents themselves and lets them arrange for a third party, such as an installer, to hold them (paragraph 8.6). Look to your scheme provider for the period that applies to you. As one example, the ecmk Retro Fit Assessor and Coordinator Quality Plan ACC-802 (version 1.3, August 2020, written to PAS 2035:2019) requires each assessment's records and supporting evidence to be "securely stored for at least 10 years". Check your own scheme's current figure.

### What may change

In June 2026 DESNZ consulted on reforming consumer protection for home upgrade schemes. It proposed a "government-owned data system" and a new integrated service to perform "many functions currently carried out by TrustMark and MCS", implemented progressively "at the latest, from 2028"; the wider system reforms would primarily apply to England. The consultation closed on 10 September 2026 and, at 14 October 2026, DESNZ was analysing the responses (GOV.UK consultation page). These are proposals, not law.

## Key Takeaways
- PAS 2035:2023 requires the assessment to cover clause 7.3.2 a) to l), and the report, data file and photographic record to go to the Retrofit Designer (clause 7.4.1).
- In PAS 2035:2023, uploading to TrustMark's data warehouse appears only in NOTEs that say "can" or "should"; the duty to lodge comes from scheme and funding rules.
- Under ECO4, the pre-installation RdSAP assessment sets the starting SAP rating and should be lodged in TrustMark's Data Warehouse (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, paragraphs 6.17 and 6.18).
- Ofgem has the Retrofit Coordinator lodging the ECO4 project, and Warm Homes: Local Grant has a retrofit coordinator lodging measures; who lodges your assessment depends on your scheme.
- An ECO4 building fabric repair claim rests on defects reported as needing repair before retrofit (clause 7.4.2; paragraphs 4.77 and 4.79).
- ECO4 documentation must be complete, consistent and properly corrected, with date and geo-stamped photos (paragraphs 8.24 to 8.30).
- PAS 2035:2023 sets no retention period for assessment records; follow your scheme provider's current requirement.
- TrustMark's own documents could not be read for this edition; check them, and DESNZ's 2026 reform proposals, yourself.

## Self-Assessment Questions

**Q1.** Under PAS 2035:2023, to whom must the whole-dwelling assessment, with its data file and photographic record, be reported?

A) TrustMark, directly, on every project
B) The EPC Register
C) The Retrofit Designer
D) The Retrofit Installer

**Correct answer: C.** Clause 7.4.1 requires the assessment to be reported to the Retrofit Designer; within clause 7, TrustMark appears only in a NOTE to clause 7.4.4.

---

**Q2.** What is the status of the PAS 2035:2023 text about the assessor uploading the assessment to TrustMark's data warehouse?

A) A NOTE to clause 7.4.4 saying the assessor "can upload" it if the certification body requires
B) A "shall" requirement in clause 7.3.2 l)
C) A provision of the ECO4 Order
D) A requirement that every assessor upload within six years

**Correct answer: A.** The upload appears in the NOTE to clause 7.4.4, worded "can upload" and conditional on the certification body; clause 7.3.2 l) covers environmental and climate risks.

---

**Q3.** On an ECO4 project that follows PAS 2035, what establishes the starting SAP rating?

A) Any EPC lodged in the previous 24 months
B) The occupancy appraisal under clause 7.3.2 k)
C) The Retrofit Coordinator's estimate
D) The pre-installation RdSAP assessment carried out as part of PAS 2035, lodged in TrustMark's Data Warehouse

**Correct answer: D.** Ofgem's ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0 gives that route for PAS projects (paragraphs 6.17 and 6.18); an existing EPC is the route only for projects outside PAS.

---

**Q4.** You find penetrating damp that must be fixed before external wall insulation, in an owner-occupied band F home under ECO4. How should your report record it?

A) As a recommended improvement only
B) As a defect that needs to be repaired before any retrofit work can proceed
C) Leave it out, as it is not an energy efficiency matter
D) Report it to Ofgem instead of the Retrofit Designer

**Correct answer: B.** Clause 7.4.2 a) requires that category, and Ofgem's building fabric repair increase depends on issues being identified under clause 7.3.2 and reported that way (ECO4 Guidance: Delivery v4.0, paragraphs 4.77 and 4.79).

---

**Q5.** Which statement about retaining your assessment records is supported by the sources in this course?

A) PAS 2035:2023 clause 7.4 requires six years from lodgement
B) Ofgem's ECO4 guidance requires each assessor personally to hold all audit documents
C) PAS 2035:2023 sets no period for assessment records, so the period comes from your scheme provider
D) Records may be deleted once a certificate of lodgement is issued

**Correct answer: C.** PAS 2035:2023 states no retention period for assessment records (its six-year rule in clause 11.1.3 covers the Coordinator's handover documents), Ofgem lets suppliers arrange for third parties to hold audit documents (paragraph 8.6), and schemes set their own figure, such as ecmk's "at least 10 years"; a certificate of lodgement can later be voided (paragraph 4.55).
